# Story rework plan: the ending, and the beats that serve it

What changes in the canon, and why, after the borrowed-beats pre-read (`team/borrowed-beats.jsonl`) and two
adversarial checks of this plan (`team/ending-check.jsonl`, `team/ending-check-2.jsonl`). Phase 2 has applied it. Beats are named by id
and people by N-id; other names are the current canon names (`team/names.jsonl`). Where this plan and an existing
canon file disagree, this plan wins once phase 2 has applied it.

**The short version.** The old ending was the stock sacrifice cycle: one person holds the world together, the mentor
offers to renew the sacrifice, and the town breaks the cycle by sharing the cost. In the new one, sharing has already
been tried by a previous you, and it failed because the living are linked to each other. The answer is a line the
Chequer's own costing struck out for want of consent. The town gives that consent, and pays with its memory of its
dead: it keeps their names and loses their faces.

---

## 1. The new ending mechanism

### The rules it runs on

- **A draw on the living cascades.** A memory of a living person is a link to someone who holds memories of their
  own (rule 3: a shared history is a link). A working that draws sense from one living mind runs along those links,
  person to person, the way chain lightning runs through a pack (kin-link cascades in combat from B23). A shared keel
  is therefore the Breaking in small: when the first sharer is drawn on, the draw jumps to everyone they share a life
  with, and each loses everything. More sharers make it worse, not better.
- **The seal shuts a holder's links.** The Chequer seal on a holder's wrist closes that mind's links to a working's
  draw, so a draw on a sealed holder takes the holder and goes no further. That is why the registry picked holders
  *present, no dependants*, and why every Black Drift stopped at you while towns 1 to 4 kept their own memories. Town
  3's sharers carried no seals (the small circles on B49's ring have no seal mark), so the draw ran through them. N3
  is sealed too. B25's charcoal copy, which works once, shows a seal is a mechanism and not a badge.
- **A draw on a memory of the burned dead stops.** Fire spends what sense a body still carries (the Taking), so a
  link to someone burned has no far end. A draw addressed to them takes what every living mind remembers of them
  (face, voice, what they were like) and goes no further. An unburned body is a hazard to the draw, which is why the
  Wall carries the dead home after B51.
- **The name is the address, and it stays only if it is written.** The draw finds its memory by the dead person's
  name, written (rule 3: a name is a link). What is written is not sense in a mind, so the draw leaves it. The Warm
  read their names off the lip of the crack, where generations of them are scratched into the rock; at B35 the elder
  scratches the porter's wife's name there before saying it. The town keeps the names and cannot put them to anyone.
- **The anchor is still you** (rule 4: only a mind holds a great working). What changes is what pays the keel's
  upkeep: the town's remembrance of its dead, entered in a roll, instead of the anchor's own memory.

Nothing of the dead is used. Their bodies are burned as before, and the respawn stone is never involved. What is
spent is the living's own memory, given by name, with leave.

### What the player has seen by Act V, without being told what it adds up to

- the town has no names for its first dead stranger, and the tally board takes his mark without one (B11);
- N4 says the names of the dead at every burning (B21);
- the Warm read long lists of names off the scratched lip of a seam crack, and cannot say what anyone on the list was
  like (B27);
- an old porter gives the Warm elder his late wife's name, as a guest's courtesy. The elder scratches it on the lip
  and says it into the crack. Days later he can't call up her face (B35, SB-face);
- the costing the keels were built on, in N6's daybook (B39). It priced the upkeep from *remembrance of the
  deceased* and struck the line: *rejected, supply irregular*. Beside it, in N6's hand: *requires the leave of every
  household; not obtainable*. That is his premise, and it is wrong: leave can be given name by name;
- a previous you tried sharing the hold among the living. The pencil ticks of the sharers are crossed through: the
  first in one stroke, then all the rest in one long one (B49).

At O-final the player can choose **E-d**: remake the Hollow keel as the Hearth, with the player still its anchor and
its upkeep drawn from the town's roll of its dead. Leave is given per name: a household brings the names it gives,
and a household that refuses keeps its dead's faces. The Hearth banks what is paid between Black Drifts, a reserve
against the next strain.

**The final act (P14, B51):** the households queue at N8's table in the hall, each with the names of its dead, the
old ones included. They bring names and nothing else: no keepsakes, no objects. N8 writes each into the roll. Not
every household comes; the Hall's resistance shows in who is not in the queue and in a shorter roll (S-names).
Nobody sacrifices themselves at the climax; the climax is a queue at a clerk's table. From then on, every death in
town whose household gives leave is entered.

### What it costs

- **The face of each dead person goes,** in every living mind. Within days of a name being entered, nobody can call
  up that person's face or voice or say what they were like. The roll keeps the names. The backlog goes first: at
  B52, N9 loses her mother's face. The player loses the town's dead along with everyone else.
- **It goes on.** Every death after B51 whose household gives leave is paid the same way, for as long as the town
  lasts. The town can record its dead but cannot grieve them in the old way.
- **The player stays bound:** unageing, kept, returning from death to the stone.
- **It is irregular.** The supply is deaths, and leave. If the reserve is short at a Black Drift, the keel takes the
  balance from its anchor: some of the player's memory, not all. Nobody can tell whether the next one is covered.
  There is never a countdown.

### What it leaves undone

The burn still burns, and the world past the Rim is still falling apart. The other keels on the B24 map that are not
crossed out still each have a holder forgetting at every Black Drift. N3 still forgets each day (unless E-c). The
Unpaid still hold R4. Whether the town will last long enough to pay is open.

### Nearest works

What each shares, and where this differs. A human genre cold read decides whether the differences are enough
(risk 1).

| Work | Shares | Differs |
|---|---|---|

| Dark Souls (bonfires on bones; kindling with Humanity) | A warm stone you return to after death, in an ash world | The dead never touch the respawn stone; the founding event is renamed (§2). |

| Ogawa, *The Memory Police* | An island loses a category from memory at once; the words remain and nobody can place them | There it happens to them, as an affliction. Here it is a price, given name by name, with leave. |

### Rejected alternatives

| Alternative | Why not |

The amnesiac's turn is now **you volunteered**, not "you were selected for never refusing". The registry's
selection field reads, in effect, *present, no dependants*, and the tick beside "offered self" is in your own hand.
The yes was always yours. That keeps the owner's rule that the joke is only about choices the player made, and
denies the BioShock reading.

**The founding event is renamed the Essart** (was the Kindling; source in `team/names.jsonl`). An essart (English
assart, which reads as a joke) was forest land grubbed up and burned for tillage, and its rents were reckoned at the
Exchequer: the Chequer burned the Deep Account to farm warm ground. "kindle-iron" loses its tie and is now ruddle.
