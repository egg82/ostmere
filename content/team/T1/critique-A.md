2. **Pick 4:** "decided to be proud" names the feeling; clean win for the fire with no cost.
3. **Pick 1 (close: pick 2):** the tag explains its own joke, and pick 2 conflicts with the coat/book. Pick 1 is the lazier line; pick 2 is the riskier.

Strongest: **pick 6.**

## Swaps
- Candidate 12 ("cold. ...meaning to do something about that.") for pick 7: it gives the cold the brief names, and it sets up the fire as an answer. "meaning to" reads mildly past-tense, so check it against the past-self ban.
- Candidate 11 ("night is black. the fire is the only thing in it.") for pick 4 or 7: it darkens the scene and moves it from daytime to night. It is a little on the nose; it would be better than 4 only if the fire's size drops out.
- Candidate 3 ("you searched the ruin... thanked it anyway") would serve better than pick 2 and fits canon (scavenging in a ruin), though it personifies the ruin, which the draft says it avoids for fire.
- Candidate 10 should stay out (says the amnesia outright).

## Sequence
It stays mostly flat. Sky, pockets, rocks, fire, sticks: the middle is a procedural run, and only 3 changes anything. Pick 6 opens the scene up and pick 8 turns inward, so by the 8th line the player has gone from outward conditions to an inner absence, which is a real if small turn. But five of eight lines end on the same deadpan tag (1, 2, 5, 7 plus 4), and the register never changes: no dread, no warmth, nothing lost. It is also all brighter than the declared first hour (cold, alone, ash) because every cost is waved off with a joke; no line carries a "no" or a "no, and". Needs one line that costs something (cold with no fire, a failed strike) and a different sentence shape in the back half.
