# Mysteries: the clue trail

What a close reader can piece together **before** the spine says it, where each clue lives, and what stays
unanswered on purpose. Clues are short, hooked, and leave the last step to the reader (`docs/research/llm-writing.md`).
Where the clue lives: **J** journal (margin, entry, crossed-out guess), **I** item lore, **C** chronicle line,
**B** a beat, **T** townsfolk chatter.

## Unanswered on purpose

- **What the Deep Account is.** Implied, never said (`world.md`, "The thing under the land, implied").
- **What the even draw was**, and what drew.
- **Where spent memory goes.** Rule 5 says it carries weight; nobody knows where the weight sits. If it's somewhere,
  someone could get it back. Nobody does. (Expansion hook.)
- **Whether the reserve covers the next Black Drift.** Under E-d nobody can tell, and there is never a countdown.
- **The Leaners.** What they are, what they lean toward and why they turn their faces away.
- **The dune thing.**
- **Why you always cut the stool's leg short.**
- **Why N3 said no.** They don't remember; the records only say *that*. The player decides what they think.
- **What lies past the glow**, and whether other keels still hold elsewhere with other holders (the Chequer map shows
  several marks not crossed out). Expansion territory.

## Journal rules for clues

- At most one ominous line per page (`decisions.md`). Clues are mostly mundane observations that become ominous only
  in hindsight.
- Crossed-out guesses get corrected when the player learns better; a few old-ink corrections are already there when an
  entry opens (B9 is the first). Old-ink notes are rare (a code-set rate, `old_hand_rate`). They come from the yous of
  towns 2 to 4, may contradict each other, and are not always right.
