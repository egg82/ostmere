# Open questions for the owner

Each is phrased so it can be asked without spoiling him. Recommendation under each. Where the question itself would
leak, it says a spoiler is behind it and lets him choose.

1. **Can one of the endings be quiet and sad for your character, if you choose it knowingly?**
   Recommendation: yes. It's a real finish, never a bad-ending punishment, and the other options are visible beside
   it.

2. **Is one informed choice near the very end fine, as long as every option's cost is shown and what you built decides
   which options exist?** (The alternative is an ending decided only by save state, with no last decision.)
   Recommendation: yes, one choice; the build decides the menu, the choice decides the ending.

3. **A season cycle of three weeks (three seasons of a week each, so a typical run sees each season four times): good
   length?** Recommendation: yes; a week is long enough to re-tune mixes and short enough to come round again soon.

4. **Spoiler behind this one:** a question about your own character's death as a story mechanic (within the
   existing guardrails). Want to look, or should I decide? Recommendation: let me decide.

5. **Is uncanny wrongness in ordinary animals (how they behave, not gore) fair game, or too close to horror?**
   Recommendation: fair game; it's the goosebumps register, and the journal plays it dry.

6. **The world's name, and the starting valley's: pick them yourself, or leave them to content?** Recommendation: leave
   them to content; they appear on the map as you reach them.

7. **Spoiler behind this one:** a question about how much of the late story happens away from the town (expeditions)
   versus at home. Want to look, or should I decide? Recommendation: let me decide (current plan: home beats
   interleave with every expedition beat late on).


## Answers

- One informed choice near the end: yes.
- The three-week season cycle (three seasons of a week each): go with it and see if it works; the simulator and play
  will tell.
- Uncanny wrongness in ordinary animals (behaviour, not gore): yes, absolutely.
- Naming the world and the starting valley: the writing team writes everything, names included.
- A quiet, sad ending for your character, chosen knowingly: yes.
- Your character's own death as a mechanic, and how much of the late story happens away from town: the writing team
  decides (not looked at, so they stay a surprise).
- The ending the game leans toward may leave the town, not only you, with a lasting, painful price (2026-10-21): yes.
