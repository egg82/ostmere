# The spine

The main story as numbered beats. Data twin: `beats.yaml` (gates, days); this file says what each beat *is*. Facts
are from `world.md`; places from `regions.md`; people from `cast.md` (N ids).

**Shape:** 53 beats, days 1–84 on a typical route, one **weekly** (bigger) beat every week, four beats a week from
week 4 to the end (seven in week 1, the first day being where everything is taught). Reveals get *bigger* toward the
end, never sparser: the front half reveals the world, the back half reveals you.

**Gates** are mostly projects and skill levels (play decides), a few region-days and arrivals, and two clock beats
(season turns) that gate nothing. The sharp route arrives ~17% early, the lazy one ~17% late, by project throughput.

Weekly beats are marked **★**.

### Day 1's one-off actions

Each runs once, says one log line and opens the next. Bare hands first; a skill that needs a tool opens only once the tool is made.

| Id | Gate | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | start | Stand up: three short pushes, a log line each; the third gets you up. |
| A2 | A1 | Look around: the ruin, the grey, nothing moving, and the cold. A **cold** status appears and stays until a fire is lit. Opens Scavenging (the ruin, by hand). |
| A3 | A2 | Search your coat: the book, and a stub of charcoal wrapped in a rag to write with. Opens the journal. (Writing is never a mechanic.) |
| A4 | A2 | Gather dead wood by hand, for the cold. It is damp through (the Still's fog). Opens gathering by hand. |
| A5 | A4 | Lay the wood against the warm side of the wall to dry: a few minutes that run while your hands do the other fire-making steps. The first time two things run at once, so the mix arrives here. |
| A6 | A2 | Clear a spot: two pushes, scraping the grey down to stone. |
| A7 | A6 | Ring it with stones from the ruin. |
| A8 | flint (scavenging 3) + A7 | Strike a fire. Available as soon as the ring is done, whether or not the wood is dry. It needs dry wood and fails every time without it: struck early, the sparks die on wet wood and the log says so (a wry line, since striking early was the player's choice). With dry wood it always lights; B2 lands; the cold clears. The fire's fuel line opens standing orders. |
| A9 | B2 | Knap the flint into an edge. Opens Woodcutting: until then, wood comes by hand. |
| A10 | B2 | Roast roots in the coals: the first cooking. Cooking's skill row opens later, with smoking on day one (D-61). |

## How the back half keeps the rate up

- **Four beats every week to the end**, the same as week 4; the weekly beat in weeks 7–12 is each a reveal about the
  player, not about scenery, so later beats are bigger, never further apart.
- **Two regions in parallel late:** R4 and R5 beats alternate with home beats (B43, B44, B50) so a slow expedition
  never stalls the spine.
- **Side beats keep the drip** between spine beats: five building falls (F1–F5, `beats.yaml`), season set pieces,
  outposts' own stories, and the social system's emergent quests, which peak late because cliques need weeks to form.
- **Notable arcs pay off late:** N3, N6 and N9's turns are all in weeks 8–12.

## The ending

**One informed choice, shaped by what you built.** No karma meter, no moral score. The real ending is reachable on
the first run on every route. The mechanism, its cost and what it leaves undone are world fact (`world.md`, "The
ending, as world fact").

### Core variants (O-final)

| Id | Who holds the Hearth | Available when | The shape | Last image |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **E-d, the roll** | You, still its anchor; its upkeep paid from the town's remembrance of its dead | The scriptorium (B33, a written chronicle exists) and self-actualization tier; no population gate | Each household gives leave for its own dead by bringing their names to N8's table; a household that refuses keeps its dead's faces. The Hearth banks what is paid between Black Drifts. The faces of the entered dead go from every living mind, the backlog first, and every later death is paid the same way. You stay bound: unageing, kept. If the reserve is short at a Black Drift, the keel takes the balance from you, some memory and not all. | The morning after (B53): roofs clean, someone sweeps them anyway. |
| **E-b, the Chequer way** | You, alone, rebound by N6, once | Always (from B44) | It works for a generation. You'll forget again at the next Black Drift, and you know it. A real finish, quieter and sadder, chosen knowingly. | You cut the stool's leg short. No line after it. The stool is not put in the cellar, and the "you said yes" catchphrase is not said. |
| **E-c, the one who said no** | N3, who offers | O-book and O-tell both accepted | N3 says yes for the first time: their first *binding* yes. You are released: you start to age and stop coming back from death. | N3's offer the next morning, not remembering they made it. Nothing explains why it costs them little. |

E-d is the one the design leans toward, but not the "true" one; each is a real end. After any variant, play continues
in the saved valley. What none of them does: the burn still burns, the world past the Rim still falls apart, the other
keels still each have a holder forgetting at every Black Drift, N3 still forgets each day (unless E-c), and the Unpaid
still hold R4. Whether the town lasts long enough to pay is open.

Design intent, never a line or a paraphrase in player text: the town supplies the leave the Chequer's clerk thought
could not be had.
