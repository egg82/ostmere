1 · What is wrong

Evenly loud, and every message in two places

The six designs agreed on the diagnosis more than on anything else. Today's screen gives everything the same signal (a 2px rim, a bevel, an ember edge), so nothing gets read first: that's the "too big, too crowded" feeling. A tap's result lands under the card and in the log at once, so neither place reads cleanly: that's the "weird". And the record has no causes. The fire question turns up with nothing anywhere saying what led to it. On a phone, the thing you open the game to touch sits below 600px of head. The comparison showed the rest: A fixed the volume, B fixed the record, C fixed the hand, and E and C were the only ones whose frames had room for a town. This page puts those four together.

What the Book replaces

The Log, the Journal button, the Completed tab and the line under a card all become one Book with four pages: Chronicle, Journal (the character's own voice, unchanged), Done, and later Map, as "front-of-the-book" asks.

Filters, not surfaces B: Story hides the tallies and keeps the story lines large. Everything shows every row with its "↳". The later kinds (Town, Fights, Chatter) arrive as filters, never as new screens. Chatter shows only under its own filter.

"Since you left" is a chapter that grows by section: made, levels, rules, waiting, and later people, seasons and fights. A death or a fall is written in it as story, never summed into a pill.

Every "↳" is a link: from a line to the thing that caused it, and from a card or a rate back to its line.

The third story line in the chapter is neutral stand-in text for the ask's arrival. The real line and cause are content.

5 · If only three ship

The three changes that matter most

I

One home per message, with its cause B A

A refusal goes under the card, a success becomes story, and everything else goes to one chronicle where each row says "↳" what caused it. This answers "weird" and "I have no idea what caused that" with a single change, and it scales by adding filters.

II

A frame with a place for the next thing E C

The rail on desktop, the bottom bar and sheets on phone, a Town view, and asks as a fixed-height list. The cards stay under your thumb, and a town, a season turn or a map never get stacked into the play column.

III

Quiet at rest, good in the hand A C

One frame, ember with two jobs and four sizes, so the scene and the one ask get read first. Then the press, the level shown on its card and the cascade lit link by link, so taps feel good with nothing calling you back.
