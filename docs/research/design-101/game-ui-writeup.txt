# Game UI and UX: research notes

Scope: game screens, not data pages. Sourcing is thinner than the brief hoped: no developer postmortem on how an idle
game's screen grows was found. A second pass read the Apple and Android size guidance (via Apple's page JSON and
developer.android.com), Hoober's correction, the Chalmers thesis itself and Hodent's own blog; those claims below carry
real labels. Sources are in `game-ui-notes.jsonl`. Labels: developer, research, guideline, verbatim, practitioner,
opinion, snippet.

# Part A: General

**1. Classify every piece of UI on two axes (in the fiction? in the world's space?) and treat plain, non-diegetic UI as
the honest fallback, not a failure.**
- Fagerholt and Lorentzon's 2009 thesis crosses fiction (does the element exist in the game's story world?) with
  spatiality (is it in the game's 3D geometry?). The thesis dropped an earlier diegesis-by-spatiality chart because
  diegesis proved "slightly ambiguous" (is blood splattered on the screen diegetic?). Its final map has six categories:
  non-diegetic (the HUD), meta-perception, meta-representation, geometric, diegetic, and signifiers (subtle cues in the
  world, such as smoke from a weapon about to break). The four-word version "diegetic, spatial, meta, non-diegetic" is
  a later popular summary, not the thesis's own list. Label: research (thesis PDF read, sections 7-8; it covers
  first-person shooters only). https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/111921.pdf
- What it concludes: the more competitive the game, the more a clear overlay HUD is reasonable ("ambiguous information
  should be avoided" where fast strategic decisions are needed); the more a world resembles a plausible real one, the
  more a stray non-diegetic element hurts; a world that does not hide being artificial tolerates them. Label: research.
  Secondary summary for the "runs the risk of frustrating the player" wording: practitioner.
  https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/user-interface-design-in-video-games
- Transferable use: ask, per element, whether bringing it into the world costs legibility. Where it does, keep it a
  plain control and spend the fiction on its words and art. Game-genre caveat: the evidence is from shooters, not
  management or idle games.

**2. Separate "can they use it" from "do they want to": a screen can pass one and fail the other.**
- Hodent's two pillars. Usability, in her words, is "removing or at least alleviating all the frustrations and
  confusion the player could experience while playing the game, if they are not intended by design"; the second pillar
  is about how enjoyable the game is. In her own blog posts that second pillar is called gameflow (after Sweetser and
  Wyeth); "engage-ability" is the name her book (chapter 12) and later posts use for it. Usability "is not about
  distorting the intended experience": a horror game may be meant to frighten. Label: practitioner (her own blog, read).
  https://celiahodent.com/cognitive-psychology-applied-to-user-experience-in-video-games/ and
  https://celiahodent.com/five-misconceptions-ux/
- Transferable use: a cold-play failure ("I didn't know what to tap") and a motivation failure ("I knew and didn't
  care") need different fixes; diagnose which before changing the screen.

**3. Show the state and its consequence together, before commitment, in small numbers.**
- Into the Breach is repeatedly credited with showing every enemy intent and previewing outcomes before the player
  commits, with health and damage rarely above single digits, so one screenshot holds the whole battlefield state.
  Label: opinion (reviewers; the GDC postmortem was seen only as a listing, and its video/slides were not available).
  One developer line is verified: Matthew Davis, PC Gamer: "a push to be more deterministic in the moment-to-moment
  gameplay"; the reporter adds the Vek "show you their intended attack" before executing it (label: practitioner,
  https://www.pcgamer.com/into-the-breach-preview). https://blog.prototypr.io/into-the-breachs-ux-makes-you-feel-smart-a9cb03210757 (fetch blocked, claim from search snippets), https://gdcvault.com/play/1025772/-Into-the-Breach-Design
- Transferable use: a number the player must compute from two others is a UI defect; precompute and print the result
  beside the thing it affects.

**4. An interface that grows should grow in one place at a time, and each addition should arrive with its reason.**
- A Dark Room opens on a title, two lines of text and one button, and adds buttons and text slowly so the player learns
  each element and the interface's growth mirrors the world's. The same analyses flag the cost: minimalism with no
  direction loses some players. Label: opinion (critical analyses; no developer account found).
  https://mediacommons.org/imr/node/3947
- Idle-game developer posts (itch.io, Miner 2149 devlog) report: hiding sections until a threshold, pruning side panels
  nobody used, players asking to see the next unlock so they can plan, and one player confused because a displayed
  unlock requirement did not match when the content really appeared. Label: snippet. https://dev.to/aguier/i-built-7-idle-games-in-30-days-what-i-learned-about-incremental-design-5d3f
- Academic grounding exists (CHI 2018 taxonomy of idle games lists user interface among the analysed features and
  notes idle games' unusual use of player attention) but the abstract reports no interface findings. Label: research
  (abstract only). https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/playing-to-wait-a-taxonomy-of-idle-games/
- Null result: no primary source on how Cookie Clicker, Kittens Game or Antimatter Dimensions manage a screen that keeps
  gaining panels turned up. The practical pattern (reveal on first relevance, show the next gate truthfully, retire
  unused panels) is supported only by the snippets above.

**5. Touch is a different device, not a small mouse: size by finger, place by task, and do not depend on hover.**
- Hoober's field study (1,333 observations, 780 touching): 49% one-handed, 36% cradled, 15% two-handed; people change
  grip, sometimes every few seconds, with the task. Label: research. https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php
  His correction, in a 2017 comment on that article, read: the reach drawings are "a bit of a lie because I
  over-assumed. People touch the center of the screen. More, more quickly, more accurately, and by preference. Not the
  corners or edges." and "Please, please everyone ignore those drawings." Label: verbatim (same URL, comments). His 2017
  follow-up gives centre targets "as small as 7 millimeters" against "about 12 millimeters" in corners, sized to contain
  95% of observed taps, and advises placing dangerous or unrelated items far from others. Label: research.
  https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2017/03/design-for-fingers-touch-and-people-part-1.php
  Consequence: a thumb-reach map is not where controls must live; put primary content and actions near the centre,
  secondary actions along the top and bottom edges, keep dangerous actions away from common ones, and test on devices.
  Hoober also calls pixel-based standards "useless" because device pixels bear no relation to human dimensions, and
  says Apple's and Google's sizes seem based on platform convenience more than human factors; the numbers below are
  floors set by platform owners, not measurements of fingers.
- Target size, four primary figures. WCAG 2.2 (2.5.8): at least 24 by 24 CSS px, or a 24 px circle around each smaller
  target that touches no other target; the stricter 2.5.5 is recommended for important controls. Label: guideline.
  https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html Apple HIG: "a button needs a hit region of at
  least 44x44 pt" (60x60 in visionOS); its control-size table gives iOS and iPadOS a 44x44 pt default and a 28x28 pt
  minimum. Label: guideline (read from the JSON behind the HIG pages, e.g.
  https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility.json). Android: "at least
  48dpx48dp. Larger is even better", for touch; mouse and trackpad targets may be smaller; padding may count, so a 24dp
  icon can carry a 48dp target; with small close buttons, 48dp targets "separated by 8dp of space or more". Label:
  guideline. https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps and
  https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858 (the m3.material.io page itself is JS-only and was
  not read; Google's help page cites it). So 44 is a recommendation with a stated 28 pt floor on iOS, 48 is Android's
  recommendation, 24 CSS px is the legal-style floor.
- Game Accessibility Guidelines, basic tier: "Ensure interactive elements / virtual controls are large and well spaced,
  particularly on small or touch screens". Label: guideline. https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/basic/
- Hover: touch has no persistent hover, and tooltips triggered by it fire unreliably. Practitioner fixes: tap-to-toggle
  dismissed by tapping elsewhere, long-press, or hiding hover-only content under a hover-capability media query. WCAG
  1.4.13 (dismissible: closable "without moving pointer hover or keyboard focus"; hoverable; persistent until the
  trigger is removed) is framed entirely around pointer and keyboard focus; the page never mentions touch. Label:
  practitioner (Board Game Arena thread, snippet) and guideline (WCAG page read).
  https://forum.boardgamearena.com/viewtopic.php?p=64452 https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/content-on-hover-or-focus.html
  Rule: anything that matters must be reachable with no hover; hover may only repeat it.
- Browser chrome: `100vh` equals the large viewport, so a full-height layout overflows while the address bar is
  showing; `dvh` tracks the live viewport, `svh` the smallest. A bottom-pinned bar needs `viewport-fit=cover` and
  `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`, else the inset is zero. Sources disagree on dvh vs svh for bottom bars; dvh can cause
  layout shifts. Label: practitioner (snippet). https://blog.openreplay.com/fix-100vh-mobile-viewport/
- A real-world accessibility report: a VoiceOver user found the browser build of a long-running idle game's mouseover
  text and worker-assign links unusable. Label: verbatim (snippet). https://www.applevis.com/forum/ios-ipados-gaming/kittens-game-access

**6. Accessibility basics that apply to any screen game, with no numbers invented.**
- Basic-tier items most relevant to a menu-heavy game: readable default font size; high contrast between text/UI and
  background; no essential information by fixed colour alone or by sound alone; text advanced at the player's own pace;
  start without multiple menu levels; all UI reachable by the gameplay's input method; save all settings; avoid flicker.
  The site gives no pixel sizes or ratios. The page footnote names remapping, text size, colourblindness and subtitles
  as the most-complained-about issues. Label: guideline. https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/basic/

Not found, so not claimed: Game UI Database pattern findings, developer accounts from Frostpunk, Darkest Dungeon,
Slay the Spire (intent icons: the Giovannetti Gamasutra interview was read and never mentions them; (unverified: no
developer source found for the reasoning)), Melvor Idle, Cookie Clicker.

# Part B: What it means for Ostmere

Read: decisions.md, systems.md headings, design/opening-screen.md (all section structure; Cards and Mix editor skimmed).

**Where the design already agrees with Part A**
- Non-diegetic plain controls over a living scene (finding 1): "What can be tapped looks tappable" keeps controls as
  obvious controls; the fiction goes into lines and art. This is the fallback the taxonomy endorses.
- Growth in one place with its reason (finding 4): "The screen arrives when it means something", "Taught by need, and
  needed again", and opening-screen.md "One thing at a time" are a stricter version of the idle-game and A Dark Room
  pattern, including reserved room so an arrival moves nothing ("Tapping never moves the layout"). Nothing to add here.
- Consequence beside the thing (finding 3): chips carry their cost line, the Camp bar states what the next level pays,
  rules say what they do. Agrees.
- Touch and chrome (finding 5): the phone layout pins art and hands strip, sets 44 px targets in the condition editor
  and pins Save under the thumb; "One type scale" is checked at 390x844 and 3840x2160. Agrees with the guideline
  direction.
- Accessibility: reduced motion is honoured throughout ("Coming back", "One thing at a time").

**Gaps and conflicts**
- **Hover is undocumented.** game.css styles `:hover` alongside `:focus-visible` (the pairing is fine), but neither
  decisions.md nor opening-screen.md states a no-hover rule, and no tooltip mechanism is described. Anything meaningful
  living only in a title attribute or hover would be invisible on a phone. Gap, not a conflict: the "looks tappable"
  rule implies it but does not say it.
- **Target size is stated once** (the editor's 44 px) and not as a rule for chips, tabs, the Log button, the strip's
  Edit, or text-button rows in the panel's foot, which are "quiet text buttons" and the most likely to fall under 24 px
  of height. The 0.9rem chip label is also the smallest text; at 16px root that is about 14px.
- **Colour-only meaning.** The ledger's red line and "warm" ownership colour are the design's main signals. The docs say
  red is information with its answer beside it, and chips carry an icon plus a word, but ledger severity, warm/cold
  ownership and the dot/glow distinction are not stated to survive without colour. Not checked in the code.
- **Pinned stage vs browser chrome.** "Phone" pins the scene at about 38vh and the strip under it. Whether the unit is
  `vh`, `svh` or `dvh`, and whether the landscape-uses-desktop rule survives a short landscape viewport with the
  keyboard open in the condition editor, is not documented. Not checked in the code.
- **Single-column growth ceiling.** Findings 4's null result applies: nothing here says how the Tasks, Completed, Rules
  tabs, hands strip, ledger, skills, map, town and combat coexist once all are unlocked. The opening screen doc is
  explicitly the first fifteen minutes. The decisions on "The town", "A world map" and "Combat is a large pillar" add
  surfaces with no stated screen budget.

**Proposals to test**
1. **Add a three-line rule to the screen doc and a mechanical check:** nothing is reachable only by hover; every
   control's hit area is at least 44 CSS px in its short dimension (or has spacing that satisfies the WCAG 24 px circle
   test); status meaning survives greyscale. Test: render the phone at 390x844 in a headless container, measure every
   clickable's bounding box, and screenshot the opening in a greyscale filter; list failures. Cheap, and it finds which
   of the gaps above are real before anything is designed.
2. **Write a screen budget for the full game, not the opening:** which regions are always on screen, which are tabs, and
   the rule for demoting a region when a new one arrives (A Dark Room and the Miner 2149 devlog both point at pruning
   unused panels, not only adding). Test with a mock of the late-game state at phone width, played by a non-genre
   reader, counting taps to reach the thing they were asked to find.
3. **Test the phone pin against real browser chrome:** load the pinned-stage layout with the address bar showing and
   hidden (or emulate `100vh` vs `100dvh` and a `safe-area-inset-bottom`), with the editor's keyboard open, and confirm
   the strip and Save stay on screen and nothing jumps when the bar collapses. If it jumps, that violates "Tapping never
   moves the layout" without a tap.
