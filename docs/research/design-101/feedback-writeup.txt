# Feedback and game feel

Evidence labels: developer, research, verbatim, practitioner, opinion, snippet (seen only in a search result).
Coverage note: about 15 searches and 10 fetches. Most primary talks (Jonasson & Purho video, Nijman's "Art of
Screenshake", Swink's book) were not retrievable as text; claims about them come from secondary summaries and are
marked. Nothing primary was found on feedback design inside Cookie Clicker, Melvor Idle, Kittens Game or Antimatter
Dimensions: that is a null result, not a gap in the search method (searches for developer accounts of popups, ticks
and completion cues returned only generic guides).

# Part A. General

**1. More feedback is not better: the measured optimum is in the middle, and feedback tied to success beats feedback that is merely bigger.**
- research: Kao 2020 built one action RPG at four juice levels (none, medium, high, extreme; visual and audio effects
  only). Medium and high beat both none and extreme on experience, intrinsic motivation, playtime and performance.
  The survey (Pichlmair & Johansen, arXiv 2011.09201, read in full) and the CHI 2024 paper (read in full; it gives
  the 2020 sample as n=3,018 and calls the result a "goldilocks effect") both report this. The 2020 paper itself was
  not found in open form, so its effect sizes are unverified.
- research: Kao et al., CHI 2024 (preregistered, 1,699 participants, 2x2+control). Read in full. Success-dependent
  feedback raised curiosity, effectance and competence alike (enjoyment only indirectly, through them). Amplification
  (making the same feedback larger) lowered effectance (-.19) and competence (-.43) against standard feedback, which
  the authors tentatively put down to impeded sense of agency; it had no direct effect on enjoyment or playtime.
  Curiosity was the strongest predictor of enjoyment and the only predictor of free-choice playtime; enjoyment did
  not predict playtime ("players played more when they were curious in the game, but not when they enjoyed it").
  Mean voluntary playtime ran from 6.17 min (standard) to 10.18 min (a success-dependent amplified cell). Authors'
  reading: motivation arises from reducing uncertainty over action success, which needs legible, differentiated feedback.
  https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/253c32f5-d124-4a23-88b5-9aaaf114608d
- research caveat: Ballou et al. (CHI Play 2021) found the effectance scale used in this literature has poor
  validity (snippet). Treat effect sizes as soft; the shape (middle beats extremes) is the usable part.
- snippet: Folmer Kelly's GDC Europe counter-talk to "Juice it or lose it" argued juice can cost immersion (snippet).
- Principle: give feedback a ladder with a ceiling, make the rung depend on what actually happened (a result, a
  rare find, a failure), and do not escalate the same event class over time.

**2. Juice's original claim is about response to input, and its evidence is demonstration, not measurement.**
- developer: Jonasson & Purho's talk makes a Breakout clone lively with tweening, squash and stretch, and sound;
  claims "maximum output for minimum input" and that crude graphics matter less than interactivity. Secondhand
  summary at https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-is-your-game-juicy-enough- . Presenters' opinion, no data.
- snippet: Swink defines game feel as "real-time control of virtual objects in a simulated space, with interactions
  emphasised by polish" (six areas: input, response, context, polish, metaphor, rules). The framework assumes a
  controlled object; it says little about screens with no avatar. Applying it there is extrapolation, not evidence.
- snippet: Nijman's Vlambeer talk stacks many small cues on each shot (a per-shot screenshake increment is the quoted
  detail). The stacking-of-small-cues idea transfers; the full talk and its numbers were not retrieved (unverified: no
  transcript found on a second attempt; the game-feel survey, read in full, cites Nijman and Jonasson & Purho for
  screen shake and recoil but gives no numbers).
- research: that survey defines juice as "excessive amounts of feedback in relation to user input", mechanically
  superfluous, and warns that juice-rich interaction makes it hard to learn which aspects have mechanical importance:
  a cost of juice not covered elsewhere here (read in full).
- Principle: the unit of feel is one input answered by several small, simultaneous cues (motion, sound, a number),
  each cheap, none load-bearing alone.

**3. An answer to input must appear within about 100 ms; what follows can take longer.**
- practitioner: Nielsen (read in full) states "0.1 second is about the limit for having the user feel that the system
  is reacting instantaneously", attributing it to Miller (1968) and Card et al. (1991); those papers were not read.
  GNOME HIG's 100 ms rule was seen only as a snippet. Hit flashes of 50-100 ms and button transitions of 80-120 ms are practitioner rules of thumb
  (one blog, snippet), unmeasured.
- Principle: acknowledge the input at once, even when the result is not ready (a press state, a refusal, a
  "queued"). Failure is also feedback: an input that does nothing reads as a bug.

**4. Reduced motion is an accessibility requirement with concrete, small asks, and screen-wide effects are the usual offenders.**
- practitioner: WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA) accepts the CSS prefers-reduced-motion query, or a user setting, as a way to stop
  motion triggered by interaction; web.dev advises making the reduced version the default for non-essential motion
  (snippet).
- practitioner: Game Accessibility Guidelines (full list, read): basic, "avoid flickering images and repetitive
  patterns"; intermediate, "provide an option to turn off / hide background movement"; advanced, "provide an option
  to turn off / hide all non interactive elements" and "avoid any sudden unexpected movement or events"; basic, "ensure
  no essential information is conveyed by sounds alone"; intermediate, replicate significant sound information as text
  or visuals. https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/
- practitioner: shipped games added separate toggles for shake and for flashes (Dreamlight Valley, Mabinogi, Diablo 4
  on/off only); the figure "no more than 3 flashes per second" is a community summary of the photosensitivity rule
  (snippets).
- Principle: one switch (OS preference honoured, plus an in-game setting) that replaces motion with a non-moving
  equivalent (a colour change, a held highlight); never remove the information, only the movement. Flashes capped;
  sound is never the only carrier.

**5. For slow and background processes the feedback problem is different from the tap: it is legibility over time, then a signal at the end.**
- research: Alharthi et al., "Playing to wait" (CHI 2018) analysed 66 idle games on rewards, interactivity, progress
  rate and UI; the abstract defines idle games as "left running with little player interaction" and makes no claim
  about feedback. The paper's body was not read. https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/playing-to-wait-a-taxonomy-of-idle-games/
- opinion: one analyst reads Cookie Clicker as visual feedback on every cookie, achievement, generator and upgrade,
  a shower of "feel-good moments" early and scarcer, more accomplishment-flavoured rewards later; also information
  shown plainly (costs, income). Author's own reading, not the designer's. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-recipe-behind-cookie-clicker
- practitioner (snippet): offline-return guides name clear feedback on return as one of three main risks; a Melvor
  Idle player thread values offline progress matching active play. Player opinion.
- Principle: pace of feedback should track pace of the process: a slow bar needs a visible creep and a distinct
  completion, a return needs a summary of what happened, and an effect that fires per tick on a process that ticks
  constantly turns into noise (inference from findings 1 and 4, not directly measured).

**6. Over-juicing has three costs: habituation, loss of agency, and bodily harm.**
- Habituation and immersion cost: finding 1 (extreme worse than medium) plus Kelly's counter-talk.
- Agency: amplification lowered motivation (Kao 2024).
- Harm: shake, flash and parallax trigger nausea and headaches (finding 4).
- Principle: the check for a new effect is "what does it show that the player did not already see," not "does it look
  good." An effect with no information content is the first thing to cut.

# Part B. What it means for Ostmere

Cited by heading: decisions.md "Rewarding at any moment", "Something to watch, any time, for hours", "Fun, but never
vying for attention"; opening-screen.md cards and strip sections; systems.md "Feedback" row.

**Where the design already agrees**
- Immediate acknowledgement of a refused input: opening-screen.md has a tap that cannot proceed answer "at once and
  taking no time" with a red glow, the thing that stopped it, and a failed line. This is finding 3 done fully.
- Effects that do not move layout ("Taps never move the layout; an arrival may, once") and the "pulse means urgency,
  never" rule fit finding 6; the quiet-dot-versus-glow distinction is feedback with information content.
- Slow-process legibility is a stated pillar ("Progress visibly ticks while you watch"; "Silent and glanceable"), and
  the return unwrap plus the dusk card cover the end-of-wait signal (finding 5).
- Where reduced motion is mentioned (line slide, arrival queue) the treatment is "same information, no movement",
  which is the right shape.

**Conflicts and gaps**
- Conflict with finding 1: "Lots of juice whatever the art style" and "Juice is everywhere... nothing touched feels
  dead" has no ceiling. The evidence says the optimum is medium-high, with extreme worse than none-to-medium and
  amplification harmful. The ladder exists in practice (+1 pop, level-up burst, Camp flourish) but is not written as
  a rule, so later additions can only go up.
- Gap (finding 4): reduced motion is handled case by case in opening-screen.md, and decisions.md has no policy. The
  bursts, pops, glows and the dusk grade are not covered by any stated rule, and there is no in-game setting named.
  Nothing states a flash limit.
- Gap (finding 4, sound): "sound on every reward" next to "sound optional" does not say that sound never carries
  information alone. It currently reads as safe because the design is text-led, but the rule is unwritten.
- Gap (finding 5): feedback is specified for a tap's result and for a return. A completion produced by the mix or a
  rule while the player is watching (the common case after the opening) has no stated tier. If it matches a tap's
  "+1", the tap stops being special; if it is silent, the watched screen is flat.
- Tension to resolve, not a conflict: tap pops would make tapping feel better than watching. That is compatible with
  the 2 AM test (efficiency is untouched) but is a feel gap the design should choose on purpose.

**Proposals to test**
1. Write the feedback ladder as a rule, with a ceiling. Define tiers by event significance (ordinary completion,
   first-of-kind, level-up, big beat), fixed effect size per tier, and no escalation within a tier. Test: have a
   cold tester play a long stretch with the current effects and with every tier cut by one step; ask which stretch
   they would rather watch, and whether either felt "busy". Kao suggests the lower build will not lose.
2. One motion setting. Honour the OS reduced-motion preference by default, add one in-game toggle, define a
   "calm" equivalent per effect (a held colour or a count change in place of a pop or burst), cap flashes, and write
   "sound never carries information alone". Test: render every effect under emulated reduced motion with a headless
   browser and check that each still tells what happened; grep the tokens in game.css for any effect without a calm
   form.
3. Give background completions a tier of their own, one step below a tap's and the same everywhere (the card's
   count changing with a brief settle). Test: with the mix running and the player not tapping, ask a tester after
   ten seconds what changed; if they cannot say, the tier is too quiet. Because it earns nothing extra, it stays
   within the 2 AM test.

Not covered, for another pass: sound design specifics, the Jonasson & Purho video itself, Nijman's numbers, Swink's
book beyond its definition.
