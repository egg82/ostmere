ENGINE: the single home of its semantics, and the invariants contract tests check. Contract: docs/systems.md,
"The engine". Code docstrings summarise and point here by the lead word of a CHOICES entry.

CHOICES (each with its reason)
- Time: integer seconds since the save began. Rates: fixed-point per hour, 1000 = one item/h, because the ledger
  reads per hour. Quantities: 3_600_000 = one item, so a rate r moves a quantity exactly r per second and
  value = v0 + r*dt needs no division; splitting a span cannot round differently, by construction. That holds for a
  store moved by flows alone. A store that card lumps move (CARDS, "Rate form") adds floor(G(t)) - floor(G(t0)), G
  the exact rational its lumps have paid by each card's work: one floor per store, and still nothing to split
  differently, since G is read off the work segments, which are integers in the save.
- Event times: exact ceiling division; the final partial second's overshoot is clamped, so values stay in [0, cap].
  A store that lumps move is one ceiling division per stretch between the times its cards' work stops (a tap's
  landing, a cap): each such time is an event already, and within a stretch the value is monotone.
- Rate formula (modifiers.py): additive basis points sum, then multipliers compound, one rounding at the end: down
  for makes and XP, up for costs. Additive sum floors at 0. Why: "+10% twice is +20%" is what a reader expects of
  sources of one kind, compounding is what separate kinds do, and rounding never favours the player.
- Shares: basis points, sum <= 100%, remainder idle and shown. Forcing 100% makes editing awkward, and idle share
  is honest; the engine never redistributes a share with no source (a hidden redistribution is a number with no
  source). A paused mix card's share goes to the rest of its mix, each gain sourced (SPENT).
- Nested mix share: compositional. A saved mix flattens to its effective per-action shares (repeats summed),
  and an outer mix scales that flattened result once: floor(outer * flattened / 10_000), then sums repeats.
  Order is depth-first, first position. Why: what the page shows for an inner mix is exactly what nesting scales.
- Cap: the store stops at cap, overflow is lost, the makers keep running. Pausing at a cap would hide the event
  and stall XP; the waste is a visible fact for an order (docs: hitting a cap is an order's job).
- Store caps: a Modifier aimed at StoreCap(store) changes what the store holds, combined as rates are (adds sum, muls
  compound) and rounded down; LedgerLine.cap is the cap now. It may only raise (validation: add_bp >= 0, mul_bp >=
  BP), so no store is ever above its cap and nothing is clamped when one lands; the rebuild at that instant restarts
  a full store's makers. Why: "stores hold half again" is
  a level's visible bonus (the opening's Camp), and a cap is one number with sources, which is what the stack is.
  Only the save's stack aims at a cap: a chip's effects are refused if they do (InvalidRulesError), since a chip is
  counted only while shown. The starting stack passed to new_save is refused (InvalidVerbError) for a lowering cap
  entry, as every rules placement is. An outcome's raise lands at each completion of a card with a cap above 1, like
  any outcome modifier (the stack is appended to for good; `finished` lands once). fills_at of a full store is
  reach_time's answer, the start of its segment, which a sample carries forward to the sample's time.
- Running dry, no order: consumers pause last-in-mix first until the store's net >= 0; each resumes when the
  store refills to one hour of its cost (or the cap). A paused action makes, costs and trains nothing; a paused
  mix card's share goes to the rest of its mix until it resumes (SPENT). A change of the running mix (CARDS, "Order mix"), or SaveMix of it, clears all pauses (re-pausing at once if
  still dry). Mix order is priority the player already wrote; the hour is hysteresis, so dry never chatters at
  one instant. The rest of the mix keeps running, as the docs ask.
- Orders: latched conditions with an explicit release (hysteresis). Evaluation runs once at any instant where a
  latch changes, the queue pops, a project lands (every TAP_DONE: a push, a completion), a
  trigger fires or a READY event falls, and right after SetOrders or SetQueue; never after a player's SwitchMix,
  SaveMix or TapCard. It applies the TapCard of the first order that is true and can act; else, when no order's mix
  runs (CARDS, "Order mix"), the queue head's verb; else nothing. A TapCard can act when its project is unlocked,
  not a spent one-off, ready, and its lane free. An order's SwitchMix is never applied as a verb: while its latch is
  true its mix runs over the player's (Order mix). An order that cannot act is passed over, as a gambit with no
  target is, so order sequence is priority among orders of one kind that can act; a tap order and a mix order never
  compete. The queue head is never passed over (the queue is the plan: it waits, also while an order taps or an
  order's mix runs). A verb that changes nothing is not an error. READY falls at the first
  time a TapCard named by a true order or by the queue head, unlocked, unspent and on a free lane, turns ready
  (its Blocker's ready_at), so nothing waits on an unready project into idleness. Content and the page never build
  an always-true condition (api/CONTRACT.txt, "a store rule"), so no order they make shadows the queue forever; the
  engine does not reject one (the contract suites' generators make them). A player's switch stands; while an order's
  or the queue's mix runs it sets only the mix that comes back. A queue head that is a SwitchMix changes nothing when
  applied: its mix runs while it controls (Queue mix).
- Same instant: (time, kind, sequence); kinds STORE_LIMIT < RESUME < TAP_DONE < LEVEL_UP < CHIP < TRIGGER <
  LATCH < QUEUE_POP < READY; sequence is the subject's index in rules (stores, actions, lanes with HANDS first,
  skills, chips, triggers, projects for READY) or in save.orders. Landings come before latches so an order sees what a project made. Each event is applied, then every
  rate is recomputed (then pauses), which may raise more events at that instant.
- Firing cap: each event processed and each verb applied by orders or the queue is one firing. When an
  instant's firings reach max_firings_per_instant, evaluation is suspended for the rest of it (latches, but for a
  mix order's below, and queue pops still update, remaining events still resolve, no verb is applied), save.cascade_count goes up by one,
  save.last_cascade becomes CascadeLimit(instant, count), and time moves on; evaluation resumes at the next
  instant that evaluates. An instant counts as a cascade once its firings reach the cap, whether or not a verb was
  still waiting: the cap bounds work per instant, it does not judge it. Past the cap, the latch change of an order
  whose verb is a SwitchMix waits too (it is not handled and is no firing): it changes the running mix, and so rates,
  with no verb applied, so latches on a Running subject could otherwise chain forever (CARDS, "Order mix"); it is
  seen at the next instant where something else fires, as a discrete subject is, and the instant it waited at
  counts as a cascade.
  A caller logs a cascade by comparing counts; nothing on the save grows unbounded.
  Deterministic, pure, never a stuck save. A player's decide() starts a fresh count, even at an instant that
  already hit the cap: the cap stops chains, not the player.
- Lanes: work happens on lanes, one project at a time each. HANDS is the player's own time and always exists; the
  running mix runs on it. A project on HANDS (a tap) takes the whole of the player's time while it runs: every mix
  action's Run is YIELDED (0/1, share kept and shown) and the mix carries on unchanged when it lands. Why: tapping
  trades mix time one for one, so attention earns nothing (the 2 AM test) and nothing finishes into idleness; a share
  reserved for taps would idle whenever nobody taps. Other lanes (Rules.lanes) never touch the player's time. A share of the player's time (the
  split panel's dividers) is a mix action, never a lane: work that should share the player's time is an ordinary
  ActionDef in a mix, and work that runs on its own (a drying rack) is a lane project shown as its bar beside them.
  A tap and a continuous action doing the same kind of work are two ActionDefs (Type Object): one project's action,
  one mix action.
- Projects: inputs are taken at the first push's start and only the first push needs them; requires must hold at
  every push's start. The outcome lands when the last push's work is done, and between them only the project's
  action (if any) runs, at a full share on its lane: its flows and XP are ordinary rates, so skills train while
  tapping exactly as in a mix. The action is part of the project: it needs no ACTION unlock of its own. A one-off
  (repeatable False) cannot start again once complete. One-off narrative actions, gathering by hand and crafting
  recipes are all projects; TapCard is the one verb for tap, craft and next push, and an order or the queue can
  apply it (OrderVerb). A landing, in order: the push ends and emits its line, the lane is freed (and any pause of
  the project's action is dropped), the outcome applies if it was the last push (so an outcome may start a project
  on the lane that just landed, except HANDS, see Validation), then orders and the queue evaluate. So "keep doing
  this until" is a queue entry and "refill when low" is an order; the controlling verb is re-applied at each
  landing, which is how a next push starts by itself.
- Pushes: a project of n pushes needs n starts; progress between pushes is kept on the save (CardState) and the lane
  is free between them. Each push ends with its own line.
- Failed tap: the player may tap a project whose inputs or requires are unmet (or that a Pause holds). It takes no
  time and changes nothing: no lane is occupied, the mix keeps running, nothing is consumed and nothing lands but
  its failed_line, emitted at the tap's instant. Unready is known at the tap, never by chance: the same save and verb
  always fail or succeed alike. An order, the queue or an Outcome never starts an unready project,
  a busy lane or a locked one: it changes nothing (a verb that changes nothing is not an error).
- Work: a push needs work_s * BP work units; it fills at BP per second at full speed, through modified_rate with
  base BP, share BP, the stack aimed at its WorkRef and its lane's LaneSpeed, and the Run of its action (a paused
  action pauses the work). Rounded down, like makes.
- Speed: a LaneSpeed modifier joins the stack of every line on that lane (mix actions on HANDS, project actions,
  work); a WorkRef modifier only that project's work. "Tired halves bar speed" is one MUL_BP 5_000 on LaneSpeed(HANDS).
  A tap on HANDS runs at the speed it had at its start: the effective stack's entries aimed at LaneSpeed(HANDS) or at
  its card's WorkRef, chips' and the save's alike, are kept on its LaneState (speed) as it starts, and its lines read
  them in place of the live ones until it lands or is interrupted (Run.speed carries them to the rate formula). A chip
  that shows or clears meanwhile, or an entry an Outcome adds, reaches it only from its end; the mix it yields and
  every other lane read the live stack. Entries aimed at a line's own RateRef, and lump amounts, stay live: they change
  what a completion yields, not how long it takes. Why: a bad state's cost runs only while its cure can, and a HANDS
  tap yields the mix that holds the cure, so the cost is settled at the start, where the player can cure it first and
  the time shown is the time it takes (docs/decisions.md, "A bad state costs something small"). The queue's head
  waits while a rule's mix runs (Order mix), so a chip's rule cures it before a queued tap starts. Other lanes stay
  live: a repeating tap there is a rate with no start per completion, so a kept speed would part it from the same
  taps landed one by one. FORMAT 8 adds LaneState.speed; UPGRADES[7] sets it None on every lane, and a tap whose speed
  is None reads the live stack to its end, as a format 7 save's tap did when it began.
- Standing: an ActionDef with standing True runs at a full share whenever unlocked, outside the mix and every lane
  (a fire burning, a body getting hungry). Running dry pauses consumers in this order, last first: standing actions
  in rules order, then HANDS (its project's action, or the mix in mix order), then other lanes in rules order. An
  unlocked standing action runs from the save's first instant (see Instant 0).
- Instant 0: new_save resolves time 0 like any instant, so a trigger already true has fired, a chip already true is
  latched, standing actions run and anything dry is paused in the save it returns. Its lines are not returned (it
  has no logged form): what the page says at time 0 is content, and what is true then is in the view.
- Explain: share_bp is the effective share the line runs at: from the running mix for a mix action, BP for an
  unlocked standing action and for a project's action while its project runs ready, 0 otherwise. An Explanation's
  adds and muls hold every entry that applies to the line (aimed at its ref, or at its lane's LaneSpeed), each
  retargeted at the Explanation's ref with its source kept, so the formula recomputes per_hour from the
  Explanation's own fields (modified_rate does it for a RateRef; a WorkRef's work rounds down, as makes do).
- Chips: a chip is a latched condition (the order vocabulary, hysteresis and all) shown while it is unlocked and
  its latch is true; while shown, its effects join every stack after save.modifiers, sourced by the chip's id. A
  chip is not a store: the store it reads (warmth, fullness, energy) is ordinary, and "the chip clears" is the store
  moving, so no part of the engine sets a chip directly. Why: the smallest model that reuses latches and modifiers.
  A chip's latch is kept whether or not it is unlocked. on_line is emitted the first time it becomes shown in the
  save, whichever of its latch rising and its unlock came second (at an Outcome that unlocks it, after the Outcome's
  lines), and never again: Save.chips_seen[i] records that chip i has shown (time 0 included, whose lines are not
  returned) and never resets. Why: a chip can clear and show again every cycle while the player is away, and the line
  says something once. off_line each time it stops being shown (its latch releasing, since unlocks only grow).
- Triggers: content gates. Each fires once per save, at the first instant its condition is true, applying an
  Outcome. A find "earned" at a level or a count is a trigger: deterministic, so it shows the moment it is earned.
  Its requires (none by default) must also hold then, each on an Unlocked or Running subject: discrete, so no rise
  time is solved for them; while one is false the trigger has no event, and the pending check at the instant it
  changes sees it (one craft built and the other not, when a card starts). The conjunction is the
  trigger's alone, never the order vocabulary's, so no latch or order watches it.
- Pending triggers (pending.py): what the page states as the next step. Each unfired trigger whose requires hold, or
  whose unmet requires are each an unlock some unfired trigger gives, with `at`, when it fires under the segments now:
  its event's time where it has one, else the latest of its condition's rise time and, per unmet require, the earliest
  `at` of the triggers giving that unlock (None when any is unknown); and `held`, every require holds now. Like
  next_event, `at` holds until the next event.
- Conditions: subjects StoreValue and SkillLevel are solved from segments; CardCount, Unlocked, Running and
  LaneRules are discrete: they change only at instants where something else fires, so the pending check at that instant sees them
  (change_time returns None for them otherwise).
- Unlocks: what the player can see and use is the save's unlock set (ids by kind: action, project, skill, store,
  chip, feature); it only grows, by Outcomes. A player verb naming a locked id raises InvalidVerbError: TapCard
  of a locked project, SaveMix with a locked action. SetOrders and SetQueue accept a locked project (planning ahead
  is the route); the verb cannot act until it is unlocked. The ACTION unlock gates mix use and standing actions,
  not a project's own action. Features are page parts the engine only switches on; the feature vocabulary is the
  feature ids the opening and the outcomes unlock (Rules declares no features). The kinds entry (an EntryId) and note
  (a NoteId) are the journal's pages and the notes written on them (api CONTRACT.txt, "JOURNAL"): ordinary kinds, held
  and gated like a feature, known where the rules mention them, renamed through IdKinds ENTRY and NOTE, and never read
  by the engine beyond a gate's Unlocked. Rules.opening None means no gating
  (everything unlocked), so rules without an opening (tests, bare simulator economies) need no unlock data; the view
  then reports the effective set (View.unlocked).
- Lines: log lines are content keys emitted by projects (push, failed tap), outcomes, and chips turning on or off; the
  engine never holds text. A line names the card that wrote it (its completion, tap or failed tap, however the
  card was run; none for chips and triggers), so a page can show it where that card is. They are not stored on the save, which would grow without bound and break the split;
  advance_logged and decide_logged return them beside the save. seq numbers the lines of one instant within one
  call, from 0, in emission order; in decide_logged the decision's lines continue the numbering of the advance's
  lines at that instant, and Step.failed names the card of a failed tap (None otherwise). Two calls may each number lines at one instant (an advance_logged to t, then a
  decide_logged at t), so (time, seq) is unique within one Step, and a later Step's lines all follow an earlier one's.
- Rule changes (rulelog.py): advance_logged and decide_logged also return each order's latch turning true or false on
  the way, after each instant and after the decision's, with the amount of the store its condition reads (None for a
  condition on no store). Orders have no id, so an order is matched across the change by equality: one that only moved
  keeps its latch history, an edited one is another order. A SetOrders matches the same way: an order equal to one in
  the old list keeps that order's latch (the first equal one's, so copies of one order all take one latch), so a rule
  moved or kept while its store sits inside its band stays on; a new or edited order is latched afresh from its
  threshold alone (latch_now), and its band plays no part until it has come on. A latch that rises and
  falls inside one instant is not reported. Like lines, they are not stored on the save.
- Stint changes (rulelog.py): a goal stint is reported as an order, Order(entry.until, entry.verb), in the same list: `on`
  when its entry comes to control (the first queue entry not dead is a SwitchMix it was not before), and `on` False with
  ended STINT when a SwitchMix entry has left the queue and its `until` holds in the saved after. A removal while its
  `until` does not hold, and a stint that holds as it is queued, report nothing; one whose until held as it came to
  control is reported ended with no start. A player's SetQueue that removes a stint whose goal already holds reports it
  as landed. A stint's start and its landing carry `levels`: the amount then of each store its mix's cards make (their
  makes and outcome stores, at a share above 0), so what the save gained in those stores while it ran is the
  difference, read by whoever keeps the two (saves/ruleruns.py).
- Decision context (context.py): decide_logged's Step.context is what held at the decision's instant before the verb
  applied: the mix the hands ran, whether a TapCard's card was in it (flattened), and the chips shown. The decision
  log keeps it beside the verb, so what the player keeps doing under which state is read off the log without a replay
  (the server's repeat offers, api/CONTRACT.txt "Repeats"). It is read off the save only, so a replay reproduces it.
- Forecast (forecast.py): every level-up of every skill, and every fill (reaching its cap as it stands then, from
  below) and emptying of each named store, by a given time, with nobody deciding: the save resolved instant by
  instant exactly as advance resolves it, so orders, the queue, taps ending and stores running dry all play out. A
  view's next_level_at, fills_at and empties_at hold only under the current segments, so a page drawing a time the
  player can trust draws the forecast's. It is the save's own future while nobody decides, so a forecast made
  earlier on the same path lists the same times. Bounded by `until`, MAX_INSTANTS and an optional stop asked before each
  instant (then `until` is where it stopped); past it nothing is listed, never estimated. Read only: it returns no save.
- Read model (view.py): ledger lines, bars, project cards with their first blocker and when it clears, chips shown
  (and chips_seen, read off Save.chips_seen) and the unlock set are derived from the save at its time and never stored. Ledger: net_per_hour is the sum of the
  contributions, unclamped, so overflow at a cap shows; falling is the segment's rate < 0 (what the store actually
  does, so a held or capped line is never falling); empties_at is reach_time(segment, 0) when falling or at 0,
  fills_at reach_time(segment, cap) when rising or at the cap, else None. covered is a fact on every line: the verb
  of some order (latched or not) or of the queue head makes its store, where a TapCard makes what its outcome
  adds, what its action makes, and what any project its outcome starts makes (followed through Outcome.starts); a
  SwitchMix makes what its flattened mix makes; a dead verb (a switch to a greyed mix, a tap of a completed card)
  makes nothing, since it cannot act. all_covered: no line is falling uncovered. View.running_mix is the running mix
  (CARDS, "Order mix"), View.mix_order the index of the order whose mix it is (None while the player's own runs),
  View.orders_on the latches (orders_on[i]: orders[i]'s condition holds now), View.order_states where each order
  stands (plan.order_states: passed over when its verb is dead, else waiting while its latch is false, else outranked
  for a mix order that is not mix_order, else on). ActionBar: every action
  in the running set (shares from the mix, standing, lane actions) with its Run, yielded and paused ones included;
  a mix action both paused and yielded reads PAUSED (the pause outlives the tap). Project card: blocker is the
  project's own first unmet precondition (inputs on the first push only, then requires), and its ready_at is when the last
  unmet one holds; a busy lane is not a blocker, it is the lane's LaneBar. ready_at counts current segments and the lumps of pushes already running (a
  landing's outcome stores at its completes_at), and nothing an order, the queue or the player might do. push_s is
  the seconds one push would take started now. needs lists the stores the next push
  needs (its inputs on the first push, then each AtLeast on a store's value, one entry per store at the larger
  quantity) with that store's segment, locked stores included, so a card shows what it takes before a tap and
  why a tap would fail. A spent one-off keeps its card,
  marked done. Lanes: HANDS always, another lane once a project on it is unlocked, free ones included.
- Codec: the save travels as canonical JSON text (sorted keys, no whitespace) carrying FORMAT, the rules digest and
  the manifest (part SAVES ACROSS RELEASES). The server stores the text as the save (saves.state); a rules
  change between requests is detected, never run against the wrong rules: decode refuses it and rebase (below) is the
  one way a save crosses rules. decode accepts exactly the texts encode gives for some Save under these rules (it
  re-encodes and compares) and raises StateFormatError for anything else: truncated, reformatted, another FORMAT,
  other rules, unknown fields. No checksum: the page never edits the text, an unkeyed hash stops no deliberate edit,
  and transport already checks bytes. An edit that is itself the encoding of some Save decodes; the engine's
  behaviour on a save it did not produce is unspecified, as on invalid rules. If a page-held save ever needs
  tamper resistance, that is a keyed MAC added by the server outside the engine (the key is configuration).
  A player's verb is a Command the server's decision log keeps: verb_to_json gives it in the codec's form and
  verb_from_json reads exactly those back, and a SaveMix logged before FORMAT 9 as the same verb without its empty
  holds (StateFormatError for anything else).
- Validation of the new parts (stage 2, InvalidRulesError): unique ids per kind, and no declared lane is HANDS;
  every project's lane is HANDS or declared; work_s >= 1, pushes >= 1, push_lines empty or one per push; amounts > 0
  and on known stores; a project's action is a known action, not standing, and not the action of a project on another
  lane (running state is keyed by action; projects on one lane may share one); chip effects sourced by the chip;
  every id in opening, outcomes and conditions known (an Unlocked condition's feature must be in the feature
  vocabulary); no outcome starts a project on HANDS while HANDS is the lane landing it (a tap must not chain the
  player's time without the player). SaveMix (InvalidVerbError, since mixes are the save's) rejects a standing
  action, a project's action and a locked action.
- XP is a segment capped at the curve's last threshold (finite, like everything); levels are derived.
- Canonical saves: every segment starts at save.time and tuples follow rules order, so equality means sameness.

KNOWN GAPS (stage 2 or later, each named where a test will need it)
- No cancel: a started project runs to its end. A cancel verb would need an order too, and refund rules.
- An Outcome start on a busy lane changes nothing, so content that lays work onto another lane gates its project with
  a Running or CardCount condition; a reserved slot is deferred.
- (Closed, day one.) READY and a discrete requires: no requires turns true at an instant that does not evaluate. Since
  CARDS, CardCount is solved off the work segment (a blocker's ready_at, so READY falls at the count); a requires may
  not read Running (validation); Unlocked changes only when an Outcome lands, which is a COMPLETION, a TAP_DONE or a
  TRIGGER, each of which evaluates. tests/engine/test_dayone_questions.py holds each case, a queued card starting at
  the instant the last of its requires holds.

INVARIANTS (testable properties; r = valid rules, s = any save the engine returned)
 1. Split: advance(advance(s, r, t1), r, t2) == advance(s, r, t2) for s.time <= t1 <= t2; also with t1 drawn
    from event times, event times +/- 1, and many split points chained.
 2. Idempotent now: advance(s, r, s.time) == s.
 3. Decide advances first: decide(s, r, t, v) == decide(advance(s, r, t), r, t, v).
 4. Reading writes nothing: value/xp/level/segment/next_event/explain leave s equal to a copy taken before.
 5. value(s, r, x, t) == the store's value read off advance(s, r, t), for all t >= s.time.
 6. Between events values are read off the segment: for s.time <= t <= next_event(s).time,
    value(s, r, x, t) == value_at(segment(s, r, x), t), linear for a store moved by flows alone and linear in a
    rational with one floor for a store that lumps move; and next_event(s).time > s.time.
 7. Bounds: 0 <= value <= cap at every integer t; 0 <= xp <= last threshold; levels never decrease.
 8. Exact event times: a store with value v and rate -k (no other event first) reaches 0 at s.time + ceil(v/k),
    and is still above 0 one second earlier; for a store that lumps move, reach_time(segment, x) is the first second
    value_at reaches x, solved piecewise between the times its cards' work stops.
 9. Canonical: every segment.start == s.time; len(s.latches) == len(s.orders); stores/skills in rules order.
10. Explain: segment rate == sum(MAKE per_hour) - sum(COST per_hour) for a store not at a limit and moved by flows
    alone; for one that lumps move, the segment's exact slope differs from that sum by under one unit per lump line
    (a lump line's per_hour is its display rate; segment.rate is the exact slope rounded down); every
    Explanation's per_hour == the formula on its own fields (modified_rate(e.ref, e.base_per_hour, e.share_bp,
    e.adds + e.muls, e.run) for a RateRef; rounded down for a WorkRef), LaneSpeed entries included, retargeted.
11. Rounding: makes/XP never above the exact rational, costs never below; a non-zero cost never rounds to 0. For lump
    lines it holds per store: the store is never above the exact rational its lumps and flows give, by under one unit.
12. Same-instant order: events at one time resolve sorted by (kind, sequence); swapping rules' tuple order
    changes only the tiebreak, never what fires at other times.
13. Cascade cap: orders that chain past max_firings_per_instant at instant T never raise; the result's
    last_cascade has time T and cascade_count rose by one, no verb is applied and no SwitchMix order's latch changes
    after the cap at T, s.time can advance past T, and invariant 1 still holds across T. A decide() at T after the cap starts a fresh count: it
    applies its verb and may evaluate again.
14. Hysteresis: with release < value, an order cannot fire twice before its subject crosses the band twice.
15. (Retired with Hold: docs/decisions.md, "No hold on a line".)
16. Dry: no store is ever negative; a paused action contributes 0 to every rate.
17. Validation: each documented invalid verb raises InvalidVerbError; each invalid rule shape InvalidRulesError.
18. Purity: the engine package imports only the stdlib and itself (an import test).
19. Hands yield: while HANDS runs a project, every mix action not paused explains with Run
    YIELDED, every one with per_hour 0 and its share unchanged; once it lands, every mix line's per_hour equals what it was before the start (nothing else
    changed), and pauses are untouched.
20. One per lane: a lane holds at most one project. A player TapCard on a busy lane (but a cut-in, 108), a locked or unknown
    project, or a completed one-off raises InvalidVerbError and returns nothing; the same verb from an order, the
    queue or an Outcome returns a save equal to the one it was given.
21. Inputs then outcome: a ready start lowers each input store by exactly its amount at that instant; with constant
    speed k the push lands at start + ceil(work_s * BP / k); the outcome's store deltas appear at that instant and
    no earlier; inputs are taken once per project, not per push.
22. Failed tap: decide_logged(s, r, t, TapCard(p)) by the player, p unready at t (and tappable, speed above 0), has
    save == advance(s, r, t), lines those of advance_logged(s, r, t) then p's failed_line at t (none without one)
    written by p, and failed == p;
    a ready tap's failed is None; deciding it twice from equal saves gives equal results.
23. No doomed orders: an order or queue entry naming an unready project changes nothing when evaluated; an order
    that cannot act is passed over for the next true one that can; a true order's or the queue head's TapCard
    that is unready on a free lane starts at its blocker's ready_at when nothing else intervenes (READY).
24. Landing evaluates: every landing at T (a push, a completion) and every trigger firing makes
    orders and the queue evaluate at T; a player's TapCard does not; with an order "store below x:
    start p" latched true, p starts again at each landing until the latch releases. Landings and starts by orders
    count toward the firing cap, and invariant 13 holds with projects in the chain.
25. Logged split: advance_logged(s, r, t).save == advance(s, r, t); the lines of s->t1 followed by those of t1->t2
    equal the lines of s->t2; decide_logged likewise equals decide.
26. Keys only: every emitted line key appears in rules (a push, failed, outcome or chip line).
27. Unlocks grow: s.unlocked of a later save contains every unlock of an earlier one; with rules.opening None every
    verb valid before projects existed stays valid and no Unlocked condition is false for a known id.
28. Chips: a chip shows iff unlocked and latched; while shown, explain() of every line its effects target lists
    them with source == the chip's id; once hidden they are absent. A HANDS tap's speed entries are those of its
    start instead (Speed). Hysteresis as in 14. Its on_line is emitted only
    at the first instant it shows in the save, and not at all if that is time 0.
29. Triggers fire once: each fires at most once per save, at the first instant its condition is true, and its
    outcome lands exactly once even if the condition falls and rises again.
30. Codec: decode(encode(s, r), r) == s; encode(s1, r) == encode(s2, r) iff s1 == s2; decode of any text that is
    not encode(s', r) for some Save s' (truncated, reformatted, reordered keys, another FORMAT, other rules, unknown
    classes or fields) raises StateFormatError.
31. View reads only: view(s, r) leaves s equal to a copy; each ledger line's empties_at == reach_time(segment, 0)
    when falling or at 0; a card's blocker is None iff a player start now, on a free lane, would not fail;
    blocker.ready_at equals the latest time any of the card's unmet preconditions first holds under the current segments and
    the landings already running, None if one never does (advance to it and the card is ready, if no order, queue entry
    or player acted first); push_s equals
    the time a ready start now takes to land.
32. Pushes: n starts land n push lines and one outcome; the outcome lands after the nth and never before.
33. Standing: an unlocked standing action contributes at a full share whatever the mix or the hands do, and is
    paused only by running dry.

REJECTED, AND WHY (the alternative turned down for each decision above)
- Instant 0 left unresolved until the first decide or advance: a new save would break "every event at or before
  time is resolved", and the first view would show a trigger earned and a fire burning as not yet so.
- Every push needing its inputs present: a hidden condition that takes nothing; a per-push cost is a requires.
- The lane still busy when the outcome starts a project on it: a rack that should refill itself would idle.
- share 0 in explain() for standing and project actions: invariant 10's sum would stop matching the segment.
- Declared features (a Rules field): a type change for ids the engine only switches on; the vocabulary the opening
  and outcomes already name catches a typo in a condition, the only place a feature id can be wrong unseen.
- Project or standing actions as mix lines: one action would run in two places at once, keyed once. The split
  panel's shares are mix actions; a tap's action and a continuous one are two ActionDefs.
- Unknown the card's lines, a declared HANDS, one action on two lanes' projects: accepted rules that crash later.
- Requiring the ACTION unlock for a project's action: an unlocked tap that makes nothing over a missed unlock line.
- Rejecting locked projects in SetOrders and SetQueue: planning ahead is the route, and the verb cannot act anyway.
- Only completions evaluating: a multi-push project would leave its lane idle until some latch moved.
- A player's TapCard evaluating: every tap would undo the player's own manual switch made a moment before.
- Passing over the queue head that cannot act, like an order: the queue is the plan, and skipping reorders it.
- When ready holding a staged card to its cap (one entry for every remaining stage), with or without a later stage
  stepping aside for ready entries behind it: played on the week-one routes, typical's B5 and B6 landed a visit later
  (83.2 to 86.4 h, 103.0 to 112.2 h; stepping aside changed neither), and eight visits of days four to six were left
  with nothing to decide, the next stage's When ready having been their decision. A step-aside alone moved the
  whenever player's week so far that the 2 AM test went from 34 differences to 69.
- No READY event (content gating every queued craft on its inputs): a queue that waits on wood would stall for good,
  which is "finishing into idleness".
- An unpassable first true order (the opening's GAPS 4 as it stood): order sequence would carry meaning the page
  must reproduce by hand; gambits skip an entry with no target, and so do orders here.
- seq continued across calls (a counter on the save): state that grows for a display order the Step already gives.
- A sha256 sum in the codec: an unkeyed hash stops no deliberate edit and transport checks bytes; re-encoding and
  comparing already rejects everything that is not some save's exact text. A keyed MAC, if ever needed, is the
  server's.
- View.unlocked empty with opening None: the page would show nothing, or learn "None means all", deciding visibility.
- A different release for an input blocker: the lump is a plain threshold, release == quantity.
- A chip's StoreCap, kept (the stock stays above a cap that fell and the store simply does not fill): no content
  aims a chip at a cap, and a cap that comes and goes with a latch is a store over its cap, the state "no store is
  ever above its cap" rules out. Refused in validation instead; a level or outcome raises for good.
- An outcome's cap raise counted once per source: the stack is appended to for good and every outcome modifier
  lands at each completion, so one rule serves rates, work and caps alike.
- A lowering StoreCap in new_save's stack accepted unchecked: the starting stack is the one place a store could
  start above its cap, and the same entry is refused in every rules placement.
- fills_at None, or the time the store filled, for a full store with rate 0: None reads "never fills" (the rejected
  reading below), and the fill time is not kept by a carried-forward segment; the segment's start is the existing rule.
- A busy lane as a card's blocker: not a condition the engine solves, and every hands card would read "blocked"
  during each tap, hiding the precondition the player can act on; the LaneBar shows it with completes_at.
- Yielded and paused actions left out of ActionBar: their shares would vanish from the panel that edits them.
- YIELDED over PAUSED for an action both: the tap ends soon, the pause outlives it and is the one to fix.
- Clamped net and fills_at None at a cap: the overflow the docs want visible would read as 0, a full store as
  "never fills". falling from net rather than the segment: a held line's rounding residue would read as falling.
- A count of cascades only when a verb was withheld: it would need evaluation to run past the cap to know.
- A failed tap as a timed attempt (a bar running for a ready tap's time, the mix yielded meanwhile): the bar read as
  progress, so tapping an unready card again and again looked like it was working (a failed tap takes no
  time, pulses red and marks what is missing, docs/decisions.md).
- covered ignoring Outcome.starts: a hands step that starts a rack project could not cover what the rack makes, so
  content would drop the step or the order would skip it (an order doing for free what a tap pays for).
- ready_at from segments only: a "needs dry wood" countdown would be None while the wood is visibly drying; the
  page may not compute it from the LaneBar (it would be simulating).
- The effective push time left to the page (work_s against explain_work): the page would simulate the speed rule;
  CardView.push_s carries it.
- Numbers in order-card text (the voice pass's terse labels): the threshold would live twice and drift; the text is
  a template with slots filled from the card's structured order (api/CONTRACT.txt).
- index() and rules_digest() re-hashing a Rules tree per call is implementation, not contract (IMPL-QUESTIONS 15):
  Rules is an immutable value, so the engine may cache derived data by identity; a caller never subclasses or wraps
  Rules for speed, and the engine benchmark holds the cost.
- No card for a spent one-off: the page would infer "done" from a card vanishing, deciding state from an absence.
- Every lane in the view, or lanes only while busy: the first would show a rack before anything uses it, the second
  would hide a free rack the player is meant to fill; visibility follows the lane's first unlocked project.
- SkillBar's bounds left to the page from a served curve: level_for would exist twice; the server sends both ends.
- "runs N h" from the page taking the earliest empties_at: choosing which lines count is a rule, so the server does.
- A label on Mix, or a content key per mix: a mix is the player's, so its id is the player's name; one field, no copy.
- Durations, rates and amounts as per-number templates in content: every number would need its own key, and the
  rounding would live in prose; one page-side rule over Scales with unit words in ui.* templates is checkable once.
- The {mix} slot: content cannot know a player's mix; a raw id in text broke its own rule.
- all_covered (with or without a minimum of lines) as the end on its own: it is true at time 0 and the threshold would
  be a number in code. The end is the content's end feature alone (api/CONTRACT.txt, "The end"): the unlock is data
  where the moment is authored, and the content gates it on a milestone the page states.
- The end feature AND all_covered: every line white is no milestone a player of the genre expects, the page could not
  state it as one, and getting by is enough in this story.
- A count trigger for the end feature (the fourth forage): a player tapping ahead reached it before the last order
  was needed. A conjunction condition type, so one trigger could wait for both: more engine than this needs; the
  engine checks the two facts itself and records when they first hold (THE END, below).

a card, a "push" a completion, a "project's action" the card's own lines, and a "mix action" a card in a mix; the type
names above are already the new ones.

- Merges four rows of docs/systems.md: Projects (ProjectDef), Actions in the mix and project roles (ActionDef), Mixes

CHOICES (cards)
- Card: work_s per completion at full speed; continuous lines (makes, costs, XP); inputs and outcome stores per
  completion; requires; an optional cap; finished, landing at the cap. One lane; HANDS by default.
- Mixable: a card on HANDS can be tapped and mixed; a card on another lane is tapped only (by the player, an order or
  the queue). SaveMix rejects an off-HANDS card.
- One bar: a card has one cumulative work segment (CardState.work) and so one bar, the remainder of its work modulo
  work_s * BP. A tap and a mix share both advance that same work. On HANDS a tap yields the whole mix (every mix card
  reads YIELDED, share kept and shown, as before), so at most one of tap or mix runs a given card at any instant, and
  the view says which (CardView.by). A tap runs the card at a full share from wherever its bar stands to the next
  completion, then ends (TAP_DONE) and the mix resumes; tapping a card the mix had half done finishes that half.
- Rate form: whoever runs a card, its lines run at the running share. Its flows and XP are per hour of running: their
  rate is the share under the modifiers aimed at their RateRef and the lane's LaneSpeed. Each lump A per completion
  (an input or an outcome store) is paid by the card's work: the store moves by A' * dW / (work_s * BP), A' the lump
  under the modifiers aimed at its RateRef alone (lumps.lump_amount, rounded down for an outcome store, up for an
  input), dW the card's work, one floor per store over its lump lines and flows together (CHOICES, "Time"). Speed
  enters only through the work (WorkRef and LaneSpeed), since faster work is more completions an hour. So a whole
  completion pays exactly A' at the second its work crosses the boundary, at any speed, share or run, tapped or
  mixed, and a lump stops where its work is clamped (a tap's landing, a cap). A completion's inputs are paid in
  proportion to its work as the work is done, by whoever does it; no lump is ever taken at a start or landed at an
  end. The save keeps a store's plain Segment (its slope rounded down); motion.store_segment adds the cards' work back
  from the save's cards and the flow lines' net from the rates, never from the saved rate (one written before lumps
  were paid by work had the old lump rates rounded into it), so the save, the codec and FORMAT do not change. A lump line's Explanation is its display
  rate: A / work_s per second at full speed (Explanation.base_den = work_s) under every modifier aimed at it and at
  the card's work, rounded once. Why: the work segment is already exact integer state, so the store needs no
  remainder of its own; segments stay exact between events, the ledger reads per hour, and one bar
  cannot be charged twice. Each store appears at most once across a card's makes, costs, inputs and outcome stores
  (validation), so one RateRef is a flow or a lump, never both.
- Completions: a card completes each time its work crosses a multiple of work_s * BP, tapped or mixed, at the speed
  it runs at (a share s fills it s / BP as fast as a tap). completions = work // (work_s * BP), solved off the
  segment, so CardCount is a solved subject: an order on it fires at the exact second, and an AtMost on it releases
  when the count passes its release. A completion is an event (COMPLETION, at the second the work first reaches the
  boundary, solved as a latch is) exactly when it has a discrete effect, which is always for a card with a cap: its
  line, its outcome's non-store parts (unlocks, modifiers, raises, starts, lines) and, at the cap, `finished`. A card
  without a cap has no discrete effects (validation: its outcome is stores only, and its one line, if any, is a
  tap's), so its completions are counted, not evented; a tap of it still ends at TAP_DONE. The work segment is
  limited only where the work must stop: at the effective cap, and at a tap's boundary (a tap does exactly one
  completion; the last partial second's overshoot is clamped there, as a store's is at its cap). Elsewhere it is
  unlimited, so a capped and an uncapped card at one share complete at the same seconds.
- Caps: effective cap = cap + raised (Outcome.raises, CapRaise). A card is completed when completions reach it:
  derived, never stored. At the COMPLETION that reaches the cap, mid-mix or mid-tap: `outcome` then `finished`
  land, the card's work stops (rate 0), every mix holding it is greyed from that instant (the running one included:
  its cards run GREYED), a tap of it ends at that instant (TAP_DONE follows), and orders and the queue evaluate.
  Whether a completion reaches the cap is judged against the cap before its own outcome applies; its raises then
  apply, so a step that lands `finished` and raises its own cap is live again at once. A completed card cannot be
  tapped (InvalidVerbError from the player; nothing from an order, the queue or an Outcome) nor saved into a mix. A
  raise returns it to the panel and un-greys its mixes at that instant. A finished story step is a capped card like
  any other and goes to the Completed set; it does not leave the screen (docs/decisions.md, "The first minutes are
  slow, made by hand, and told").
- Inputs against a dry store: an input is a cost line like any other. When its store runs dry the dry rule pauses
  consumers last first, in this order read backwards: standing actions (rules order), then HANDS (the mix in mix order,
  then the tapped card), then other lanes (rules order); so another lane's tap pauses first and standing actions last.
  A paused card's work stops with it, so a completion is never finished unpaid, and the store's line reads starved.
  The Pause belongs to the card, not to who ran it: it resumes when the store reaches one hour of the cost it was
  paused at (a tap's at BP, a mix card's at its share) and outlives a tap's end. A tap needs, at its start, the rest
  of the current completion's inputs in store and no Pause on the card (its Blocker is then AtLeast(the pause's
  store, resume_at), and READY falls at the resume); else the player's tap is a failed tap and an order's does
  nothing. A tap ends, interrupted (work.interrupt: work kept, lane free, mix resumes, orders evaluate), when its card
  is paused, a requires turns false, or its speed falls to 0; it never holds the hands while its card is not moving.
  A hands tap the dry rule interrupts goes to the queue's front as its When ready entry unless a true order or a queue
  entry already taps the card (installs.requeued, as CURE CUT-IN does), so it takes up its kept work when the store
  refills: the tap was the player's choice and resuming it picks nothing for them.
  After an interrupt the dry rule runs again at that instant over what then runs (existing pauses kept, new ones
  added last first); nothing is recomputed from scratch. Work that has reached a completion boundary at the instant
  its store runs dry has finished that completion, which counts (by work, capped or not). What happens next depends
  on whether the card stops there: a tap lands (TAP_DONE) and stops, so the dry rule passes the tapped card over and
  the landing stands; a mix card carries on into its next completion, which nothing has paid for, so the dry rule
  pauses it like any consumer, capped or uncapped alike. Test: card c on HANDS, work_s 100, input 1 item of x per
  completion (36_000 per second at full speed), no other flow on x; x starts at 1 item, nothing refills it; the
  active mix runs c at 100%. x reaches 0 at t = 100: at t = 100 and at t = 1_000, c has 1 completion, its run is
  PAUSED and x is 0, for cap None and for cap 5 alike (the rejected reading gives 10 at t = 1_000). The same world
  with c tapped at 0 instead of mixed: the tap lands at 100, 1 completion, the lane free, an uncapped tap line.
- Multi-step one-offs: a told step of n parts is a card with cap n and one line per completion number; what pushes
  did. Inputs are per completion, so content that took an input once across n pushes takes its share on each.
- Requires: plain thresholds, read without a latch (validation: release == value; a Running subject is invalid,
  since running reads requires). They hold at a tap's start and while the card runs. In the mix a false one makes
  the card WAITING (0/1, share idles) until it holds; for a tap it interrupts the tap. A REQUIRES event falls when
  the truth changes.
- Failed tap: a player's tap of an unready card returns the save unchanged and api emits its failed_line at once
  (decisions.failed_tap names it). A tap at speed 0 (tap_seconds None), ready or not, is refused: InvalidVerbError
  from the player, nothing from an order.
- Greyed mix: a saved mix whose flattened cards include a completed one. Derived, never stored. An order naming a
  greyed mix is passed over: its mix does not run, and the next order's mix or the player's own runs (Order mix);
  when a raise or a SaveMix un-greys it, it runs again at that instant if its latch is true. In the queue, an entry whose verb is dead (a switch to a greyed mix, a tap of a
  completed card; both alike) is passed over and kept: the controlling entry is the first live one, QUEUE_POP watches
  that entry's `until`, and a dead entry is frozen (never popped while dead) unless it settles. When a raise revives
  it, it controls again at that instant, popping at once if its `until` already holds. A dead entry settles, and pops
  at that instant, once its `until` holds and cannot fall (an AfterTime, or an AtLeast on a card count, a skill level
  or an unlock): a revival would pop it at once, so keeping it would only list a finished step as the plan. This is
  how the page's "when ready" entry for a one-off (tap c until c's count reaches 1) leaves the queue: c is dead from
  the instant it completes, the instant its `until` holds. The player may still switch to a greyed mix
  (nothing runs). DeleteMix removes a saved mix not nested in another and not named by an order or queue entry;
  deleting the active one leaves no mix active, clears every pause if the running mix changed, and evaluates at once.
  Why: the decision (docs/decisions.md, "The mix") that a saved mix never quietly runs something else, and the 2 AM
  test's "falls through".
- Order mix: save.active is the player's own mix; only the player's SwitchMix and DeleteMix write it, so no order
  and no queue entry ever changes it. The running mix is the mix of the first order, in sequence, whose latch is
  true, whose verb is a SwitchMix and whose mix is not greyed; else the queue mix (below); else save.active
  (plan.running_mix_id). It is a pure
  function of the latches and of greying, never stored: a pushdown of one over the player's mix, popped when the
  latch releases or the mix greys, and the player's mix comes back. Everything above that reads "the active mix"
  (rates, dry pauses, Explain, the card panel, explain_work, runs_out_at, requires events) reads the running
  mix. Order sequence is priority among mix orders; a tap order and
  a mix order never compete: a HANDS tap yields whichever mix runs, and a tap on another lane runs beside it. The
  queue waits while an order's mix runs, except an entry taking up a hands task with kept work (CURE CUT-IN).
  Whenever the running mix changes (a latch rising or releasing, a mix
  greying or un-greying, SetOrders, SaveMix, a switch, a delete), every pause clears and rates are rebuilt at that
  instant, as a switch does; a change that leaves the running mix as it was clears nothing. A player's switch while
  an order's mix runs sets only save.active, the mix that comes back. The queue head still pops on its `until` while
  an order's mix runs; only its verb waits. Only the
  player's own mix can be greyed while it runs: a greyed order mix is never the running one. Below the firing cap a
  latch change takes effect at once, so an order's mix takes over even at an instant that then reaches the cap; past
  it, a mix order's latch change waits (Firing cap). Why: "when low on x, run Rest" means run it while low; a switch that stays lost the player's mix, and a
  latched switch, always able to act, shadowed every later order.
- Queue mix (goal stints: "run {mix} until {goal}", docs/design/worth-a-change.md): when the controlling queue entry
  (the first not dead) is a SwitchMix, its mix runs over save.active while no order's mix runs, a pushdown of one
  exactly as an order's mix is, and save.active comes back at the entry's pop (plan.queue_mix_id). Evaluation still
  picks the entry's verb last, as any queue head's, and applying it changes nothing (one firing, which ends the loop);
  its pop is a QUEUE_POP like any entry's, and pops still resolve past the firing cap, so the mix changes at the pop's
  instant even past the cap (a run of pops ends with the queue). The next live entry then controls: its mix runs if it is a switch, else save.active runs and its verb
  applies as before. A queue entry whose `until` never comes true (its goal's rate is 0, a store goal over its cap,
  a store that falls) keeps controlling and its mix keeps running: nothing passes it over, pops it or rescues the
  plan, and the entries behind it wait. A greyed queue mix is a dead entry (Greyed mix): the next live entry
  controls. Why: a switch that stood lost the player's mix at the first stint, the reason Order mix rejected it for
  orders; the queue writing save.active was never sent by the page, and no live save held such an entry.
- Retired: an Outcome retires a card by unlocking Unlock(RETIRED, card) (a newer tool replaces older work,
  docs/decisions.md, "The mix"). A retired card is out of play exactly as a completed one is, by the one predicate
  "completed or retired": it leaves View.cards for View.completed, every mix holding it greys, a tap of it is refused
  (InvalidVerbError from the player, nothing from an order) and SaveMix rejects it; a ready tap of it running when it
  retires ends at that instant as an interrupt does (work kept, lane free). Unlocks only grow, so a retirement is
  for good, and with rules.opening None (no gating) nothing is retired. Why: retirement is a fact that only grows,
  keyed by kind and id, which is what the unlock set already is; a separate set would be state, codec and FORMAT
  for the same thing. The page tells retired from finished by the unlock (api/CONTRACT.txt).
- Starved line (LedgerLine.starved): the store is at 0 and something waits on it: a runner paused on it, or a tap a
  true order or the queue's controlling entry names, blocked by an AtLeast on its value. Shown as a problem whether
  covered or not; all_covered is false while any line is starved. Why: a dry store with a waiting order read as
  covered and white, so the opening needed a spare unit to make its end wait on the right order.
- Card panel (view.py): View.cards (unlocked, not completed: completions, cap, the one bar with who runs it and when
  it next fills, share and Run in the running mix, blocker, needs, tap_s; needs are one whole completion's inputs under
  the modifiers aimed at them, constant while the bar runs, and the blocker asks only for the rest of the current
  completion's, prorated and rounded up), View.completed (the Completed tab),
  View.greyed_mixes, View.standing (every unlocked standing action, makers and consumers, in rules order, with its
  Run), and a LaneBar per lane naming the tapped card. The page samples the bar segment and draws it modulo its
  total; that is sampling, not a rule (api/CONTRACT.txt). CardView.by is who moves the work now: TAP while it is
  tapped, MIX while its mix share runs (FULL),
  else None (paused, waiting, greyed or yielded: the bar holds still). CardView.completes_at is a TAP's
  next landing (the tap keeps its speed and a rule waits for it, CURE CUT-IN), None otherwise: a share claims no
  landing, since a rule that takes the hands yields the mix partway. CardView.run is its mix share's
  state, whoever runs the card: a card tapped while in the mix reads YIELDED there while explain() gives its tap
  FULL. A card's mix Run, when several apply, is the longest-lasting: GREYED, then PAUSED, then WAITING, then
  YIELDED. explain_work: the tap if tapped; else, if the running mix holds the card, its mix share and Run
  (per_hour 0 when it does not move); else a tap started now.
- Ids: CardId and ActionId (standing) share one namespace, Runner = ActionId | CardId, so a RateRef, a Pause, a
  Running subject and a Contribution name one rate owner. The UnlockKind PROJECT is now CARD; ACTION unlocks a
  standing action only; mix use needs the CARD unlock.
- Validation of cards (InvalidRulesError): runner ids unique across standing and cards; lane HANDS or declared;
  work_s >= 1; cap None or >= 1; skill known, or None with xp_per_hour 0; flows and amounts on known stores, > 0 for
  amounts; each store at most once across a card's makes, costs, inputs and outcome stores (so no store listed twice
  in makes or in costs, as for standing actions, and none on both sides); requires with release == value and no
  Running subject; without a cap: the outcome holds stores only, `finished` is empty, at most one line; with a cap:
  lines empty or one per completion of the base cap; every CapRaise names a capped card, by > 0; every id in
  outcomes, conditions and the opening known; no chip effect aims at a StoreCap; no outcome taps a HANDS card from a HANDS completion. Only HANDS cards
  are mixable until the town's mix (a default pending the owner). An order or queue entry may name an unknown card: it is
  never tappable, covers nothing and is never a candidate.
- State, canonical: a CardState is present iff its work's value, its rate or its raise is non-zero. A free lane is
  card None.
- Evaluation after a cascade: when the firing cap suspended evaluation at instant T, READY falls at T + 1 for every
  candidate already ready, so a true order whose card was ready all along still acts and the hands never idle on the
  cap. Computed from save.last_cascade.time == save.time; no new state.
- Migration (stage 2): opening.toml's actions and projects become `standing` and `cards`; each tap/mix pair (a
  gathering tap and its mix action, a rest tap and its mix action, a felling tap and its mix action) becomes one card;
  repeatable becomes no cap, a one-off cap 1, n pushes cap n; order cards use tap_card; the template slot {project}
  becomes {card}; the opening's spare unit for the end wait goes once starved lines show. Taps now move stores over
  their bar instead of in lumps, which the opening's timings absorb (bars are seconds). FORMAT is 2: old saves decode
  as StateFormatError, which the API already answers as rules_changed or bad_state.

INVARIANTS (cards; numbered on from 33)
34. One bar: a card's work rises under a tap or under a mix share, never both; a tap started at work w lands when the
    work reaches the next multiple of work_s * BP above w.
35. One card both ways: running a card for one completion's work, by a tap or by any mix share, changes each store by
    exactly the same amount (invariant 80) and trains the same XP; no input is taken twice.
36. Linear: every store and work segment is linear between events; no event falls at an uncapped card's completion.
37. Counts: CardCount equals work // (work_s * BP) at every t; an order on it fires at the exact second the work
    crosses the boundary.
38. Caps: completions never exceed the effective cap; a capped card's COMPLETION falls at each boundary, tapped or
    mixed; a completed card's tap from the player raises InvalidVerbError and from an order changes nothing;
    `finished` lands exactly once per reach of the cap.
39. Greyed: from the instant the running mix holds a completed card, every one of its cards contributes 0 to every
    rate and orders and the queue evaluate; a cap raise un-greys it with no player action.
40. Dead queue heads: a queue whose head is dead behaves as the same queue with the head removed, and the head is
    still there after unless it settled (its `until` holds and cannot fall), when it is gone from that instant.
41. Starved: a line is starved iff its store is at 0 and a runner is paused on it or a candidate tap is blocked on it;
    all_covered is false while one is. A paused card's work does not move.
42. DeleteMix: refused for a nested or named mix (the save unchanged); otherwise the mix is gone and, if it was active,
    nothing is active and evaluation ran.
43. Order mix: view(s, r).running_mix is the mix of the first order whose latch is true and whose verb is a SwitchMix
    to a mix that is not greyed, else the controlling queue entry's mix when that entry is a SwitchMix, else
    s.active, and mix_order is that order's index (None otherwise); no evaluation ever changes s.active (only the
    player's SwitchMix and DeleteMix do); while no such order is latched and no queue entry is a SwitchMix, every
    segment equals that of the same save with its SwitchMix orders removed. When the
    running mix changes at an instant, pauses are cleared there; a player's switch that leaves it unchanged changes
    only s.active.
44. Kinds don't shadow: an order's TapCard that can act acts whether or not an earlier order's mix runs, and a mix
    order's mix runs whether or not an earlier order taps.
80. Whole completions (numbered after the last part's, added with lumps paid by work): while a card is the only line
    on a store, its work going from k to m whole completions changes the store by exactly (m - k) * A', tapped or
    mixed, at any speed and share, while the modifiers aimed at its line are unchanged and the store reaches no limit
    (its cap or 0) on the way.

REJECTED, AND WHY (cards)
- Lumps for taps (inputs taken at a tap's start, outcome landed at its end) with rates in the mix, on one bar: a card
  charged twice. The sequence: card C (input A per completion) runs in the mix at 50% for half a completion's time,
  so its bar is half full and A/2 has been drawn as a rate; the player taps C; the tap takes A at its start and runs
  the bar's remaining half; it lands, and that one completion has cost 1.5A (and, with outcome stores, paid out A/2
  as a rate plus A as a lump). Splitting the bars (a tap's own timer beside the mix's) avoids that but gives one card
  two bars, against "same card, same visual"; prorating the lump to the bar's remainder is the rate form with extra
  steps. So both use the rate form.
- Lumps for both, a completion event for every card: stores become steps, empties_at and
  "runs 7 h" stop being exact, and a season of a seconds-long card is millions of events for the simulator. Only
  completions with a discrete effect (capped cards) are events; a count is solved off the work segment.
- A tap that refuses unless its whole input is in store and then drains it as a rate: a tap of a half-done card
  would demand more than it will use; the Blocker asks for the rest of the current completion only.
- A tap that holds the hands while its card is paused: the mix would yield to a tap that is not moving, finishing
  into idleness; the tap ends, interrupted, and keeps its work.
- Standing actions as cards with a flag: a card that is never tapped, mixed or capped is not a card.
- Mixes on every lane now: a mix per lane is the town's mix, with its own questions (who owns the share, what greys);
  until then the rack is driven by orders, as it is today.
- A stored Completed set: a cap raise and the set would have to be kept in step; derived from counts it cannot drift.
- Greying only the completed card's share: the saved mix would quietly run a different split (docs/decisions.md).
- A dead queue head that waits: a greyed mix never ungreys by itself, so the hands would idle for good. Popping it:
  the plan would lose the entry a cap raise could revive (unless it has settled: a revival would pop it at once).
- Inputs once across a multi-step card: needs a per-card "first step" counter, the pushes machinery this part removes.
- One id type for cards and standing actions: nothing would say which definition an id looks up; a union of two
  NewTypes keeps one namespace and two lookups.
- The engine dressing cards (an enum on CardDef for narrative, action, craft): it changes no rule, so it is content.
- A separate starved event or chip: starved is a fact of the read model, like covered; nothing in the engine acts on
  it.
- A dead queue head that blocks the pop of the live entry behind it (the head's own `until` the only one watched):
  the live entry would control forever, so a dead head would change what the queue does. Dropping a dead tap of a
  completed card while keeping a dead switch: the two are one case (a raise revives either), so one rule.
- Interrupting a tap whose work reached its boundary at the instant its store ran dry: the count would stand without
  the landing, a completion half-registered by event order alone.
- Passing over a mix card at its boundary as a tap is passed over: the mix card does not stop there, so it would run
  on with its input store at 0, every later completion unpaid (one starved day measured 213 completions against 160
  for the paused reading); and only capped cards would get it, since only their boundaries are events, so capped and
  uncapped cards would diverge.
- Requires with hysteresis: a latch per card per requires is state the save would carry for a gate a tap reads once;
  a band belongs in an order.
- WorkRef scaling a card's flows and XP: a flow is per hour of running, and faster work is more completions, not more
  per hour; only per-completion lumps scale with it. A flow and a lump on one store, merged into one base: the
  WorkRef would then scale the flow's part too; forbidding the pair costs content nothing.
- A capped card's work limited at every completion: each completion would lose the last partial second, so a capped
  and an uncapped card at one share would drift apart.
- A lump as its own per-second rate (A / work_s under the card's whole work speed, rounded once), as before lumps
  were paid by work: a second clock beside the work's. A completion came up short or over by under one rate step
  (a 4.6 h one-item card paid 0.998 items and its beat never fired), a tap paid its lump on through the last partial
  second after its work had stopped (a 4 s card at 1.1x paid 2.2 items a tap), and a whole input could empty its
  store before the work reached the boundary, stalling the tap.
- Refusing lumps that do not divide at reachable speeds (in its tractable form, lump * work rate / (work_s * BP)
  whole at every speed iff work_s * BP divides the lump): it refuses long one-item cards, which content writes for
  story beats, and leaves the last partial second's over-pay.
- Conditions that tolerate the miss: no tolerance fits both a long card's 0.002 and a short card's 0.3 items, a
  tolerance fires early by what it forgives (invariant 8), and every reader of a store would need it.
- Lumps rounded the other way (outcomes up, inputs down): a store reached a completion's amount before the
  completion itself, so a beat keyed on it fired before the card's own line, and rounding favoured the player.
- A rate from the integer work speed, a divisibility check at load, and keeping a tap's overshoot: three patches for
  one defect, still over-paying at a capped card's last completion and refusing the long cards.
- A remainder on CardState (what has been paid so far): a Save field and a FORMAT bump for what the work segment
  already carries, and steps at completions (holds, empties_at) where the work gives a line.
- A pause dropped with the tap it interrupted: the card would restart in the mix on a store still dry and pause again
  at once, chattering at one instant.
- View.standing listing only consumers: a maker (a generator) is a standing action the player sees running too.
- Storing the player's mix in the save (a field beside active, for the mix to come back to): a new Save field fails
  decode for every live save, a reset, for state the latches already give.
- An order's switch that stands (the order writes save.active, as before Order mix): the player's mix is lost once
  the condition releases, and a latched switch, always able to act, shadows every later order.
- An order's mix taking over past the firing cap (latch changes handled after the cap like any event): two mix
  orders on a Running subject flip each other within one instant forever (one's mix stops the card, which releases
  it, the other mix runs the card, which raises it again), each flip an event with no verb, so the cap would bound
  nothing and the instant would never settle.
- Strict first match between tap orders and mix orders: the running mix would depend on whether a tap order is
  ready, and would flip within one instant when a rack tap lands.

KNOWN GAPS (cards)
- A mix card's work has no limit, so at the instant an input store runs dry its last second has run past the
  completion boundary: its outcome lumps are paid for that part (under one second's work) and the dry input, clamped
  at 0, is not charged for it. Closing it needs the work limited where the input runs out. Decided by: a card whose
  second's work pays enough to matter.
- A save written before lumps were paid by work, in the middle of a completion, keeps what the old rates had paid of
  it, under one unit a second short (or over, past a tap's last partial second): no save field records that history.
  From then on it is paid exactly by the work, so that completion lands that much off and a trigger at exactly one
  completion's amount does not fire, as on the engine that wrote it (tests/engine/test_old_lump_saves.py).

THE END (recorded in the save). Where this part and an entry above disagree, this part holds.

FIT PASS
- An instance of Triggers (docs/systems.md): a condition that holds once per save, recorded at the first instant it is
  true and never reset, which is invariant 29's rule. Two things keep it off TriggerDef: its condition reads the read
  model (every ledger line covered: covered, falling and starved), which the order vocabulary cannot solve for, and it
  records a time, where `fired` holds booleans. Making it a trigger would take an AllCovered subject and a conjunction
  in the order vocabulary that every order could then watch (change_time, latches, READY): more engine than one fact.
- The unlock set (only grows) was the other home weighed: an engine-granted Unlock would latch with no new field, but
  it carries no time, and the page must tell "just now" from "long ago"; an unlock is also something the player can
  see and use, which the end is not.
- How games do it: a completion flag with its time in the save (an achievement's unlock time), checked where the
  state changes (Nystrom's Observer chapter uses achievements as its example). The engine is pure, so the check runs
  at the tail of every resolved instant, as triggers do, rather than by a listener.

CHOICES (the end)
- The end: Rules.end names the content's end feature (data, where the moment is authored; validation: a known
  feature). Save.ended_at is None until the first resolved instant (new_save's time 0, each event instant, each
  decision's instant) at which the feature is unlocked; it is then that instant's time and never changes. The unlock
  set changes only at resolved instants, so the first resolved instant it holds at is the first second it holds.
  View.ended_at serves it. The check runs only while the end is unrecorded.
- FORMAT is 3 (Save.ended_at). Every FORMAT 2 save raises StateFormatError; its rules digest changed as well
  (Rules.end), so no older save could decode under these rules even with the field omitted.

INVARIANTS (the end; numbered on from 44)
45. First instant: over every resolved instant of a run, ended_at is None while rules.end has never been unlocked, and
    equals the time of the first instant it is; with rules.end None it is always None.
46. Never clears: once set, ended_at is unchanged by every later advance and decision.

REJECTED, AND WHY (the end)
- The page deciding the end from two live facts (the end feature and all_covered), with a page-life latch: a reload
  forgot the latch (the dusk went back to day while a line was starved) and could not tell an end a moment ago from
  one long ago (the card came at once and the end lines were skipped).
- A boolean: the page needs when, to replay the end lines only for an end that has just happened.
- Recording the end at a request's time (in the API, after an advance): a plain advance spanning the end would date it
  to the request, and splitting time would move it (invariant 1).
- Omitting ended_at from the encoding while None, to keep FORMAT 2 saves decoding: their digest changes with Rules.end
  anyway, and a save made under rules with no end could already be past it, so the first advance would date the end
  to the reload.

- Dropping an id the new rules lack: earned state lost with no error; the lock turns the same change red in CI.
- Resetting fired for a renamed or retuned trigger: its outcome would land twice.
- Positional chips and fired with a copy of the old rules in each save: the whole rules tree per save for the few
  facts a rebase reads.
- Work kept as absolute units through a work_s change: a retune would silently change completion counts, granting or
  taking back completions.
- A tapped card keeping the work left in its completion, so its shown time holds across a retune: a lump is paid by
  the bar's fraction (CARDS, "Rate form"), so a longer work_s paid the rest of that completion short (a walk retuned
  7200 -> 14400 s a quarter done paid 0.63 of its item, and the beat waiting on the item never fired). Keeping the time
  as well would mean re-settling every lump store at the rebase, the remainder the rate form exists to avoid.
- Rebasing without evaluation: a new trigger already true would wait for the next instant something else fires, which
  may be days of game time away.
- The full rebase (with evaluation) when the digest is the same: identity would break, since evaluation at load acts
  for the player where a player's tap deliberately does not evaluate.
- A digest of each trigger's outcome in the lock: renaming an id the outcome names changes the digest, so a mapped
  rename would read as a changed outcome. The lock keeps the outcome itself and compares the two after renames.

  docs/decisions.md, "The mix"), so it runs at its mix share off HANDS until the player edits the mix. Content that moves

FIT PASS
- A townsperson who works is a lane (Lanes, docs/systems.md): work beside the player's hands, one tap at a time, as
  the smoke lane already is. Its task is a tap order on that lane (Standing orders): "a side lane's rule is a single
  task, not a mix" (docs/decisions.md, "The mix"), and a tap order is already that. What is new is only that the
  player never taps such a lane by hand: LaneDef.ruled. The town's mix with people as shares waits for a second
  person (CARDS, "Mixable").
- An installed rule is an Outcome part (Triggers, Cards: "what lands") whose payload is an ordinary Order, a ruled
  lane's always rule and nothing else (a new save holds none). Nothing marks it as installed afterwards, so the player edits or
  removes it as any rule (Command: the order is data the plan holds, not a special case).
- An offer is a Type Object (OfferDef, AnswerDef) whose state lives in the unlock set, as retirement does (CARDS,
  "Retired"): shown, answered, and which answer are facts that only grow, keyed by kind and id. Save holds no new field
  (docs/systems.md, "The engine": a new part never adds one), and the gate that shows an offer is an ordinary trigger
  (Gates, Triggers) whose outcome unlocks it. Answering is a Command, AnswerOffer, which joins OrderVerb, so an order
  or the queue can issue it (docs/systems.md, "Every verb has an order").
- How games do it: a quest log is a set of flags per quest (offered, accepted, declined) checked by story conditions
  (Nystrom's Type Object for the quest kinds, Command for the answer); Idle Awakening and Melvor give a worker a task
  from a list, which here is the order list. Factorio's and RimWorld's work priorities are the per-lane order
  sequence (first true rule that can act).

CHOICES (townsfolk, installed rules, offers)
- Ruled lane: LaneDef.ruled (bool, default False). A player's TapCard of a card on a ruled lane raises
  InvalidVerbError ("set by rules"); an order, the queue or an Outcome taps it as on any lane. A ruled lane is a
  declared lane (never HANDS), so its cards are never mixable (SaveMix already rejects off-HANDS cards). Nothing
  else differs: rates, LaneSpeed, dry pauses (other lanes, rules order), landings, READY. Why: the person works by
  the player's rules, not under their finger; a hand tap would also make the row's "what the lane is doing" a third
  source.
- The lane's task is its first rule that can act: at each landing on the lane, evaluation taps the card of the first
  true tap order on that lane whose card is ready (unchanged rule, Orders). A task change (a SetOrders editing the
  rule) takes effect at the lane's next landing: the card running runs to its completion (no cancel, KNOWN GAPS
  above). "Always" is AfterTime(0): true from the first instant, never released (can_fall False). A rule on the lane
  below an always rule acts only while the always rule's card cannot, so the lane's default task is an always rule
  placed after the lane's other rules, and the page builds it so (api/CONTRACT.txt, "A lane run by rules"). Of
  several always rules on a lane, the one that acts next is the first that is not passed over.
- The queue is the hands' plan: SetQueue refuses (InvalidVerbError) an entry tapping a card on a ruled lane. The
  queue's head is never passed over, and a ruled lane's always rule retaps that lane at each of its landings before
  the queue is read, so such a head would wait forever and hold every entry behind it.
- Evaluation applies every verb that can act (replaces "It applies the TapCard of the first order ... else nothing"
  in Orders for what one evaluation does). Evaluation is a loop: pick the controlling verb, apply it (one firing),
  handle what that raises at this instant, and pick again; it ends when there is no controlling verb or applying it
  changed nothing, or at the firing cap. The controlling verb, in this order: the AnswerOffer of the first order whose
  latch is true and whose answer can act; else the TapCard of the first order whose latch is true and whose card can
  act (ready: unlocked, not completed or retired, lane free, no blocker; and its speed above 0, tap_seconds not
  None); else, while no order's mix runs, the verb of the controlling queue entry. Orders whose verb cannot act are
  skipped when choosing, never chosen, so only the queue's head (which waits, never passed over) can be a chosen verb
  that changes nothing, and it is read last. It terminates: a changing tap fills a free lane, a changing answer
  closes its offer, and the queue's switch changes nothing (Queue mix). Why: one verb per
  evaluation left every other lane idle until something else evaluated, and a ruled lane's always-true rule,
  reapplied at each of its own landings, then kept the queue's head from ever acting (a lane idling on a ready order
  is "finishing into idleness"). It is a behaviour change, shipped as below.
- Shipping the evaluation change: on its own, ahead of the rest of this part, as its own commit and its own deploy.
  It fixes a defect reachable in day one: an instant where a smoke-lane tap order and a hands verb (the queue's head,
  or a hands tap order) could both act applied only the first, and the other lane waited for the next instant that
  evaluated. Whether a shipped route or a live save actually hits such an instant is what the commit measures (below);
  nothing here assumes it. Without offers the controlling verb is the tap order, then the queue; the AnswerOffer step
  joins in front when offers land. The rule it follows is the one the SetOrders latch change followed (Rule changes):
  - The stored save is the save. Loading is the whole promise (engineering.md, "Rules from day one"): every live save
    decodes exactly as before (FORMAT and the rules digest do not change, so there is no rebase and no release is
    raised: the release lock numbers rules, never engine code), and its time away from its last write runs under the
    new evaluation, forward only. Nothing is rewritten, no flag marks old saves.
  - A save written by the old build may stand at an instant with a ready order left unapplied. It stays so until the
    next instant that evaluates (a landing, a latch, a READY, a trigger, a SetOrders), exactly as under the old build;
    invariant 57 holds only from there on, which is why it is stated for saves the new engine has run.
  - Replays diverge, logs do not break. saves/support.py's `replay` rebuilds from a day's snapshot through the logged
    decisions under the running engine and compares with the stored save. For a save whose span from a kept snapshot
    to its last write crosses an instant where the old build left a second verb unapplied, the rebuilt save differs
    (the other lane started at once, so stores, work and every later landing move), and `replay` reports no match;
    `restore` from such a snapshot would write the new engine's version. Every other save replays as before. A
    divergent save matches again once its snapshots and decisions from before the release are gone: the writer prunes
    them past 30 days (SNAPSHOT_DAYS, DECISIONS_KEPT, saves/write.py), but only when that save is written again, so a
    save still played stops diverging 30 days after the release and a save never written again keeps its mismatch.
  - Two builds during the rollout: with no release raised, old and new pods both serve a save until the rollout ends
    (engineering.md, "Deploys with two builds live"). Each write is a valid save under the build that made it and only
    moves forward, so a save's history may mix the two for those minutes; it shows in `replay` as above, nothing more.
  - The commit carries its exposure, as the latch change's did: day one's playthroughs and simulator re-run (every held
    counter that moves is named in the commit and brought to the lead before it lands, never raised to pass), and
    `python -m ashfall_app.saves replay` from every kept snapshot of every live save, read-only against prod, with
    the count of saves that no longer match and the date their logs age out. A divergent ended save is reported,
    never repaired.

THE CAMP: HELD LEVELS AND LIFTS (the loader's use of Store caps, app/ashfall_app/opening/convert.py). Where this part
and an entry above disagree, this part holds. Examples name ids only.

FIT PASS
- An instance of Store caps (CHOICES, above): the camp store's cap is its StoreDef.cap under ADD_BP raises on
  StoreCap(camp) that outcomes land, the mechanism day one already uses; this part fixes which outcomes carry them
  and how big each is, and adds no engine type, field or system.

CHOICES (the Camp)
- Terms. L1..Lk are the camp's levels after every chapter is merged, at(m) their at_items, strictly increasing. H is
  the first held level whose lifted_by is not empty. B = at(H) - 1 items is the camp store's StoreDef.cap (with no such
  level, at(Lk) and nothing below applies). S is the sum of the ADD_BP entries aimed at StoreCap(camp) in a save's
  stack, so the cap is units(B) * (BP + S) // BP (Store caps). For a level m: R(m) = ceil_div(units(at(m)) * BP,
  units(B)) - BP, the least S at which the cap reaches at(m) (the level can land); Hd(m) = R(m) - 1, the greatest S at
  which it stays below (the level cannot land). Both are exact: nothing is rounded past a level.
- The chain. The cap climbs by steps, each one raise landed by one outcome, each target an exact R or Hd:
  lift H, on the finish of any card in H's lifted_by, to R(H);
  then for each level m above H, in order: to m, when level m-1 lands, to Hd(m) if m is held, else R(m); and if m
  is held with a lifted_by, lift m, on the finish of any of its cards, to R(m).
  A step's raise is its target less the previous step's: lift H is R(H); to m is Hd(m) or R(m), less R(m-1); lift m
  above H is exactly 1 bp. "to m" is a trigger the loader makes, id "to_camp_" + m (1-based, as camp_m), condition
  AtLeast(StoreValue(camp), units(at(m-1))) (camp_(m-1)'s own), outcome that one raise, source its id. A lift is the
  raise appended to the `finished` of each card in the level's lifted_by, source the card's id. Nothing else in the
  content aims at StoreCap(camp).
- The cap at each moment: with the landed steps a prefix of the chain, S is the last landed step's target (raises
  are adds, so they telescope). So the bar holds one item under H until H is lifted, then climbs a level at a time,
  each landing opening the next, and holds just below each held level until one of its cards finishes.
- lifted_by is a list of capped card ids (`lifted_by = ["take_stock"]`); empty, the level is not lifted (and must be
  the last level, landing nothing: a later chapter fills it in at the same at_items). Two or more are alternatives:
  each is unlocked by no start and no outcome but answers' outcomes, those answers belong to one offer, and no answer
  unlocks two of them. An offer is answered once (invariant 61), so at most one of them ever finishes, and whichever
  it is lands the same raise.
- Rebase: nothing new. A release that only appends levels above the last released one (or fills in a released
  unlifted last marker at its at_items) leaves H, B and every released step's raise as they were, and only adds
  steps after them; so a save's stack, kept by rebase (invariant 49), holds a prefix of the new chain, and each new
  to_camp trigger already true fires at the rebase instant (invariant 50) and lands the rest. A save that landed the
  released last level gets the next level's room at its load.
- Release lock: adding a Camp level above the last released one is Safe (its camp_m and to_camp_m are new triggers).
  The lock holds each trigger's outcome, so a change that would alter a released step's raise (a released at_items
  retuned, B moved, a level inserted) turns a released to_camp trigger's outcome red. lifted_by is not in the lock: a
  lift rides card outcomes, which are Safe to retune, so turning lifted_by into a list or adding a card to it is Safe.
- Codec: none. to_camp_m is a TriggerDef like any other, carried by the manifest and Save.fired; no Save field and no
  FORMAT change.

REPEATING TAPS (a lane's task run as a rate, engine/retap.py). Where this part and an entry above disagree, this part
holds.

FIT PASS
- Fits into: Lanes and Cards (docs/systems.md), reusing the mix's rate machinery (CARDS, "One bar", "Rate form"): a
  card's work is one cumulative segment, its completions are counted off it, and its lumps already run as rates while
  it is tapped. A mix card's work has no limit, so its completions cost no instant; a tap's work is limited at its
  next completion, so every landing is one (TAP_DONE), and an always rule re-taps the card there. A repeating tap is a
  tap whose work, like a mix card's, has no limit. No new type, field, verb, event kind or system.
- The cost it removes: a lane's always rule turns every completion into an instant that changes nothing (the lane
  lands, evaluation taps the same card at the same second, every rate as it was). After B3 a forecast spent most of its
  MAX_INSTANTS on her re-taps, and an hour away cost an instant per completion.
- How games do it: Factorio's and RimWorld's workers are given a job and keep doing it until a priority changes; the
  simulation re-plans when something it reads changes, not on every unit of work. Here "something it reads" is the
  event set: every input of evaluation's choice already changes only at an instant.

CHOICES (repeating taps)
- The same card again: at a landing on lane L of card c, the controlling verb (Orders, "Evaluation applies every
  verb") with L free is TapCard(c). Then the per-landing engine's landing changes nothing but the work's limit: by
  invariant 57 no other verb can act at that instant, the stores and XP run at the same rates from the same second,
  and the one remaining effect is the limit moving to the next completion. A repeating tap skips that landing.
- Who may repeat: a tap of a card that is uncapped, takes no inputs and has no tap line, on a declared lane (never
  HANDS). A capped card's completions are events anyway (COMPLETION); an input's whole completion is checked at each
  landing (its Blocker reads the store there), a threshold a rate would cross unseen; a tap line is said at each
  landing. On the hands a landing lets the mix run for that instant, which re-runs the dry rule. The lane need not be
  ruled: the rule is evaluation's, and the same rules with ruled False still give the same saves (invariant 58).
  Day one's rack card takes inputs, so it keeps its landings.
- The state: a tap's mode is its work segment's limit. A bounded tap's limit is its next completion (as before); a
  repeating tap's work has none, as a mix card's has none. CardState.work already carries it, so the codec, FORMAT,
  the manifest and Save's fields do not change. rebuild keeps a repeating tap's work unlimited and every other tap's
  limited at its next completion; a fresh tap (work.tap) always starts bounded.
- When it repeats (the settle, at the end of every instant once no event is pending and evaluation has ended): a
  bounded tap that may repeat and whose task holds (the same card again, above, read at that instant) loses its limit.
  This creates no event at the instant (a work segment's limit is read only by TAP_DONE), so the instant ends.
- When it stops: a repeating tap whose task no longer holds is bounded at its next completion, and lands there
  (TAP_DONE), where evaluation picks the lane's next task as before; the card runs to its completion, as a task change
  always has (Ruled lane, "a task change takes effect at the lane's next landing"). One exception keeps it exact: at
  an instant reached by time passing (one with arrivals, so not a decision's), a tap that was repeating when the
  instant began and whose work stands exactly on a completion lands at that instant. The per-landing engine had a
  TAP_DONE there in the same instant, and evaluation ran after it, so the switch landed then. At a decision's instant
  it never lands: the per-landing engine landed and re-tapped inside the advance to that time, before the verb. The
  settle bounds or lands every lane whose task no longer holds before it lets any tap repeat, so a tap started at an
  instant is never landed at it.
- What can end a task, each an instant: a latch changing (LATCH), the orders changing (SetOrders, an install), a card
  unlocked, completed or retired, a speed changing (an outcome, a chip), a pause or an interrupt (the tapped card's
  own: dry, requires, speed 0, as for any tap), the queue popping, and a card that a true order earlier than the lane's
  own would tap there turning ready. That last is the one change no event marked while the lane was busy (READY falls
  only for a card on a free lane), so while a lane repeats, READY also falls at the ready_at of each such card that is
  unlocked, not completed and blocked (sequence and subject as a candidate's). Its handler finds the lane busy and
  asks for nothing; the settle at that instant bounds the tap. A change the other way (that earlier card turning
  unready) needs no event: the bounded tap lands, evaluation taps the same card, and it repeats again.
- Outcomes over time: flows and XP run per second at the rates the per-landing engine ran, from the same seconds, so
  they are equal. CardCount, landing times and the stores its lumps move are equal when the card's work speed divides
  its work_s * BP, which day two's content keeps (work_s a multiple of 6 under the shelter's 1.2). Otherwise each
  per-landing landing drops the last partial second's overshoot (under one second's work, clamped at its limit) and a
  repeating tap keeps it, as a mix card does: its count, and the lumps paid by its work, run ahead by at most that
  overshoot summed over the landings it skipped, a fraction of a completion per landing, and a switch lands no
  later. One effect of the per-landing engine is not kept: at a landing,
  the card stopped for that instant, so a store it feeds standing at exactly 0 that second could go dry and pause a
  consumer for an hour; a repeating card never stops, so that store does not go dry.
- What the view shows: CardView.bar is the work segment with no limit, sampled and drawn modulo bar_total as a mix
  card's bar is (its completes_at is the next completion, as for any running card); LaneBar.completes_at is None while
  the lane repeats ("the tap does not end by itself") and the next completion once it is bounded; LaneBar.order and
  card are as before. next_event_at moves past her completions, so the page samples her bar for as long as nothing
  changes and asks again only when something does. The client draws the lane row from Card.bar modulo bar_total
  already (app/client/src/ui/lanerows.ts) and does not read LaneBar.completes_at, so it needs no change.
- Lines, rule changes and decision context are unchanged: an eligible card has no tap line, and latches change at the
  same instants.
- Live saves: a save written with a bounded tap that may repeat decodes as before and repeats from its next resolved
  instant; nothing is rewritten and the release does not move (no rules change). Replays from older snapshots rebuild
  the same values (stores and XP above); the decoded save differs in that segment's limit, so `replay` may report a
  mismatch until those snapshots age out (the evaluation change's note above).

    eligible card's work speed divides its work_s * BP and no store a repeating card makes stands at 0 at one of its
    completions; CardCount equal too. Otherwise flows and XP are equal while no switch lands, and CardCount, and each
    store the card's lumps move, are at least the per-landing engine's.
76. O(1) while the task holds: advancing a save whose only running lane repeats over any span in which nothing else
    falls resolves no instant at the card's completions (no TAP_DONE among the events); invariant 1 holds across it.
77. Switch on time: a task change at T on a lane repeating card c lands c at the first completion at or after T
    (at T itself when T was reached by time passing and c's work stands on a completion; never at T for a decision),
    and the next task starts at that landing.
78. Eligible only: a tap of a capped card, a card with inputs or with a tap line, or any tap on HANDS, keeps its limit
    and lands at each completion as before.
79. View: while a lane repeats, its LaneBar.completes_at is None and its card's bar has no limit; once bounded, they
    are the next completion's time and work.
Invariant 24 reads "p starts again at each landing, or repeats, until the latch releases"; invariant 34 holds for a
bounded tap; a repeating tap lands only once bounded.

REJECTED, AND WHY (repeating taps)
- Coarser cards (perf2's first fix, shipped as her 12-120 s cards): it cut the cost tenfold and still left a p95
  forecast ratio of 2.25 against 2.4, and it bends content (lumps against caps, work_s multiples) to pay an engine
  cost that day three's lanes would raise again.
- A one-share mix on her lane (the town's mix early): a mix per lane is the town's mix, with its own questions (who
  owns a share, what greys; CARDS, "Mixes on every lane now"), and with one person a share is always all of her. It
  would change the wire and the page for a cost the engine can drop alone. The town's mix, when it comes, may
  subsume this part; that fit pass decides.
- A forecast-only shortcut (skipping or extrapolating her landings inside forecast.py): the forecast must be the
  save's own future, so that an earlier forecast lists the same times; a second resolver could drift from advance, and
  absences (Bounded advance) and the simulator would still pay per landing.
- Ending the babysitter's visit after B3 in the test harness: it narrows what the tests play and leaves the cost every
  absence pays.
- A repeat flag on LaneState (or a new state type): a codec change, FORMAT 8 and a rebase of every live save at load,
  for what the work segment's limit already says.
- No state, the mode derived at each rebuild: at the instant a task change meets a completion, the engine must tell a
  tap that ran through that completion (it lands now) from one that began there (it runs one more), and only the
  state says which.
- Any tap on any lane: on the hands a landing lets the mix run for the instant and so re-runs the dry rule, and hands
  cards carry lines; a card with inputs is checked against a whole completion at each landing.
- Ruled lanes only: the condition is evaluation's choice, which does not read `ruled`; restricting it would make
  invariant 58 false for no gain.
- Bounding a repeating tap at the task change's instant without the land-now exception: a change that coincides with
  a completion (a latch on a store crossing at that second) would run one completion more than the per-landing engine.

KNOWN GAPS (repeating taps)
- A card with inputs keeps its landings. A rate would need the whole-completion check at each completion as an event.

FIGHTS (combat's first slice: docs/design/combat-plan.md, items 1 to 3). Where this part and an entry above disagree,
this part holds. Examples name ids only; which creatures, stakes and numbers is content's.

FIT PASS
- A fight is work with odds on it, not a new kind of runner. A fight the player chooses (a hunt, clearing a path) is
  a card (Cards: "fighting is an action in your mix", docs/core-loop.md), tapped, mixed, queued or ruled like any
  card, so every verb already has an order. A threat that comes to the town (something at the stores, something at the
  camp's edge) is a standing action (Standing): it runs whenever unlocked, whatever the player does, which is
  "threats resolve on their own, with no response window". What both gain is one Component, FightDef, attached to a
  CardDef or a StandingDef (Nystrom, Component: one entity spans a second domain without the domains coupling;
  outside code, the content, supplies it, and the runner's own machinery never reads it).
- Chance never touches the engine (docs/systems.md, "The engine"), so a fight's result is a flow: the engine pays
  what a fight wins scaled by the share of fights won, and what a lost fight costs scaled by the share lost, both as
  the lines a card's lumps already run as (CARDS, "Rate form"). An uncapped card's single completion is already
  invisible to the engine (its lumps are rates), so no fight is ever an event and none costs an instant. The share
  lost is the odds; they are MUL_BP entries in the effective stack, sourced by the fight, so explain() names them
  like a chip's effects (Chips; Modifiers row: "why is it that number").
- Power and threat are two more Modifier targets with a base (Store caps' shape: a base under ADD_BP and MUL_BP,
  rounded down). Gear, skill pays, walls and guards append entries to a side's strength; the region's schedule
  appends entries to a threat's strength. Both are Type Objects in the rules (SideDef, ThreatDef), so a region's
  roster is data (Nystrom, Type Object: new kinds without code, and the typed thing holds a reference to its kind).
  "Threats ramp by region and time, never by your defences" is a validation rule on who may aim at a threat.
- The schedule's clock is a never-unlocked store filled by a standing action, the idiom day one's night and the
  visitor's clock already use; its thresholds are ordinary triggers. No region state, no Save field.
- Which fights the log calls lost is presentation: the engine keeps an exact count (a tally store, made by lost
  fights only), and the Chronicle picks which fights in a window carry the losses with a shuffle-bag draw from
  Chance (decisions.md, "Chance shows, preparation decides"). The engine never sees the draw.
- Odds are derived data, recomputed from the effective stack wherever it is read, never cached in state. Nystrom's
  Dirty Flag fits only when primary data changes more often than the derived data is read, and warns that a flag
  missed in one place serves stale data; here the inputs change only at instants, which already rebuild the stack, so
  a cache would be a second home with nothing to save.
- How games do it: Melvor and Idle Fantasy run a fight as a repeating action whose rate and loot follow your stats
  against the monster's, with the numbers shown before you start; RimWorld's raids grow with the colony's wealth and
  time, and here only the region's clock moves them (never wealth or defences, done differently on purpose). Tower
  defence's "walls and guards" are strength entries on the town's side, not a placed-unit simulation.
- What is not new: no Save field, no event kind, no verb, no system. The Combat row's "threat schedule" is content
  data until a second schedule is run beside it (docs/systems.md, Combat).

CHOICES (fights)
- Kinds. SideDef(id: SideId, base: int >= 0): who fights, by strength (the player's hands, later a lane's person,
  a region's guard). ThreatDef(id: ThreatId, base: int > 0): one entry of a roster. Rules gain sides and threats
  (tuples, rules order). Neither has state: a strength is read off the effective stack.
- Strength targets (Modifiers, Target union): SideStrength(side) and ThreatStrength(threat). Value = base under the
  entries aimed there, the Store caps arithmetic (ADD_BP summed and floored at 0, MUL_BP multiplied in), rounded down
  once. explain() over either names every entry's source, so "strength 12: base 4, spear +100%, Melee 3 +50%" is a
  query. Never a rate: modified_rate never reads them.
- Who may aim where. Entries aimed at ThreatStrength appear only in a TriggerDef's outcome, only ADD_BP >= 0 or
  MUL_BP >= BP (a threat only rises, like a store cap), and only with a source that is that trigger's id. Validation
  refuses one in a card's outcome or finished, an answer's outcome or a chip's effects. Why: "threats ramp by region
  and time, never by your defences" becomes a rule the loader cannot break; which triggers count as the schedule
  (their `when` reads only a clock store) is the content check's (combat-plan item 6). Entries aimed at SideStrength
  may come from anywhere modifiers come from, and a chip may lower one (an injury makes you weaker as well as slower).
- FightDef(side: SideId, threat: ThreatId, lost: StoreId, fought: StoreId | None = None, per_hour: int = 0,
  loses: tuple[Amount, ...] = (), wounds: tuple[Amount, ...] = ()). CardDef and StandingDef gain
  `fight: FightDef | None = None`.
  - A fight's rate f, in fights per hour at full speed: a card's completions per hour (3600 / work_s, under its work
    speed and share, exactly as its lumps run: CARDS, "Rate form"); a standing action's per_hour (> 0, required on a
    standing fight, refused on a card's, in rate units like a Flow's: RATE_SCALE per fight an hour).
  - Lines. `loses`: per lost fight, taken from a store: a cost line of the runner, RateRef(runner, COST, store).
    `wounds`: per lost fight, added to a store: a make line, RateRef(runner, MAKE, store). `lost`: one unit per lost
    fight, a make line on the tally store. `fought` (standing only; a card counts its completions, CardCount): one
    unit per fight, a make line. Each runs at amount * f, a lump run as a rate, rounded as every line is (makes down,
    costs up). Each store appears at most once across the runner's makes, costs, inputs, outcome stores, loses,
    wounds and tallies (the card rule, extended).
  - Odds. P = SideStrength(side), T = ThreatStrength(threat). loss_bp = ceil_div(BP * T, T + P), so 0 < loss_bp <= BP
    (T > 0 always; P = 0 loses every fight). win_bp = BP - loss_bp. Rounded up, so the odds never favour the player
    (the rate rule's direction). At P = T half are lost; at P = 4T one in five.
  - The odds entries. While a fight runner runs, the effective stack (gates.effective_stack) holds MUL_BP entries
    sourced "odds:" + runner: win_bp on each of the runner's ordinary makes and outcome-store lines (what a won fight
    brings), loss_bp on each loses, wounds and `lost` line. Its costs and inputs are untouched: a lost fight still
    spent the attempt (decisions.md, "Combat has fangs"). `fought` carries none. Content may not use an "odds:" source
    (validation).
  - A tap keeps its odds. A fight card's tap keeps its odds entries with its speed entries in LaneState.speed, as
    they stood at its start, and runs at them to its end (Speed: "a task runs at the speed it had when it started"):
    the odds the card showed when it was tapped are the odds it pays. In the mix, on a repeating tap (REPEATING
    TAPS) and on a standing fight they are live: a new spear changes them at its instant.
  - Dry: a loses line is a cost, so a store it drains to empty pauses the runner as any cost does (the visitor's
    pause): a raid with nothing left to take stops taking. Its tallies pause with it, so fought and lost stay true.
- Tally stores: StoreDefs no outcome unlocks (never drawn as a store), made only by their fight's line, read by
  conditions (a gate on "a third fight lost" is allowed), never cost, never an input, never shared by two fights. Cap:
  content sets it at least a year of the fastest fight rate the rules can reach; a tally at its cap stops counting
  (KNOWN GAPS).
- What a fight is not: a story fight (a beat's fight that goes as written) is a card or a trigger with lines and no
  FightDef, so a beat's outcome never carries odds. A fight card has no cap (validation; KNOWN GAPS, "Capped fights").
- View: fights, one FightView per unlocked fight runner in rules order (cards, then standing): runner, side,
  threat, power (the SideStrength Explanation), threat_strength (its Explanation), loss_bp now (for a running tap,
  the kept one), fought (CardCount for a card, else the fought tally) and lost (the tally), both whole fights (the tally stores
  floored to whole units, so lost <= fought holds). Reading writes nothing.
- Codec: SideStrength and ThreatStrength are new Target tags in a Modifier's encoding, and LaneState.speed may hold
  odds entries. No live save holds either before this part ships, so decoding old saves is unchanged and FORMAT
  stays. Confirmed against codec.py: a Target tag is one entry in its class table, and rules_digest leaves sides and
  threats out of the rules' encoding while empty, so rules that declare none keep their digest and old saves decode as
  they were (tests/engine/test_pre_fights_save.py).
- Rebase: a release that adds a FightDef to a released card changes what its lines pay (Safe, as a retuned lump);
  adding a ThreatStrength entry to a released trigger's outcome changes a locked outcome and is refused by the lock.
  A ramp step is always a new trigger.
- Held entries: a card's while_running entries join the stack the odds are read from, scaled by the card's share of
  its lane's time while it runs (ENTRIES HELD WHILE A CARD RUNS, the last part).

INVARIANTS (fights; numbered on from 79. A "fight runner" is a card or standing action with a FightDef.)
80. Flow: over a span where a fight runner runs at a constant share and P and T are constant, each of its lines pays
    exactly what the same runner with the odds entries written into its stack by hand pays; no fight is an event.
81. Timing does not matter: for any two ways of reaching time t from the same save with the same decisions at the same
    times (one advance, or any slicing into advances, views and snapshots in between), the saves at t are equal,
    tallies included. This is invariant 1 for fights, and the 2 AM test's fight half.
82. Odds move one way: adding an entry that raises a SideStrength never raises any loss_bp; one that raises a
    ThreatStrength never lowers one. 0 < loss_bp <= BP always.
83. Never by defences: no entry aimed at a ThreatStrength is in any outcome but a trigger's, and in no save does a
    ThreatStrength fall.
84. Tallies: at any t, a fight runner's lost <= fought, and lost and fought differ from the exact integrals of their
    lines by less than one unit (rounding once, as every store).
85. A tap keeps its odds: a fight card tapped at t pays at loss_bp(t) until it lands, whatever is appended meanwhile.
86. No instant: adding a FightDef to a runner adds no event to any advance (TAP_DONE and READY as without it).

CHOICES (banked time)
- Trip: an uncapped CardDef with inputs (the supplies, per completion) and makes, work_s long enough that a
  completion is a trip (an hour or more at full speed; content's number). Run in the mix it draws its inputs as cost
  lines at its share (CARDS, "Inputs against a dry store"): production goes on trips instead of past the cap. A
  reserve of the supply for another consumer is a rule on the store (Orders); a trip that drains its supply dry hands
  its share to the rest of its mix (SPENT, "Giver"). Tapped, it takes the hands for one trip, as any tap. When supplies run dry the dry rule
  pauses it last-in-mix first and resumes it at one hour's cost, so an empty camp never sends a trip.
- Uncapped, not capped. A capped card completing in a mix greys the whole mix (CARDS, "Greyed mix"), so a capped trip
  finishing at 03:00 would stop the hands until a visit: "Nothing finishes into idleness" broken by the sink meant to
  fix idleness. Uncapped, a trip's single completion is a rate (Rate form), costs no instant, and repeats as long as
  the mix runs it.
- Finds: a trip's discrete returns (a map piece, a clue, a find, a line) are ordinary TriggerDefs on
  AtLeast(CardCount(trip), n), one per n content gives, each with its outcome. CardCount is a solved subject
  (Conditions; invariant 37), so the nth find lands at the exact second the nth trip's work completes, whatever the
  share or the slicing (invariant 1). After the last find the trip still pays its makes (content: plan, BT2).
- A fighting trip: FightDef(side = the hands' SideId, threat, lost, ...) on the trip's CardDef, as FIGHTS defines.

  fangs"); its makes are scaled by the share won. Finds read CardCount, so they count trips made, won or lost:

- Comfort: a capped CardDef (cap 1) with costs or inputs of several caps' worth, a scene stamp and a pay (a

- Arrival: a trigger on a food store's level ("food to spare") unlocks an offer; its yes queues a make-room one-off

  order that answers offers of that kind (day-two plan, item 4) keeps arrivals coming while the player is away.
- No spend buys raw speed: no sink's outcome or make aims at a WorkSpeed, LaneSpeed or a rate of a card already
  running for its own sake. A comfort's pay may be a modifier like any build's (a build is how a camp gets better),

  bar, so there is nothing for a check to refuse.
- No decay or upkeep: no sink takes anything over time while nothing runs it. A trip takes only while the mix or a

THE TOWN'S MIX (people as shares: docs/design/day-three-plan.md, item 3). Where this part and an entry above

FIT PASS
- The town's mix is a Mix (Mixes, docs/systems.md: "one mechanism for both layers: the town's buildings will run
  cards, staff are shares"; docs/decisions.md, "The mix", last line). Nothing about Mix, CardShare, SubMix,

  time a mix runs: a tap on HANDS takes the whole of the player's time and every mix card yields (Lanes). A joined
  person's lane gets exactly that rule: a tap on it (only ever a rule's or an outcome's) takes the whole of that
  person and their shares yield until it lands. So the first person's installed and player-set tap rules keep
  working unchanged after the join, as taps over the mix, which is how "the first person's rules become the town's
  standing orders" holds without converting them.
- Which town mix runs is set the way a ruled lane's task is: by its always rule, Order(AfterTime(0), SwitchMix(m)),

- How games do it: RimWorld and Dwarf Fortress give each colonist a work-priority table over shared jobs; Banished

CHOICES (the town's mix)
- Layer: a mix's layer is derived from its flattened cards: HANDS if every card is on HANDS, TOWN if every card is on
  a ruled lane; a mix with no card is TOWN when a join of the rules makes it (RulesIndex.town_mixes) or its
  Mix.layer is TOWN, else HANDS. A mix holding both raises InvalidVerbError at SaveMix; nesting a mix of the other layer
  is the same refusal (checked on the flattened result). plan.mix_layer(mixes, mix) is the one place it is read. Mix.layer
  is set where a mix is emptied: SaveMix of a mix with no component keeps the TOWN the saved mix of that id read, and a
  leave marks each mix it empties that no join made (a verb's own value is replaced; a mix with cards is read from
  them); it is left out of the encoding while HANDS, so no save changes its text and FORMAT stays. Why a field and not the cards alone: read by its cards
  alone, the game's town mix a leave empties (LEAVES, step 4) or a join makes with no share card was HANDS, and the
  town's always rule then took the hands for good (a player's switch set only save.active beneath it); as TOWN, its
  rule runs nothing and the player can still edit it back into use.
- Joined: UnlockKind gains JOINED, id a LaneId. A ruled lane's person is present once Unlock(JOINED, lane) is held;
  present lanes are rules.lanes order filtered by that. Only Outcome.joins adds it (validation refuses it in any
  Outcome.unlocks and in Rules.opening, as ANSWERED), and it is written to save.unlocked whatever rules.opening is,
  as ANSWERED and ANSWER are. Why not a LaneDef flag: who has joined is the story's moment, not the lane's shape (a
  person's lane runs a build before they join), and some ruled lanes are never people (a cure lane whose task an
  installed rule sets).
- Town share: in a TOWN mix a CardShare's share_bp is basis points of the time of the person whose lane the card is
  on. SaveMix of a TOWN mix refuses (InvalidVerbError): a card on a lane not joined, a card with a cap, and any lane
  whose flattened shares sum above BP. A HANDS mix is checked as before (sum <= BP). The engine never moves a share
  between people, nor between cards (Shares: idle is shown, never redistributed).
- Uncapped only: a TOWN mix holds no capped card (refused above), so it greys only by retirement (Greyed mix, Retired,
  unchanged). A person's one-off is their lane's rule tap (the day-two idiom: an installed first=True always rule),
  which yields their shares while it runs and passes over once done. Why: a town mix greyed by a finished build
  would idle every person in it until a visit, the 2 AM test's failure, for work a tap rule already does.
- Running town mix: plan.running_town_mix_id(save, rules) is the mix of the first order, in sequence, whose latch is
  true, whose verb is a SwitchMix of a TOWN mix and whose mix is not greyed; else None (nobody runs shares). The
  hands' running mix (Order mix) reads only SwitchMix orders of HANDS mixes, then the queue mix, then save.active; so orders of one
  layer never change the other's running mix, and order sequence is priority within a layer only. Everything Order
  mix says of the hands' pushdown holds for the town's: a pure function of latches and greying, rates rebuilt and
  every pause cleared when either running mix changes, a latch change past the firing cap waits (Firing cap).
- Verbs: a player's SwitchMix of a TOWN mix raises InvalidVerbError ("set by rules"), as a player's TapCard on a
  ruled lane does; SetQueue refuses an entry whose verb is a SwitchMix of a TOWN mix (the queue is the hands' plan);
  SetOrders accepts one (it is the town's rule) when the mix is in save.mixes, as for any SwitchMix, so a town mix's
  rule can be set only after its join has created the mix. save.active never names a TOWN mix: DeleteMix and SaveMix
  are as before, and the only writer of active is the player's HANDS switch. A player's SaveMix of the
  running town mix applies at once (rates rebuilt, pauses cleared), as SaveMix of the running hands mix does.
- Reserved ids: a MixId beginning "town:" is content's. SaveMix refuses (InvalidVerbError) one not already in
  save.mixes, so only a join creates it; once created the player edits it as any mix. Why: mix ids are the player's
  own text, never renamed, so a join's id must be one no player can already hold.
- Rates on a joined lane L: while L holds no tap, each card of the flattened running town mix that is on L runs at
  its share, as a hands mix card does on HANDS (Rate form, dry pauses, L's
  LaneSpeed joins its stack, its lumps paid as rates). While L holds a tap, every running town card on L is YIELDED
  (0/1, share kept and shown) and runs again when the tap lands; cards of the same mix on other lanes are not
  affected. A tap on L is started only by an order, an Outcome's start, or nothing (the player never taps a ruled
  lane), and evaluation reads L as free whenever it holds no tap, whatever its shares run (as HANDS is free while
  its mix runs). A card of a TOWN mix on a lane not joined, or unlocked-not, runs nothing (its share idles).
- Dry pauses: TOWN mix cards are mix consumers. A dry store pauses the hands mix's consumers last-in-mix first (as
  before), then the running town mix's last-in-mix first, then other lanes' taps (rules order, as before). Any fixed
  order would do (the hour of hysteresis prevents chatter); this one leaves the hands' pause order exactly as today,
  so no day-one or day-two route moves.
- No XP: a card on a ruled lane carries no skill and no XP line; validation (InvalidRulesError) now refuses it in the
  engine, not only in the loader (docs/decisions.md, "You and the town": townsfolk do not level). Camp make flows
  (CampEntry) are unchanged.
- Join: Outcome.joins, a tuple of Join(lane: LaneId, mix: MixId, card: CardId | None = None). Allowed only in a
  TriggerDef's and an AnswerDef's outcome (once-only, as installs). It lands after `orders` and before `queues`.

  1. add Unlock(JOINED, lane);
  2. if save.mixes holds no mix `mix`, append Mix(mix, ()) and, unless save.orders already holds an equal order,

     append, never insert: a town rule set by the player above it keeps priority;
  3. the share card: the card of the first order on L that is an always rule (AfterTime(0), TapCard) of an uncapped

REJECTED, AND WHY (the town's mix)
- A mix per person (each ruled lane its own mix): "one mix whose shares are people's time"
  (docs/design/day-three-plan.md, item 3) and the Mixes row's staff-as-shares both want one table the player edits
  at once; per-person mixes would need a per-lane
  running mix and so a per-lane always rule to switch each, multiplying rules for one decision.
- A pooled town lane (people times BP of anyone's time over shared town cards): content's cards are per person, and
  pooling loses who is doing what, which the scene and the lines draw. Pooled work is KNOWN GAPS.
- The town's own active mix (save.active for the town, set by a player's switch): a new Save field, and a second way
  to say what the always rule says (the same refusal as a lane's default task).
- A static LaneDef.person flag: changes a released lane (the first person's lane shipped in day two), cannot say
  when someone joins, and a cure lane is ruled without being a person.
- Deriving who is present from the town mix's cards: a person dropped from the split would vanish from the strip
  instead of standing idle, which is the honest picture (Shares: idle is shown).
- Converting the first person's tap rules into SwitchMix orders: it invents a mix per rule; taps that yield are the
  hands' rule and keep the first person's rules working as they were set.
- Taps on a joined lane running beside its shares: a person would work more than one person's time, and "shares sum
  to the people present" would be false.
- Capped cards in a TOWN mix: one finished build greys the whole town mix and idles everyone until a visit; a rule tap
  already does a person's one-off.
- A FORMAT bump: nothing in the codec changes shape; the content release already keeps two builds off one save.
- A new event at the join: a join lands inside an outcome, at an instant that already evaluates.

KNOWN GAPS (the town's mix)

- Pooled work (a building any townsperson can staff, wide expeditions as several people on one trip): a card on no
  one person's lane needs a share unit of "people", not one person's BP. Decided by: the first staffed building or
  the first wide expedition (BANKED TIME, KNOWN GAPS, "Wide expeditions": a trip card on one person's lane at a

- An always rule the player writes on a joined lane through the full editor: the engine accepts it and it yields that

FIT PASS
- People (a person is a ruled lane, present once joined), Lanes, Mixes, Standing orders and Unlocks, each run
  backwards. Joining is an outcome part landing once with an only-growing fact; leaving is the same: an outcome part
  whose fact, this lane's person has left, is one more only-growing unlock kind. No Save field, verb, event kind or
  system: what follows from the fact (the lane's cards out of play, the listed standings stopped) is derived wherever
  it is read, as retirement is; what the player's plan held (orders, shares) is edited once, as a join edits it.
- Retirement already says "out of play for good, by an only-growing fact"; a leave retires a lane's cards by that one
  predicate, except that it never greys a mix: a person's shares are their own time (THE TOWN'S MIX, "Town share"), so
  removing them changes nobody else's, and greying would stop every lane for one person.
- How games do it: RimWorld and Banished remove a colonist and their work-table rows go with them, the other workers'
  priorities untouched; a departed companion's skills leave the party sheet (Nystrom: Command, the leave a part in the
  log; Type Object, a lane's cards are data that go out of play by a fact, never deleted from the rules).

OFFER KINDS (answering a later offer by rule). Where this part and an entry above disagree, this part holds. Examples

FIT PASS
- An instance of Offers (a Type Object: the kind is data on the offer, as the answer is) and of Standing orders (one
  more order verb, as AnswerOffer was). No new system, no new state: an offer's state is still its three unlocks.
- How games do it: a quest log answering "every offer of this type" is a filter over the log (Nystrom's Type Object:

CHOICES (offer kinds)
- OfferDef gains `kind: KindId | None` (default None) and AnswerDef `role: RoleId | None` (default None); KindId and
  RoleId are new NewTypes. A kind groups offers that ask the same question; a role names what an answer means in it
  ("yes", "no"), the same in every offer of the kind. Validation (InvalidRulesError): an offer with a kind has a role
  on every answer, no two of its answers share a role, and every offer of that kind has the same set of roles (their
  order and ids may differ); an offer without a kind has no role on any answer. A kind with one offer is valid.
- AnswerKind(kind: KindId, role: RoleId), an OrderVerb and a Verb (the player's decide refuses it, below). It can act
  while some offer of `kind` is open (shown, not answered). Applying it applies AnswerOffer(o, a) where o is the first
  open offer of `kind` in rules.offers order and a is o's answer whose role is `role`: the same unlocks, outcome and
  lines as that AnswerOffer, nothing else. It is resolved when chosen, not stored: nothing in the save names o or a.
- Never dead: is_dead is False for an AnswerKind, so an order holding one is never PASSED_OVER (its state is ON while
  its latch is true, WAITING while it is not), and it keeps answering each later offer of the kind as it shows. A rule
  that has nothing to answer simply cannot act, as an AnswerOffer of a not-yet-shown offer cannot.
- Evaluation: the controlling verb's first source stays "the answer orders", now the first order whose latch is true
  and whose verb is an AnswerOffer that can act or an AnswerKind that can act, in the orders' own order; an AnswerKind
  is chosen as the AnswerOffer it resolves to. The evaluation loop already runs on after each firing, so two open
  offers of one kind are answered at one instant, each a firing under the firing cap. An offer shows only when an
  outcome lands, which evaluates, so a true AnswerKind answers an offer at the instant it shows, and one whose latch
  rises later answers at that LATCH, exactly as an AnswerOffer order does ("No new event", above). The cap's READY
  at T + 1 counts an AnswerKind that can act as it counts an AnswerOffer that can act.
- Refusals. A player's decide of an AnswerKind raises InvalidVerbError (the player answers an offer, by id). SetQueue
  raises InvalidVerbError for an entry holding one: the queue's head is never passed over and an AnswerKind is never
  dead, so a head that had nothing to answer would hold every entry behind it for good. SetOrders accepts an
  AnswerKind whose kind and role are declared by some offer (one with no open offer yet is accepted: planning ahead);
  an undeclared kind, or a role the kind does not have, raises InvalidVerbError.
- Covered: an AnswerKind covers no store. Decision context records an order's answer as it does an AnswerOffer's,
  with the resolved AnswerOffer's ids, since that is what was applied.
- Codec: FORMAT stays 8 and no upgrade is added. A manifest offer gains `kind` (the kind id) and `roles` (the role
  ids, in the offer's answer order, so roles[i] belongs to answers[i]) only when the offer has a kind; an offer
  without one is written as before, {"id", "answers"}, and the reader accepts both shapes. Likewise the rules
  encoding leaves a None `kind` or `role` out, so rules that use no kind keep their digest, their manifest text and
  every save's encoding: a format 8 text decodes as it did, and a text from before this part reads as every offer
  unkinded, which is what it was. (The plan called for a format upgrade that adds none; with the optional keys there
  is nothing for one to do, and a bump would change the FORMAT every released save and test is pinned to.) AnswerKind
  joins the codec's classes and _VERB_TYPES; Save's fields are unchanged (app/tests/held/save_fields.json).
- Rebase: a kind and a role are never renamed (the release lock forbids changing one), so renaming passes an
  AnswerKind through. Step 4 also checks that an offer the manifest gives a kind has, in the new rules, that same
  kind, and each answer the manifest gives a role the same role (RebaseError(OFFER, id) for the offer); an offer the
  manifest gives no kind may have gained one. A save's AnswerKind order is then valid under the new rules because
  its kind and roles are.
- Release lock: ids.json gains `offer_kinds` (offer id to kind, kinded offers only) and `answer_roles` (answer id to
  role, answers with a role only). Safe: give a kind, and roles on its answers, to a released offer that had none; a
  new kinded offer; a new answer with a role. Forbidden: change or remove a released offer's kind; change or remove
  a released answer's role (offer and answer ids compared after renames, as an id's kind is). Why forbidden: a saved
  order may hold AnswerKind(kind, role) and means it against every offer of the kind, past and to come, so the
  meaning of a name is as fixed as an id's.
- Content (the loader, app/ashfall_app/opening): OfferEntry gains `kind` (optional) and AnswerEntry `role` (optional).
  The loader fails, naming the offer, for a role on an offer without a kind, a kinded offer's answer without a role,
  two answers of one offer with one role, and two offers of one kind whose role sets differ. Day three's arrival
  offer and day five's carry the arrival kind; the engine and the loader are tested on a fixture chapter.

ENTRIES HELD WHILE A CARD RUNS (a watch or a guard raising a side while it works: docs/design/week-two-plan.md, item 2;
blind tests from this part and api/CONTRACT.txt alone, item 3, against app/tests/engine_contract/MUTANTS_held.txt).
Where this part and an entry above disagree, this part holds. Examples name ids only; which cards, sides and amounts

FIT PASS
- An instance of Modifiers (an entry with a source; docs/systems.md, "Modifiers") placed by Lanes and Mixes (what runs

  is true: a chip's effects while it shows (Chips) and a fight's odds while it runs (FIGHTS, "The odds entries"). This

CHOICES (entries held while a card runs)
- Shape: CardDef gains `while_running: tuple[WhileRunning, ...] = ()`, WhileRunning(side: SideId, amount_bp: int), a
  new frozen type exported by the engine package. Each is an ADD_BP entry on SideStrength(side) at a whole share. A
  dedicated type, not a Modifier: kind, target and source are fixed by the rule, so a held entry aimed at a threat, at
  a rate, as a multiplier or under another source cannot be written. Standing actions do not take one (REJECTED).
- Validation (InvalidRulesError): each side is one rules.sides declares; amount_bp >= 1; no side twice in one card's
  while_running; and no Modifier in the rules (outcomes, finished, chip effects) has a source beginning "held:", the
  engine's tag below. new_save's starting stack with such a source raises InvalidVerbError, as one with "odds:" does
  (every refusal of a starting-stack entry is that error). A WhileRunning message names the card id and the side id;
  a test may assert both appear, and nothing else of its wording. Side and threat ids are separate kinds and no check
  spans them, so a threat may share a side's id; a held entry then lands on SideStrength(id) only and that threat's
  strength is untouched.
- The held share h(c) of card c, in basis points of its lane's time, read off the save at an instant:
  1. BP while a lane holds a tap of c: a tap by the player, an order, the queue or an Outcome's start, on HANDS or on
     a declared lane, bounded or repeating (REPEATING TAPS). This covers a queued or rule-run hands task, a ruled
     lane's always task and a lane's one-off.
  2. Else, when c is in the running mix of its own layer (the hands' running mix for a HANDS card, the running town
     mix for a card on a ruled lane; CARDS, "Order mix"; THE TOWN'S MIX, "Running town mix"), its flattened share
     there (Nested mix share: already a whole number of basis points) when its Run is FULL: not
     yielded to a tap on its lane, not paused, not waiting on a requires, its mix not greyed, and for a TOWN mix card
     its lane joined and the card unlocked.
  3. Else 0: a card unlocked but in no running mix and untapped, a yielded share, a paused or waiting card, a greyed
     mix, a completed or retired card, a When ready entry still waiting, a mix the player saved but no order or switch
     runs (save.active is read only through the running mix).
  A card both tapped and in the running mix counts once, at BP (rule 1), its mix share being yielded (One bar). A card
  sits on one lane and a mix holds one layer, so no card has two shares. SaveMix refuses a locked card and a card on an
  unjoined lane, so the only way play puts a locked card in a running mix is a Join whose share card (its lane's
  always rule's card, or Join.card) is not yet unlocked; that is the path rule 3's "unlocked" exists for, and a test
  reaches it so. An unjoined lane's card in a TOWN mix is not reachable by play (a join adds JOINED before the share);
  rule 3 defines it for completeness and no test needs it. The hands' mix never holds a locked card (with
  rules.opening None every card counts as unlocked).
- Why the share: the share is the time the player gave the card, and its idle remainder goes to nobody (Shares);
  the rates of a fight's lines read the odds and the odds read the side, so reading a rate here would make the stack
  depend on the rates it sets. Speed likewise: a mix card's LaneSpeed changes how fast its work
  fills, never its share. A tap at speed 0 never holds a lane (it is interrupted: CARDS, "Inputs against a dry store").
- Why paused, waiting and yielded count 0: each is the card not running and its time given to nothing or to another
  card. A guard whose food runs dry stops guarding, as a raid with nothing left to take stops taking (FIGHTS, "Dry").
- The entries: while h(c) > 0, the effective stack holds, for each WhileRunning(side, a) of c in declaration order,
  Modifier(SideStrength(side), ADD_BP, a * h(c) // BP, SourceId("held:" + c)); while h(c) = 0, none. Rounded down
  once, on the flattened share, so it never favours the player; an entry whose product rounds to 0 is still held at
  amount 0 (the card runs; the formula is unchanged by it). The strength then rounds once more by its own formula
  (FIGHTS, "Strength targets"). So the two zeros differ: h(c) = 0 (paused, yielded, not running) means no entry, and
  explain lists none for c; h(c) > 0 with a product below BP means an entry at amount 0, which explain lists and the
  wire serves with its share_bp.
- Why a tagged source and not the bare card id: a card's own outcome may land a permanent entry sourced by its id (the
  Camp's lifts do), and the page must tell the held one from it to show the share (View, below).
- Where the engine reads it: gates.base_stack becomes the save's modifiers, then the shown chips' effects, then the
  held entries of every card in rules.cards order. Everything built from base_stack sees them: explain_strength,
  strength, the odds entries (odds_entries reads base_stack), FightView's power and threat_strength, and so
  effective_stack. They aim only at sides, so no rate, work, lane speed or cap stack changes, and modified_rate never
  reads them. h(c) reads only discrete state at the save's instant (lanes' taps, latches and greying through the
  running mixes, save.mixes, save.pauses, requires against values at save.time, unlocks): never rates or holds, so
  building the stack never recurses into running(). The stack's memo (stack_inputs) must then cover that state when
  any card declares while_running.
- Cut-in and interrupt: a task interrupted with its work kept (CUT-IN, CURE CUT-IN, a requires falling, a pause, a
  retirement) leaves the lane, so its entries leave at that instant; the card that cuts in holds its own (rule 1);
  when the interrupted task is tapped again its entries return at that instant. Kept work is progress, not presence:
  the bar waits, the person does not stand watch while doing something else. A cure's cut-in is the same as a glowing
  step's, word for word: the interrupted task's entries leave at the cut-in, the cure's own (if any) are held while it
  runs, and the task's return when an order or the queue taps it again; a hand tap the cure sends to the queue's front
  holds nothing while it waits there.
- Pause: a dry pause drops a mix card's entries at the instant it lands and they return at its RESUME. The dry rule
  pauses one consumer at a time and rebuilds after each, so each net is read under the stack the pauses so far leave
  (pausing a guard can raise a fight's losses on the very store that ran dry, and the next pick sees that). The order
  is rates.Running's read backwards, among the store's consumers not yet paused: taps on declared lanes (lanes in
  reverse rules order), then a HANDS tap, then the running town mix's cards last-in-mix first, then the hands' mix
  last-in-mix first, then standing actions in reverse rules order. So a guard (a share or a tap) and a standing raid
  on one dry store: the guard pauses first; the raid pauses at that same instant only if the store is still dry once
  the guard's pause (and so its lost entry and the raid's higher loss rate) is rebuilt. It terminates as before: each
  step adds a pause.
- Completion and locking: an uncapped card's completions change nothing. A capped card's tap ends at the completion
  that reaches its cap and its mixes grey (CARDS, "Caps"), so its entries leave there. A cap raise un-greys every mix
  holding the card at that instant (Caps; Greyed mix): if an un-greyed mix is then its layer's running mix, its cards'
  entries return at that instant with no tap; a card that only a tap ran returns when something taps it. A retirement
  ends a tap and greys mixes for good. Unlocks only
  grow, so no card is locked again; a card never unlocked is never tapped and its town share runs nothing (rule 3).
- A tap keeps its odds (FIGHTS): a HANDS fight tap's kept odds are read, as today, on the save just before the tap,
  so the odds the card showed are the odds it pays. Held entries the tap then yields stay in those kept odds, and a
  held entry that comes or goes during the tap moves them only from its end. Mixed fights, fights on a repeating or
  any declared-lane tap, and standing fights read the live stack and move at the instant.
- Instants: h(c) changes only at an instant where a tap starts, lands or is interrupted; a running mix changes (a
  latch, a greying or un-greying, SetOrders, SaveMix, a switch, DeleteMix, a join); a pause lands or resumes; a
  requires turns (REQUIRES); an outcome unlocks, retires or joins. Each of those already rebuilds every rate at that
  instant, so the stack, the odds and every fight line's rate are constant between events (docs/systems.md, "The
  engine"), and no event kind is added. Reading writes nothing: the entries are never in save.modifiers nor in a
  LaneState.speed (they aim at no LaneSpeed, WorkRef or odds line), so encode, the codec and the save are untouched.
- Repeating taps: a repeating card holds its entries through its completions. The per-landing engine (invariant 75)
  drops them for the landing's instant and re-taps, which can re-run the dry rule there with them absent; invariant
  75's exception therefore also covers a store a fight on a held card's side touches standing at 0 at one of that
  card's completions. Same totals otherwise.
- View: View gains `held: tuple[HeldView, ...]`, HeldView(card: CardId, share_bp: int, entries: tuple[Modifier,

- Codec: the rules encoding leaves `while_running` out while empty (as a None `fight`), so rules without it keep
  their digest and every save its text; WhileRunning joins the codec's classes for the rules encoding only. No Save
  field, no manifest change (the manifest's cards carry id, work_s, lane and cap), FORMAT unchanged.

REJECTED, AND WHY (entries held while a card runs)
- A chip that raises (the plan's first alternative): it bends chips, which are bad states with cures.
- A store-backed side (a watch store the guard fills and the side reads): modifiers never read stores.
- A capped "post a guard" one-off landing a permanent entry: permanent, so moving someone off the watch would not
  lower the side, and the share would stop being a decision.
- Reading the Running subject: the stack would depend on the rates it sets (CHOICES, "Why the share").
- Keeping the entry while an interrupted task's work is kept, or while its card is paused: one person's time would
  hold two cards' entries at once (the cut-in card's and the waiting task's), and a starving guard would guard.
- Multipliers held at a share: amount * s of a factor lowers the side below its base at a small share, and the other
  reading (scaling the excess over BP) is a second formula for one idea; an add scales linearly with one meaning.
- Held entries on rates, work, lane speeds or caps: a card's own effect on rates is its lines; a speed another card
  holds would change a HANDS tap's kept speed by who else works; a cap that comes and goes is refused for chips for
  the same reason (Store caps). A threat: invariant 83, and the type cannot name one. Amended 2026-11-10: a lane speed
  off HANDS is allowed, since no other lane keeps a speed (HELD SPEED, "Why lane speed is now allowed"); rates, work,
  caps, threats and HANDS stay refused.
- while_running on standing actions: a standing action runs whenever unlocked, so its held entry would be an outcome
  entry landed at the unlock, which content already writes. Lifted 2026-11-10: a standing action paused on a dry store
  does not run, and that pause is the state a fed bonus turns on (HELD SPEED, "Why standing actions may now hold
  entries").
- The bare card id as the source: indistinguishable from the card's permanent entries, so the page could not show
  the share (CHOICES).
- The kept odds re-read with the tap running (or live): the odds a card showed would not be the odds it pays.
- A new event when a share changes, or a held-entry state on the save (cached, with a dirty flag): every change is
  already an instant that rebuilds, and a cache is a second home (FIGHTS, Dirty Flag).
- CardInfo carrying each card's whole-share amounts so the page can show what a share would give: the page would
  multiply by the share itself, a rule kept twice; the served entry says what the share gives now.
- A loader check that some fight names the side: the entry then changes no number and draws nowhere, a typo is
  already refused as an undeclared side, and a later chapter may add the fight.

DRAINS (a take forgiven at 0) AND FRACTIONS AT THE LOADER (week one's lost-fight chip, open-work item 10). Where this

FIT PASS
- The need: a chip on a store x that shows at x >= θ, a cure that takes from x, a cap C, and three wants: the cure is
  tappable at every level the chip shows, one tap clears the chip from any level, and C holds several whole hurts.
  With a fixed take A per completion (an input) no content can give all three, at any scale: a tap needs the rest of
  its completion's inputs in store, so tappable at x = θ needs A <= θ; one tap clears from x = C needs C - A < θ; so

- A drain is an input with the dry rule turned the other way, which is Cap's rule mirrored at 0: at a cap overflow is
  lost and the makers keep running (CHOICES, "Cap"); at 0 a drain's underflow is forgiven and its card keeps running.

  every other consumer). No new type (a drain is an Amount), Save field, event kind, verb or system; CardDef gains one

CHOICES (drains)
- Shape: CardDef.drains: tuple[Amount, ...] = (). Cards only: StandingDef has no drains (REJECTED, KNOWN GAPS).
- Paid as an input: A', the drain's quantity under the modifiers aimed at RateRef(card, COST, store) alone and rounded
  up (an input's rule, lumps.lump_amount), moves the store by A' * dW / (work_s * BP), dW the card's work, whoever runs
  it, at any speed or share: no lump at a tap's start or end. Its line is a COST line, as an input's is: a modifier
  aimed at it acts as on an input, and the fight odds leave it untouched, as costs and inputs.
- The dry rule turned the other way. Let n be the store's exact net from every line but its drains, after the dry
  rule's pauses, and d the drains' sum. Above 0 the store moves at n - d, and reaching 0 is a STORE_LIMIT event as
  for any store: at the first whole second its exact value is at or below 0, where it reads 0. At 0: the dry rule
  pauses the store's other consumers as if no drain were there (last first, until n >= 0, each Pause's level one hour
  of that consumer's cost, as before: a heal costing 3 an hour resumes when the store first holds 3 items); then, if
  n - d >= 0, the store moves at n - d and every drain is paid in full; else the store stays at 0 at rate 0 and the
  drains take exactly n between them, the rest forgiven (lost, never created, as at a cap). What a drain takes is recorded nowhere (it makes nothing), so how n is shared
  among several drains at 0 is not defined and nothing can observe it.
- Never pauses its card: a drain never pauses, interrupts or makes WAITING the card it is on; the card's work, makes,
  XP, outcome stores and other inputs run on while the drain takes less or nothing. A mix card at any share keeps
  Run(MIX, FULL) with its drain store at 0, as with it above.
- Up to its amount: over any span a drain takes at most A' * dW / (work_s * BP), and exactly that while its store is
  above 0 for the whole span; so at most A' per completion of work.
- Only drains are forgiven: the card's inputs and costs keep the dry rule as they were. An input store at 0 pauses the
  card (CARDS, "Inputs against a dry store"), which stops its drains too. The invariant "a completion is never finished
  unpaid" (CARDS, the same entry) holds with this one exception: the unpaid part of a drain.
- Ready above 0: at a tap's start each drain's store must hold at least 1 quantity unit. The card's blocker is its
  first unmet precondition read in the order inputs, then drains (in declaration order), then requires, then a Pause;
  a drain's is AtLeast(StoreValue(store), 1, 1), its ready_at the first whole second the store's segment holds 1 unit
  (None while nothing raises it). A player's tap with a drain store at 0 is a failed tap (its failed_line, nothing
  changed); an order's or the queue's changes nothing and logs no line (invariant 23), and READY falls at the blocker's
  ready_at, as for any blocker; so a ruled lane's rule taps the card again only once the store exceeds 0. Once started,
  a tap never ends for a drain: it lands at its completion with the store at 0 if it got there.
  In the mix nothing is asked: a mix card whose drain store is 0 runs and takes nothing from it.
- Read model: CardView.needs lists a drain's store at quantity 1 (what a tap's start asks), after the inputs'
  whole-completion amounts. Starved is unchanged: a candidate tap blocked by a drain's blocker makes its store starved,
  as any AtLeast blocker does (no special case; a drained store is never drawn in the content that uses one).
  Explain and the ledger read a drain as an input line; its Explanation is its display rate, never clamped, so at 0 with
  drains forgiven the store is at a limit (invariant 10's sum does not apply there), net_per_hour sums the drain in
  unclamped as at a cap, and falling reads the segment (rate 0 there: not falling). A drain covers nothing.
- Repeating taps: a tap of a card with drains is never repeat-eligible, as one with inputs is not (REPEATING TAPS): its

- Cures: cure_reading.takes counts each drain's store, so a card that drains the store of an at_least chip is a cure

- Validation (InvalidRulesError, its message holding the card's id and the store's): each drain on a declared store,

- Codec: the rules encoding leaves `drains` out while empty (as `while_running`), so rules without a drain keep their

- Refused, never rounded: an inexact fraction fails the load naming the entry, as an inexact ItemAmount does. Every
  refusal of this part is a ValueError whose message holds the entry's id (the card, standing action, trigger or chip;
  for a drain on a tally store, the card or the store), raised by load_opening or by to_rules: either stage, since
  no rules exist until both have run.

INVARIANTS (drains and fractions; numbered on from 120)
121. Never owed: no store is ever below 0 (invariant 16), and a card is never paused, interrupted or WAITING because
    of a drain: with its drain store at 0 and its other preconditions held, a mixed card keeps its Run and a tap keeps
    its lane, and its work moves at its speed.
122. Up to its amount: with a drain of A' the only line on its store x, x at t equals max(0, x0 - A' * (W(t) - W(t0)) /
    (work_s * BP)) to within one unit below and never above the exact rational (invariant 11): exact - 1 < x <= exact,
    so x equals it wherever it is whole, at every completion among them; W the card's work; once x is 0
    it stays 0 until something adds to it. A card's completion takes at most A' from x.
123. Ready above 0: a player's TapCard of a card whose drain store holds 0, every other precondition met, is a failed
    tap (invariant 22) with blocker AtLeast(StoreValue(x), 1, 1); with x at 1 unit or more it starts, and x is unchanged
    at the start instant (no lump at the start). An order or queue entry naming it changes nothing at 0 and starts it
    at the instant x first exceeds 0 (READY), the lane free.
124. One tap clears: a tap of a card with drain A' started with 0 < x <= A' and nothing else on x lands at its
    completion (start + its usual time), never interrupted, with x at 0 from the second the work has paid x (to within
    one unit) to the landing.
125. Forgiveness is the drain's alone: for a card with drains and inputs, the inputs, costs and their pauses equal
    those of the same card without its drains at every t; an input store running dry pauses the card (and so its
    drains), and no completion lands with an input unpaid.
126. Other consumers pause: at a drain's store at 0, every other consumer of it is paused or running exactly as in the
    same save without the drain's card running: a standing cost on x with no inflow is PAUSED while a drain runs on x
    at 0, and resumes as before (one hour of its cost); and an inflow above that consumer's cost keeps it running.
127. The remainder rises: at x = 0 with n the net of x's other lines and d its drains, x rises at n - d when that is
    >= 0 and stays at 0 otherwise; a drain never makes x rise slower than n - d, nor stay at 0 while n > d.
128. Codec and digest: for rules with no drain, encode, the digest and the manifest equal those of the engine before
    this part; adding a drain to a card changes rules_digest; decode(encode(s, r), r) == s for rules with drains; Save's
    fields are unchanged (app/tests/held/save_fields.json).
129. Cures: a card draining x is a cure for an at_least chip on x and not for an at_most one (invariant 120); the cut-in
    (109) reads it.
130. Not repeat-eligible: a tap of a card with drains on a declared lane keeps its limit and lands at each completion
    (invariant 78).
131. Split: invariant 1 holds across a drain reaching 0 and across the instant it starts being forgiven.
132. Loader fractions: a FlowEntry with every_hours k gives per_hour items_per_hour * RATE_SCALE / k, and a store
    ConditionEntry with every k gives value units(value) / k and release units(release) / k (both, never one); each
    is refused (ValueError naming the entry) when it does not divide exactly, never rounded; `every` other than 1 on
    after_time, on a non-store subject or on an order card's `when` is refused; a drain's ItemAmount is exact as an
    input's; a drain on a tally store is refused. With every_hours and every at 1, the rules are those of the loader
    before this part.

REJECTED, AND WHY (drains and fractions)
- A fixed take (any input amount A): the bound in FIT PASS; no A, C and θ give a cure tappable at every shown level,
  a clear in one tap and stacking.
- Quarters with a take of 1 (the store counting quarter hurts, the cure taking one quarter): tappable at every level,
  but clearing one hurt takes four taps and a full stack twelve, or the heal; one tap no longer clears.
- A content-only threshold band that drops stacking (the cap at one hurt, or the chip set to read one level only):
  meets the bound by giving up what the chip is for, a loss adding to the hurt.
- A finer chip scale alone (a smaller θ or a store in smaller units, the take still fixed): the bound holds at every
  scale, since all three wants scale together.
- A drain as a plain input, ready above 0: the tap would pause at 0 mid-completion and end interrupted (CARDS, "A tap
  ... never holds the hands while its card is not moving"), so a tap at a low level would not land, and the next tap
  would find the store empty and fail.
- A lump at the tap's start (take min(x, A) at once): a lump per tap is the two-bar charge REJECTED for lumps (CARDS),
  and a mixed card would take differently from a tapped one.
- Ready at the whole amount (x >= A' at a start, as an input's rest of the completion): the fixed take's bound again.
- The dry rule counting drains in the net it reads: a consumer that the inflow could pay would be paused to feed a take
  that is forgiven anyway, and the forgiven part would land on the wrong line.
- Forgiving every take at 0 (inputs too): "a completion is never finished unpaid" would go for every card, and a trip
  with no supplies would run on (BANKED TIME, "A dry trip sends nothing").
- `every` per field (one divisor for value, another for release): two scales for one band, and a band whose ends sit
  on different scales misreads; one divisor for both.
- Rounding an inexact fraction to the nearest unit: a threshold or a rate that differs from what content wrote, with
  nothing saying so (the loader refuses inexact ItemAmounts today for the same reason).
- Drains on standing actions now (a heal that takes up to a rate and never pauses): a standing action has no completion
  to bound "up to its amount", so it would be a second rule (a flow clamped at 0) beside this one. KNOWN GAPS.

KNOWN GAPS (drains and fractions)
- A standing consumer paused at 0 resumes at one hour of its cost (CHOICES, "Running dry"); a chip whose threshold is
  below that level, cleared by that consumer, can show with nothing healing it once its store stops rising between the
  two (a cure still clears it). A standing take forgiven at 0, which never pauses, would close it. Interim (the
  orchestrator's, for the first retune that uses drains): a chip's threshold sits at or above its standing healer's
  resume level, so that retune's chip reads at a quarter (`every = 4`), not a sixteenth, and the heal never stalls with
  the chip showing.
- An at_least order card taps a card that takes its store through an input or a cost (opening/checks.py); a card that
  only drains the store is refused as that rule's card. Interim: as that; a chip whose `when` has `every` has no order
  card anyway. Decided by: content's first order card meant to tap a draining cure.
- "Every loader reader of a store threshold" names makers.py, which reads only skill levels (granted_by); it reads no
  store threshold, so it is unchanged. The readers are convert.condition, refs.py's band check and its count of makers
  (_counted), and checks.py's order-card comparisons with a cap, all through convert.levels.

SPENT (a paused mix card's share yields to the rest of its mix until its Pause lifts, whether or not anything can
make its store again: docs/design/week-three-plan.md, item 1, and docs/decisions.md, "A mix keeps going without a dry
store"; blind tests from this part and api/CONTRACT.txt alone, item 2, against
app/tests/engine_contract/MUTANTS_spent.txt). Where this part and an entry above disagree, this part holds; it replaces
CHOICES "Shares" ("the engine never redistributes a share") and "Running dry" ("its share idles") for every mix card
that holds a Pause, and amends invariant 41. Examples name ids only; which stores are pools, and when a season refills
them, is content's.

FIT PASS
- An instance of Mixes (the flattened shares: what a share is a share of is unchanged) and of the dry rule (Stores
  and caps: a paused card stays paused by the same Pause, resumes by the same level). What is new is what a paused
  card's share does while its Pause holds, and one derived reading, "this store cannot be made again by anything in
  play", which names the card's state (SPENT) and the starved line but moves no share. No new Save field, verb, event
  kind or system: both are pure functions of the save at an instant, read wherever shares are read, as greying and
  the held entries are (Nystrom: a derived query over the Type Objects, never cached state).
- The need: a pool (a store only outcomes fill, such as a season's turn) empties mid-mix, and its card pauses. Idling
  its share until the store refills to one hour of its cost means, for a pool, until the season's next turn: the
  player must re-tune the mix for a season or lose that time, which the 2 AM test fails at every pool's end. A store a
  running maker refills pauses its card the same way and looks the same on the page, so it yields the same way (a
  card that behaves differently must look different; docs/decisions.md, "A mix keeps going without a dry store").
- The source rule stays: the yield is a number with a source, named by explain() on every line that receives it
  (Modifiers' sourced explain). What was rejected in "Shares" was a hidden redistribution; this one is sourced.
- How games do it: a worker whose job runs out of material goes to the next job on its list (RimWorld's priorities,
  Factorio's inserters taking the next recipe), rather than standing at the empty one, and comes back when there is
  material again.

CHOICES (spent)
- Makers: the makers of a store x are every unlocked standing action whose makes include x, and every card that is
  unlocked, neither completed nor retired (LEAVES: a card on a lane whose person left counts as retired), and has x
  among its makes, its outcome's stores or its finished's stores. Triggers and answers are not makers: an outcome
  that will land later (a season's next turn) is not something in play now. A maker is not asked to be running: a
  card the player could tap, or a rule could, can make x, so x is not spent.
- What a maker takes: its costs and its inputs. Drains are left out (a drain never stops its card, DRAINS).
- Spent stores: the largest set S of stores such that, for every x in S, every maker of x takes from some store y in
  S whose value is 0. Sets with that property are closed under union, so the largest is unique; it is found by starting
  from every store and removing, until nothing changes, each x that has a maker taking from no store of S at 0. So a
  store with no maker is spent; a store whose every maker needs a spent store that is empty is spent (resolved to the
  fixpoint, cycles included: two empty stores each made only from the other are both spent); a store with a maker that
  takes nothing, or takes only from stores above 0 or not spent, is not. Why the largest set: a dry cycle with nothing
  feeding it in never refills, and the smallest set would call it refillable and idle its shares for good.
- Spent card: a card of a running mix (the hands' or the town's) holding a Pause on a spent store. Its Run is SPENT
  (RunKind.SPENT "spent", 0/1, `store` the spent store), which takes PAUSED's place in the longest-lasting order:
  GREYED, then SPENT, then PAUSED, WAITING, YIELDED. A card holding a Pause on a store that is not spent (a
  store with any maker in play: "a store a running maker can refill") is PAUSED and resumes at one hour of its cost,
  as the dry rule says. SPENT and PAUSED differ in what lifts the Pause and in the starved line (Read model), never in
  the yield: both give their share (Giver).
- Giver: a card of a running mix that holds a Pause, its Run SPENT or PAUSED. Every giver yields by the one rule
  below, whatever made its store dry and whatever can refill it; no reading of the store (spent, its level, its
  makers) decides whether a share yields. A card that is greyed, waiting or yielded to a tap is no giver: it
  holds no Pause, and its share idles or runs as before.
- The yield, per lane: shares move only among the cards of one running mix on one lane (the hands for a hands mix;
  one person's lane for the town's mix, each person's shares being their own time), never across lanes and never into
  a mix that is not running. On lane L, with K the givers on L, R the other cards of that mix on L (whatever their
  Run: FULL, WAITING or YIELDED), s each card's saved share (flattened, repeats summed) and Rsum the sum of s
  over R: when Rsum > 0, each r in R gains floor(s_k * s_r / Rsum) from each k in K, and its effective share is s_r
  plus its gains; each giver's share runs at 0 (its Run). When Rsum is 0 (every card on L a giver, or the rest at
  share 0) nothing moves and every share idles. The remainder each floor leaves idles, as an unshared remainder does.
- Rounded down once per pair (giver, receiver): each gain is one whole number with one source, so a line's gains
  sum exactly to its effective share less its saved one, and explain() can list them. Rounding never favours the
  player: the lane's effective shares sum to at most its saved ones.
- Proportion over saved shares, not running ones: a receiver's gain does not move when it or a sibling waits
  or yields to a tap, so the effective shares change only when the set of givers changes, which is when a Pause
  is made or lifted. A receiver that is waiting holds its gain as it holds its own share, unused while
  waiting.
- Where it is read: everything that reads a mix card's share for a rate reads its effective share: the rates and
  segments, the held entries (ENTRIES HELD WHILE A CARD
  RUNS: h(c) reads the effective share), explain and explain_work, runs_out_at, and the dry rule's pauses. What the
  player wrote (the saved mix, CardView.share_bp, the split panel) is never changed.
- The instant, and the hour: the givers are the cards holding a Pause, so the yield starts at the instant a Pause is
  made and ends at the instant it is lifted, and at no other: the dry rule's own instants. A Pause on a store a maker
  refills lifts at its RESUME, when the store reaches the resume level (one hour of the card's cost at its saved share,
  or the cap), so the share stays with the receivers while the store refills below that level, the store's rising off
  0 included; the hour of hysteresis that keeps dry from chattering keeps the split still the same way, and the yield
  adds no instant of its own. A change of the running mix, or SaveMix of it, clears its pauses (Running dry), and the
  shares come back with them (re-yielding at once for a card that pauses again). S and the spent cards are a function
  of the save at one instant (store values, unlocks, completions, pauses, the running mixes) and change only at
  instants that already rebuild every rate: a store reaching 0 (its STORE_LIMIT), a Pause made or lifted, an outcome
  (stores, unlocks, completions, retirements, leaves), a running mix changing. So no new event kind, and between events
  the effective shares are constant. Within one rebuild the dry rule's pauses and the yields settle together: the rule
  pauses with the yields of the pauses in force (a receiver drinking its gain can dry a store and pause in turn), then
  reads the givers again, until neither changes; a rebuild only adds pauses, so it ends (at most one pass per mix
  card, plus one).
- The share comes back at the first instant its card holds no Pause. An outcome that raises a spent card's store to or
  past the Pause's resume level lifts the Pause at that instant (RESUME at that instant), and a maker refilling a
  paused card's store to that level lifts it at its RESUME: either way the card runs at its saved share from that
  instant, the receivers back to theirs (less any other giver's gains). An outcome that brings a maker into play
  (unlocks one, raises a cap that makes one live again) takes the store out of S at that instant: the card is PAUSED
  from then, still holding its Pause, so its share stays with the receivers until it resumes. A refill below the
  resume level leaves the card paused, so the share stays with the receivers until something lifts it.
- Explain: Explanation gains `gained: tuple[SpentShare, ...] = ()`, SpentShare(card: CardId, share_bp: int) exported
  from the engine package: one entry per giver that gives this line's card a non-zero gain, in the flattened mix order
  of the givers. Explanation.share_bp is the effective share (saved plus every gain), so invariant 10's formula on its
  own fields is unchanged. A giver's own lines explain with its saved share_bp and its Run, SPENT or PAUSED (per_hour
  0), and list no gains.
- Read model (view.py): CardView.run is SPENT for a spent card and PAUSED for another giver; CardView.share_bp stays
  its saved share; CardView gains `spent_store: StoreId | None` (the spent card's store, None unless its run is SPENT)
  and `gained: tuple[SpentShare, ...]` (as explain's, () when none). The wire carries both (api/CONTRACT.txt,
  "SPENT"). A running mix's actual split is each card's share_bp plus its gained, a giver's 0; it differs from the set
  split exactly while the mix holds a giver. Starved (amends invariant 41): a runner paused on a store makes
  it starved only when it is not a spent card; a spent store has no cure in reach, and a red line all season would
  say there is one (docs/decisions.md, "Every bad state has a cure in reach"). Covered is unchanged.
- Codec: nothing. Run is not in a save; Save's fields are unchanged (app/tests/held/save_fields.json); Pause is as it
  was. FORMAT, the manifest, the rules encoding and rules_digest are unchanged. Rebase: nothing new.
- Release lock: nothing new is locked (no rule shape changes). Shipping is a behaviour change, as "Shipping the
  evaluation change" was: the stored save is the save, every live save decodes exactly as before, and a saved mix
  holding a paused card runs differently from the deploy on, forward only, from its last write. That is the decision's
  own point (docs/decisions.md: a re-tune is only ever set off by something the player chose): the player chose the
  split; a dry store did not. Replays from a snapshot across the deploy diverge for such saves (saves/support.py's
  `replay` reports no match) until their pre-release snapshots age out. The commit carries its exposure: the
  playthroughs and the simulator re-run, every held counter that moves named and brought to the lead, never raised.

INVARIANTS (spent; numbered on from 132. Invariant 41 now reads: "a line is starved iff its store is at 0 and a
runner that is not a spent card is paused on it, or a candidate tap is blocked on it". Invariants 16 and 19 hold
unchanged.)
133. Spent stores: for any s and r, the spent set is the largest S with the property in CHOICES "Spent stores". In
    particular a store no unlocked, live card or unlocked standing action makes is spent; a store whose every maker
    takes from a spent store at 0 is spent; a store with a maker taking nothing, or taking only from stores above 0 or
    not spent, is not; a maker that is locked, completed or retired, or a trigger or answer that adds to it, does not
    keep a store out of S.
134. The yield: for a running mix and each lane L, with K, R, s and Rsum as in CHOICES "The yield": when Rsum > 0
    each r in R explains with share_bp s_r + sum over k in K of floor(s_k * s_r / Rsum) and `gained` listing exactly
    the non-zero terms, sourced by k; each k explains with its Run (SPENT or PAUSED) and per_hour 0. The effective
    shares on L sum to at most the saved ones, and to within |K| * |R| units of them. When Rsum is 0 nothing moves. No
    card of another lane, of another mix, or of a mix that is not running gains anything.
135. Only from a Pause: a card that holds no Pause gives no card a gain, whatever its Run; on a save whose running
    mixes hold no paused card every rate, segment and view equals the engine's before this part.
136. At that instant and nowhere else: a card that pauses at T under a running mix (its store emptying, a pool or a
    store a maker refills alike) leaves every rate before T as it was; from T it runs 0 and every receiver runs at
    its effective share (134); between T and the next instant a Pause is made or lifted, the receivers' shares stay
    constant. Invariant 1 holds across T.
137. Explain names the source: every Explanation of a receiver's lines has share_bp equal to its saved share plus the
    sum of `gained`, each entry's card a giver on the same lane of the same running mix; invariant 10 holds on its own
    fields.
138. Back at the resume: an outcome at T that raises a giver's store to or past its Pause's resume level, or a maker
    that refills it to that level by T, gives, from T, that card Run FULL (or as its other state says) at its saved
    share and every receiver its saved share plus the gains of the givers left; an outcome at T that brings a maker
    of a spent card's store into play gives, from T, the card Run PAUSED, and the receivers keep its gains.
139. Dry pauses, and yields until the hour: a card paused on a store that a running maker refills is PAUSED, not
    SPENT, gives its share from the instant it pauses, and holds it given while the store refills below the resume
    level (at 0 or above it); it resumes, and takes its share back, at the instant the store reaches one hour of its
    cost.
140. Never stored: Save's fields are unchanged; encode(s, r) is the same text whether or not a card in s is a giver,
    and saves that are equal give equal yields at every t; a save whose mix holds a giver at T and the same save
    re-decoded give equal stores, work and views at every later t.
141. The 2 AM property: with no decision, a running hands mix whose saved shares sum to BP, holding one giver and
    receivers R with Rsum > 0, every receiver at Run FULL, has effective shares summing to more than BP - |R|, where
    before this part they summed to BP minus the giver's share.

REJECTED, AND WHY (spent)
- Idling a paused card's share: for a pool the 2 AM test fails at every pool's end, and the player must re-tune the
  mix for a whole season or lose the time; for a store a maker refills, the card would look like every other paused
  card and be treated differently (docs/decisions.md, "A mix keeps going without a dry store").
- Pointing at a spent card so the player re-tunes, its share idle: the same 2 AM failure, with a note added.
- A yield read off the store rather than the Pause (the share back once the store rises off 0, or a yield that waits
  for the store to stay at 0): the split would move between the pause and the resume, at instants the dry rule does
  not have, where the hour of hysteresis holds it still.
- Greying the mix when a store is spent: a season's end would stop every saved mix holding a pool's card, and nothing
  runs until a visit.
- Retiring the spent card: the next season brings it back, so it would need un-retiring (unlocks only grow), and a
  retired card greys every mix holding it.
- A hidden redistribution (shares moved with nothing saying so): a number with no source (CHOICES, "Shares"); each
  gain is named in explain() and on the card, and the page shows the split that runs beside the one set.
- Giving only to receivers that take nothing from the giver's store (each giver over its own Rsum): a second rule
  beside the one yield, for a case no shipped content has (two mix cards on one lane drawing one dry store); there the
  second pauses too and the inflow waits for the hourly resume, so throughput over time is unchanged.
- Yielding across lanes or across mixes: a person's shares are their own time (THE TOWN'S MIX); the player's time is
  never handed to the town nor back.
- Proportion over running shares (a waiting or held receiver getting nothing): every sibling's requires turning or
  hold settling would move every other card's share, so the yield would chatter at instants of its own.
- One rounding per receiver (floor of the summed gain): its gain could not be split among several givers in whole
  numbers that sum, so explain could not name each source exactly.
- Storing the yield or the spent set in the save: a Save field for what the save at an instant already says, and it
  could disagree with the stores after a rebase.
- Counting a trigger or answer that will add to the store as a maker: every season pool has one, so nothing would
  ever be spent.

KNOWN GAPS (spent)
- A maker whose input store holds less than one completion's input but more than 0 keeps its store out of S, so a
  card paused on that store reads PAUSED, not SPENT, and starves its line; its share yields either way. Interim: as
  that. Decided by: the first content where a pool's last part is smaller than one input.
- A standing consumer paused on a spent store still makes its line starved (invariant 41 amends only spent cards).
  Decided by: the first season pool a standing action costs whose starved line is seen after the season's end.

QUESTIONS (spent; asked while the interface was built against, each answered here)
- Invariant 19, a spent card during a hands tap: SPENT. It holds a Pause, so it is not among the mix actions "not
  paused" that 19 makes YIELDED, and SPENT comes before YIELDED in the longest-lasting order.
- Invariant 19, a receiver's share_bp during a tap: its effective share. The yield reads saved shares whatever the
  receivers' Runs, so a tap moves nothing: "its share unchanged" is unchanged by the tap, the effective share before
  and after.
- The resume level of a Pause made while a card receives a gain: one hour of its cost at its saved share, not its
  effective one. A Pause is saved, and a level read off the yield would write the yield into the save (invariant 140);
  the resume level is then the dry rule's own, whether or not the store is spent. The dry rule still reads effective
  shares to choose what to pause.
- View.completed and retired makers: unchanged by this part. It lists the unlocked cards that are completed, and a
  retired card counts as completed (counts.is_completed), so a retired maker is listed there as before; it is no maker.
- Answers as makers: an answer (an AnswerDef's outcome) is never a maker, whatever stores its outcome adds, nor is a
  trigger. A card an answer queues, installs or starts is a maker by the card rule alone (unlocked, neither completed
  nor retired, the store among its makes), as any card is.
- A card's makes and takes include its fight's lines: its wounds and tallies are makes (cardsides.card_makes), its
  loses are takes. Its finished's stores count as makes.
- Existing contract tests that asserted PAUSED, or a starved line, for a card paused on a store with no maker in play
  (test_dry, test_explain, test_banked_trip_contract) read SPENT and not starved now, per invariants 133 and 41 as
  amended; a test whose share check read the saved share reads saved plus `gained`.

PRESTIGE PHASES (a skill at its top waits for the player's click: docs/design/prestige-phases.md;
nodes.py, nodechecks.py, phases.py, phaseevents.py, prestige.py, prestigeverbs.py, pickentries.py, Forecast.tops_out). Where this

FIT PASS
- The design note's table: the phase's top extends the XP cap the way THE CAMP's held levels extend a store's cap; a

CHOICES (prestige phases)
- State: one PhaseState per skill on its SkillState (SkillState.phase), not a Save field ("A new engine part adds its
  own state type"). Its default, FIRST_PHASE, is left out of the codec's text, so a save that never topped out encodes
  as before, FORMAT is unchanged, and every engine write of a SkillState carries the phase on.
- Rules: Rules.prestige (PrestigeDef: top_level, carry_window_s, menu_size) and Rules.nodes (NodeDef, a Type Object:
  skill, target, ranks, per_rank_bp). The digest leaves both out while unused, so rules without prestige keep theirs.
- What a node may change: a make, a card's work or a store's cap, of work outside its skill (nodechecks.check_nodes;
  the loader's schema has no way to write another, opening/prestige.py). XP, costs, lane speed and strengths are
  refused, since anything aimed at XP or at a lane's speed would make levels come sooner and the loop spiral.
- A rank's entry: one MUL_BP entry per node, BP + n * per_rank_bp for n ranks, sourced NODE_PREFIX + the node's id,
  joining the base stack after the held entries, and the entries a store's cap reads (gates.store_cap). Ranks add within a node; separate nodes compound, so a full track
  is bounded by the product over its nodes, fixed by the content.
- XP: with prestige set, a skill in phase n has limit xp_ceiling (one unit below the top of phase n + 1, so a click
  always lands below the new top and one absence leaves one click waiting) and accrues until carry_ends_at
  (topped_at + carry_window_s); its shown level reads the curve from level 1 in each phase
  (level_shown), and SkillLevel conditions read levels counted across phases (level_counted), which never fall at
  a click. LEVEL_UP falls at the phase's own thresholds and none past its top (phases.next_level_xp).
- Events: TOP_OUT (XP reaches the phase's top, or holds it with topped_at None: topped_at becomes the instant) and
  CARRY_END (the carry's end while XP still rises: rebuilt, XP rate 0), both solved off the XP segment, sequence the
  skill's index, ordered right after LEVEL_UP (phaseevents.py). Neither makes orders evaluate: no condition reads
  topped_at.
- Rebase keeps each skill's phase, its XP held at or under the phase's ceiling; picks are renamed as IdKind.NODE.
  The release lock holds node ids as any content id (kind "node", so renames.toml can name a node's rename), and no
  rules modifier, nor a new save's starting one, may be sourced NODE_PREFIX: only a pick's entry is.
- A finished track: the skill is mastered. Prestige is refused TRACK_DONE for good, and the skill rests at its top,
  its XP stopping at the carry's end as at any top.
- The loader's bar (opening/prestige.check_phase_spans): carry_window_s <= nodes.ORDINARY_ABSENCE_S, and per skill
  span >= the fastest base xp_per_hour of a card or standing action training it times ORDINARY_ABSENCE_S. So the
  window binds before the ceiling, which is only the guarantee.
- The menu: the first menu_size unfinished nodes of the track in rules order. A function of the picks alone, so fixed
  for the phase with no draw, and an undo shows the menu the undone pick came from.
- Undo: UndoPick removes the last pick and leaves a pick owed in the same phase; the next Prestige of the skill fills
  it. Open while the shown level is still the one the click landed at (PhaseState.click_level).
- Move: MovePick(skill, node, to) takes the rank `node` holds and parks it on `to`, a node on the menu now. The cost is
  that rank's progress: it is out of play (PhaseState.parked, held by neither node) for the rest of the phase and the
  whole of the next, and takes effect at the click that lifts the skill into the phase after next. Its due phase is
  stored, so the cost is the same whenever in a phase the move is made. A parked rank holds its node's rank for the
  menu, so the menu moves on; pick_entries reads picks only, so the stack never counts a parked rank. A click settles
  the ranks due, just before its own pick, so UndoPick still takes back the click's. Refused PICK_OWED while an undone
  pick is owed, NOT_PICKED, NOT_ON_MENU (a node to itself too, as it is full).
- By hand only: Prestige, UndoPick and MovePick are in Verb, not OrderVerb, and SetOrders or SetQueue naming one is
  refused (PrestigeRefused, reason BY_HAND_ONLY: prestigeverbs.refuse_by_hand). Refusals are PrestigeRefused, an
  InvalidVerbError carrying a PrestigeRefusal. The decision log's kinds are "prestige", "undo_pick" and "move_pick".
- Forecast: level_ups are the LEVEL_UPs (phases.next_level_xp, so they end at the phase's top), and tops_out is each
  skill's XP reaching its phase's top, solved off its segment as a level-up is: the instant TOP_OUT sets topped_at.
  A skill at its top, or mastered (prestige.mastered), has none.
- Read model: a SkillBar reads the shown level, the bar's ends within the phase (next_level_xp None at the top) and a
  SkillPhase (phaseview.py): phase, level_counted, at_top, waiting_levels, the menu with each node's ranks taken and
  its target's value before and after one more rank at a full share, undo_open, mastered, the nodes the picks hold and the
  ranks parked; None with rules.prestige
  None, and left out of the repr. api.level reads level_shown.
