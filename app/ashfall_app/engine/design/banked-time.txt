BANKED TIME: WHERE PRODUCTION PAST A CAP GOES (docs/design/banked-time-plan.md). Where this part and an entry above
disagree, this part holds. Examples name ids only; which trips, comforts and arrivals, and their numbers, are content's.

FIT PASS
- First question: does anything already take overflow at a cap? No. "Cap" (above) drops it on purpose and names the
  answer, "hitting a cap is an order's job"; what was missing is something worth an order's switch once a chapter's
  builds are done. Measured at 5f2b4c1 (docs/design/banked-time-plan.md, "Measured"): over day two as played, every
  gathered store loses 82-100% of what it makes to its cap; over an 8, 24 or 72 h absence from day two's end with
  nobody deciding, 96-100%. XP still trains, so levels are not lost; materials are.
- It is not an attention bug: the 2 AM test is level on day two, because a babysitter has nothing to spend on either.
  Loss no decision could avoid is a content ceiling, not an offline cap; loss a visit could have avoided would be one,
  and the 2 AM test already fails that. So this part is about value ("a return after 8 h looks like one after 2 h"),
  and its check is a number the content is sized to, not an engine guarantee.
- What a sink is an instance of: work that takes stores. That is a card with inputs (CARDS), run in the mix or
  queued like any card, so every spend already has an order. Three shapes cover the item's directions, each on
  existing rows: a trip (an expedition's first slice) is an uncapped card with inputs and a long work_s; a comfort
  (the camp made less miserable) is a capped craft with a big cost; an arrival ("surplus that lets people stay") is
  an offer whose answer queues a make-room one-off (TOWNSFOLK, INSTALLED RULES AND OFFERS, "Queued one-off").
  Nystrom, Type Object: a trip, a comfort and an arrival are kinds as data on CardDef and OfferDef, not classes, and
  the chapter's cost (behaviour is harder than data) does not bite, because none needs behaviour a card lacks.
- The combat seam (FIGHTS, KNOWN GAPS, "Who you bring" and "Stance"): a trip that fights carries a FightDef on its
  CardDef, the Component FIGHTS already defined (Nystrom, Component: the trip's runner never reads it). Its side in
  this slice is the hands' SideDef, so "a side made of who goes" is the player alone until backup is decided.
- How games do it: Melvor's and Idle Fantasy's dungeon runs and Idle Awakening's expeditions take supplies per run
  and return loot at a rate set by stats; Anno's and RimWorld's colonists turn surplus food into newcomers. None of
  them banks idle time as a currency the player spends on speed, which is the one the constraints rule out.
- What is new: nothing in the engine. No type, field, verb, event kind, Save field or system. The work is the
  simulator's measure and checks, content, and the page's telling of a trip.

CHOICES (banked time)
- Trip: an uncapped CardDef with inputs (the supplies, per completion) and makes, work_s long enough that a
  completion is a trip (an hour or more at full speed; content's number). Run in the mix it draws its inputs as cost
  lines at its share (CARDS, "Inputs against a dry store"): production goes on trips instead of past the cap. A
  reserve of the supply for another consumer is a rule on the store (Orders); a trip that drains its supply dry hands
  its share to the rest of its mix (SPENT, "Giver"). Tapped, it takes the hands for one trip, as any tap. When supplies run dry the dry rule
  pauses it last-in-mix first and resumes it at one hour's cost, so an empty camp never sends a trip.
- Uncapped, not capped. A capped card completing in a mix greys the whole mix (CARDS, "Greyed mix"), so a capped trip
  finishing at 03:00 would stop the hands until a visit: "Nothing finishes into idleness" broken by the sink meant to
  fix idleness. Uncapped, a trip's single completion is a rate (Rate form), costs no instant, and repeats as long as
  the mix runs it.
- Finds: a trip's discrete returns (a map piece, a clue, a find, a line) are ordinary TriggerDefs on
  AtLeast(CardCount(trip), n), one per n content gives, each with its outcome. CardCount is a solved subject
  (Conditions; invariant 37), so the nth find lands at the exact second the nth trip's work completes, whatever the
  share or the slicing (invariant 1). After the last find the trip still pays its makes (content: plan, BT2).
- A fighting trip: FightDef(side = the hands' SideId, threat, lost, ...) on the trip's CardDef, as FIGHTS defines.
  Its inputs and costs are untouched by the odds (a lost trip spent its supplies: decisions.md, "Combat has
  fangs"); its makes are scaled by the share won. Finds read CardCount, so they count trips made, won or lost:
  a find is story, and a beat's outcome never carries odds (FIGHTS, "What a fight is not").
- Comfort: a capped CardDef (cap 1) with costs or inputs of several caps' worth, a scene stamp and a pay (a
  modifier) or a need an arrival reads, built by the queue's "When ready" entry while the player is away. It must be
  unlocked, in view and unready, before its inputs can be met: a sink first shown while the player is away cannot be
  queued, and a babysitter could start it first (the day-two lesson behind A16's builds). The 2 AM test is the gate.
- Arrival: a trigger on a food store's level ("food to spare") unlocks an offer; its yes queues a make-room one-off
  (Outcome.queues: a capped HANDS card whose inputs are the pile) and installs nothing; a trigger on
  AtLeast(CardCount(make_room), 1) unlocks the newcomer's lane and installs their default rule (Outcome.orders). An
  order that answers offers of that kind (day-two plan, item 4) keeps arrivals coming while the player is away.
- No spend buys raw speed: no sink's outcome or make aims at a WorkSpeed, LaneSpeed or a rate of a card already
  running for its own sake. A comfort's pay may be a modifier like any build's (a build is how a camp gets better),
  but nothing converts time away into a shorter bar. Held by absence: no verb or Outcome part shortens a running
  bar, so there is nothing for a check to refuse.
- No decay or upkeep: no sink takes anything over time while nothing runs it. A trip takes only while the mix or a
  tap runs it; a comfort and an arrival take once. A standing action that costs stores (the visitor's idiom) is never
  a sink; the only standing drains are story ones on a clock store (the visitor's idiom), reviewed as content.

INVARIANTS (banked time; numbered on from 86. No engine invariant is new; these are existing ones on the new shapes,
checked as engine-contract tests on fixture rules.)
87. (Retired with Hold: docs/decisions.md, "No hold on a line".)
88. Finds land on time: the trigger on AtLeast(CardCount(trip), n) fires at the instant CardCount reaches n, for any
    slicing into advances, views and snapshots (invariants 1 and 37), and at the same instant whether the trip ran
    mixed or as repeated taps of equal total work.
89. A dry trip sends nothing: while its input store is below one completion's inputs and the trip is paused, its makes
    pay nothing and CardCount does not rise.

REJECTED, AND WHY (banked time)
- An offline cap, a bank of idle hours, or decay while away: decisions.md, "No offline cap, full rate"; each sets a
  check-in schedule.
- Banked time spent on speed (a skip, a rush, a timer cut): the constraint "a spend buys no raw speed", and the
  shop-shaped element decisions.md forbids.
- Overflow spilling into another store (excess roots into a surplus pool): the engine would redistribute with no
  decision ("Shares": a hidden redistribution is a number with no source), caps would stop meaning anything, and the
  pool is an infinite point dump (decisions.md, "Finite, and the walls are the world").
- A separate expedition runner with a roster, a destination and its own state (the Expeditions row as first planned):
  a second copy of tap, mix, queue, rules and lanes, and a Save field the held list refuses, for what an uncapped card
  with inputs and a few triggers already express. Revisited when a second member goes (KNOWN GAPS).
- A trip as a hands tap re-sent by a hands tap rule: the page draws only mix rules on the hands (TOWNSFOLK, Rejected,
  "A tap rule on the hands"), so a whenever player could not set it, and a trip that needed a visit per trip is the
  babysitting the item exists to remove. Tapping a trip stays possible; repeating one is the mix's job.
- A capped trip (a destination done after n trips): it greys the mix at 03:00 (CHOICES, "Uncapped, not capped").
  A destination that ends is its finds ending; capped region-clearing fights stay FIGHTS' KNOWN GAP.
- "Lost to caps" served to the page: decisions.md, "Shown in the design, not claimed".
  It is the simulator's number only.
- Loss to caps as an engine invariant ("nothing is ever lost"): it needs an infinite sink, which decisions.md rules
  out. The check is a content target per chapter (plan item 1), not a guarantee.

KNOWN GAPS (banked time)
- Who goes (backup): the proposed shape is a support card on each member's ruled lane, run by their rule while
  Running(trip) holds, and an entry on the trip's SideStrength sourced by that support card while it runs (as the
  odds entries are while a fight runs). The entry is now a while_running entry on the support card (ENTRIES HELD
  WHILE A CARD RUNS, the last part); what remains is content's rule and the trip's side. Decided by: the first
  expedition with backup (R2's, by the spine).
- Stance (retreat or push through): stays FIGHTS' gap; its decider moves from "the first expedition" to the first
  expedition with backup, since a solo trip in the mix already stops when its supplies do.
- Wide expeditions (the town sends people): a share of the town's mix on a trip, once the town has a mix (day-two
  plan, Open 3). Decided by: the town's mix.
- A trip's away pose in the scene while it runs mixed (the figure gone for the trip's share of a cycle) is the page's;
  nothing on the wire marks a trip. Decided by: plan item 6's render.
