FULL OUTPUT (a job never spends its inputs on an outcome its stores cannot hold: issue #438). Where this part and an
entry above disagree, this part holds; it amends CHOICES "Cap" for the cards it names and leaves it as it was for
every other runner.

FIT PASS
- The mirror of the dry rule (Running dry; Inputs against a dry store): a consumer pauses when its input store runs
  dry, and a fillable card pauses when its output stores are full. Same Pause, same last-first order, same yield of a
  paused mix card's share to the rest of its mix (SPENT, "Giver"). No new Save
  field, verb, event kind or system: a full pause is a Pause on a store the card makes, a dry pause one on a store it
  takes, and a store is on one side of a card only (validation), so which kind a Pause is reads off the rules.
- The need: a card that draws a pool (a store nothing refills) into a store with a cap below what the pool holds threw
  the overflow away at the cap, and the pool with it, so the player could lose for good what a later project needs.
- How games do it: a machine whose output is blocked stops and keeps its inputs (Factorio's assemblers and inserters,
  a full chest), and starts again when there is room.

CHOICES (full output)
- Fillable card: a card with at least one input, at least one outcome store, and no cap. A capped card never pauses
  full: each completion counts toward its cap and lands its discrete effects, so it is never lost entirely. A card with
  no inputs gathers and keeps CHOICES "Cap": the overflow is lost and the waste is a visible fact for an order. Standing
  actions are no fillable runners (they take costs, not inputs).
- Pools only: the rule reads a fillable card only while one of its input stores is a pool, a store no maker in play
  can make (SPENT, "Makers", read now: spent.unmade). A card turning a store something in play refills keeps CHOICES
  "Cap": what it wastes comes back. Why: the loss that cannot be undone is of a pool; and every full pause shows a
  falling line while its store drains to the resume level, which on a store a maker keeps full would be a red line
  for nothing. Whether a store is a pool can change as makers come into play; a full pause made while it was one lifts
  by its resume rule as any.
- Outcome stores: the card's Outcome.stores, the per-completion amounts. Its flow makes (the camp bar every hands card
  fills by its work) and its fight's tallies and wounds are no outcome here: by-products of the work, as XP is. A card
  whose only full stores are those runs on.
- Full: a fillable card, not tapped, holding no Pause, whose work is moving (its work segment's rate above 0), whose
  every outcome store stands at its cap (gates.store_cap) with an unclamped exact net above 0. Nothing fits, so
  everything its inputs would pay for is lost. One outcome store full while another has room: the card runs, and the
  full store's overflow is lost as CHOICES "Cap" says. Why: pausing when any output is full would stall a job forever
  on a by-product nothing uses (a hunt's second yield); the loader's finite-supply check (opening/finite_checks.py)
  refuses content where such a loss could cost progress.
- The full rule: at an instant where a card is full, pause it, last in running order first (rates.Running, as the dry
  rule reads it), re-reading after each pause, since pausing one card lowers its stores' nets and can leave an earlier
  card no longer overflowing; until no card is full. The Pause: (card, its first outcome store in declaration order,
  that store's resume level at the pause).
- When it runs: in the STORE_LIMIT handler, after the dry pass. A store reaching its cap is a STORE_LIMIT already; a
  STORE_LIMIT also falls at save.time, subject the full card's first outcome store, while any card is full (a mix
  change, a tap ending, a resume, a consumer pausing or a cap reached on another of its stores can make one full at a
  store already standing at its cap).
- Resume: a full pause lifts at the first instant any of its card's outcome stores has room for one whole completion
  of it: at or below its cap now (gates.store_cap) less the card's lump on it (lumps.lump_amount), both read at that
  instant, so a cap raised meanwhile is read as raised. One completion of room, not the dry rule's hour nor a share of
  the cap: the store stays near full, so a rule of the player's on that store (an at_most) does not come on for a band
  the engine made, and no rate is written into the save; a pause and its resume are one completion of a consumer's
  draw apart, so they cannot chatter within an instant. Pause.resume_at holds the first store's level at the pause, for
  a reader; the resume reads the levels now.
- Run: a full-paused card reads PAUSED with `store` its Pause's store, and gives its share as any giver. It is never
  SPENT: spent.spend reads only pauses on a store the card takes. Its line is never starved (a full store is not 0).
- View: CardView.full_store is the Pause's store while the card's run is PAUSED on a store it makes, None otherwise;
  the card face words it (api/contract/full-output.txt, "FULL OUTPUT"), since a full pause is no blocker and draws no need row.
- Read model: every outcome store of a full-paused card reads covered (coverage.covered), since the card resumes as
  the store falls: the band down to the resume level is a store being drawn as planned, not one that needs the
  player, so its falling line is white.
- Taps: a tap is never full, and a full pause is no blocker: a tap, whoever starts it (the player, an order, the
  queue), runs its one completion at a full store and its overflow is lost, as CHOICES "Cap" says; the card's Run
  while tapped ignores a full pause (rates._tap_run reads dry pauses only). A tap is a choice of one completion with
  the store in view; the full rule stops what runs on its own, the mix. When the tap lands, the card is back in its mix
  with its Pause as it was. Why: the card face draws each need of a tappable card, and "no room" is no need a face
  can draw, so a full card that refused taps would look ready and do nothing; and the loader's check holds converters
  to a cap that keeps what progress needs, so a tap can lose only what progress does not.
- Queue entries and order rules: nothing new, since a tap is never held by a full pause; a queue stint whose mix card
  pauses full hands the hands back (Dry stint reads any Pause). A change of the running
  mix clears the pauses on every store its cards make or cost (Running dry), full ones included, and the full rule
  pauses again at once where a card is still full.
- Rebase: step 5 drops every Pause on a store its card makes under the new rules, and the resolved instant pauses again
  at once where a card is still full. The kind of a Pause reads off the rules, so one written under other rules is
  re-decided rather than read.

INVARIANTS (full output; numbered on from 178)
179. Nothing spent on nothing: in a resolved save, no running fillable card holding no Pause has every outcome store at
     its cap with an exact net above 0.
180. Gathering kept: a card with no inputs, a capped card, a standing action and a card drawing no pool are never
     paused full; rules with no fillable card drawing a pool advance exactly as before this part.
181. A by-product never stalls: a fillable card with an outcome store below its cap is never paused full.
182. Room resumes: a full pause lifts at the first instant one of its card's outcome stores has room for one whole
     completion of that card's lump on it.
183. Inputs kept: while a card holds a full pause its input stores take nothing from it.

REJECTED, AND WHY (full output)
- Pausing when any outcome store is full: a job would stand forever on a by-product with no sink (the opposite soft
  lock), its other yields lost with it.
- Clamping the inputs to what the outputs can hold (paying part of a completion): a completion's inputs are paid in
  proportion to its work (Rate form); a second rate on the inputs would split one bar into two.
- Every fillable card, pool or not: a renewable store's maker would show a falling line through each pause's band
  and the economy everywhere would shift for losses that come back.
- Every runner that takes anything, standing actions and flow costs included: chores and the body's needs would stop
  at a full store and stall XP, which CHOICES "Cap" keeps for gathering; the pools issue #438 names are all drawn by
  card inputs.
- Counting flow makes as outcomes: every hands card fills the camp bar, whose cap is far above anything, so no hands
  card would ever pause.
- An hour of make as the resume level, as the dry rule's hour of cost: a card with several outcome stores would need a
  level per store written into the save, and an hour of a fast card's make is above many caps, so it would wait for
  the store to empty.
- Half the cap as the resume level: the store drained through half its range before the card ran again, which brought
  a player's rule on that store (an at_most, "when low, fetch more") on at every pause, a cycle the engine made.
- A new RunKind for a full pause: the page and the yield treat it as every paused card; a new kind is wire, client and
  contract for one word. The store a Pause names says which.
- Pausing taps too (a full pause as a blocker, AtMost on its store): the card face words each need of a tappable card
  and has no words for "no room", so the card would show every need met and not move (tests/dayone/sinkchecks.py's
  invariant); whether a card overflows also reads the rates (a consumer may keep up), and a tap's preconditions read
  values only.

KNOWN GAPS (full output)
- A store reaching its cap mid-second: the event falls at the end of that second (Event times), so the card's last
  partial second is paid by its inputs while its overflow, under one second of its make, is lost; once per pause.
  Closing it needs the card's work limited where its stores fill, as Inputs against a dry store's mirror gap does.
  Interim: the loader's finite-supply check asks a converter's target to hold all progress needs of it, so the loss
  is only ever of what progress does not need.
- Modifiers that raise an outcome amount are read through the exact net, as everything is; the loader's check reads
  base amounts (opening/finite_checks.py).
