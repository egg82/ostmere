CURE CUT-IN (a cure for a chip shown now, tapped by the player while the hands run a task). Where this part and an
entry above disagree, this part holds.

FIT PASS
- An instance of CUT-IN (a player's verb; interrupt, work kept) with a second kind of card that may cut in, and of the
  Queued one-off for taking a task up again. No new state, verb or event. The reading of a cure is the cures gate's,
  moved into the engine so both read one thing (cure_reading.cures_chip).
- A cure moves the store its chip watches toward clearing the chip, by the chip's own condition: a chip that shows
  while the store is low (at_most) is cured by a card that puts the store up (cure_reading.puts_up), one that shows
  while it is high (at_least) by a card that takes from it (cure_reading.takes: its inputs, its cost lines, and the
  cost lines of the standing actions it unlocks). No card is named; a card that moves the store the wrong way makes
  the chip worse and is not a cure.
- The defect: a hands task runs to its end at the speed it started with, so a chip that shows during a long stage
  (half an hour, or three hours) had no cure in reach until it ended (docs/decisions.md, "Every bad state has a cure
  in reach"), while some chips cost something every second they show.

CHOICES
- decisions.cure_cuts_in(save, rules, card): card is on HANDS, a cure for a chip shown now (cure_reading.cures_shown),
  the hands run another card that is not itself a cure for a shown chip, and card would start were the hands free.
  decisions.cuts_in, and so CardView.cuts_in, is the glowing step's rule or this one. The player's tap of such a card
  interrupts the task (work.interrupt) and taps the card (decisions.cut_in).
- The interrupted task is taken up again: a task a true order or a queue entry taps is left to it; any other (a hand
  tap) goes to the queue's front as the page's When ready entry for its current completion (installs.queued), so
  after the cure lands the queue starts it from its kept work, behind any tap order that is true then. A true order's
  mix does not hold it back: as a tap it held the hands over that mix, so the queue's entry for a HANDS card stopped
  mid-completion acts while an order's mix runs (decisions._resumes; a dry store's requeued tap alike).
- An order's cure does not cut in; it waits for the task to land, as before. Every drawn landing then still comes true
  with nobody there (docs/decisions.md, "Leaving and coming back always pays").

INVARIANTS (cure cut-in; 109, then 120 after the file's highest)
109. With a chip shown and a cure for it ready, a player's TapCard of the cure on a busy HANDS lane leaves the
    interrupted card's work as it stood and runs the cure; the interrupted card is then tapped by an order or a queue
    entry, and is at the queue's front if none tapped it before. An order's cure never cuts in. A cure never cuts into
    a cure, and with no chip shown the busy-lane tap raises as invariant 20.
120. A card is a cure for a chip exactly when it puts up the chip's store and the chip is at_most, or takes from it and
    the chip is at_least; the cut-in (109) and the cures gate read this one answer.

REJECTED, AND WHY (cure cut-in)
- Every hands build stage under the cure window: either many more stages (more taps, and the released completion-count
  triggers on builds would move) or less work per build (every band moves).
- Builds off the hands: building would stop costing the player's time, which the economy prices in.
- An order's cure cutting in too: played on the routes, a rule's cut-in broke the landing a page had drawn for the
  task it paused (the plumbing checks), and moved day two's exact cap loss and two visits' decisions. KNOWN GAP: a
  present player can cure mid-stage where a rule waits for the stage's end, a small edge for attention (the 2 AM
  test's babysitter does not tap cures, so it does not measure it). Closing it needs drawn landings read off the
  forecast, which would see a rule's cut-in.
