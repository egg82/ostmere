NEXT'S ROW (the queue as a row among the hands' rules: issue #320). Where this part and an entry above disagree, this
part holds; "Next's row" in code points here.

FIT PASS
- The row is an instance of Standing orders: order sequence is already the priority among rules, and the queue was the
  last place in it. The row makes the queue's place one the player sets. No new event kind, verb, Save field or part:
  the place is a field on the orders, as each rule's place is its index.

CHOICES (Next's row)
- Shape: Order.below_next, False by default, so a save whose rules all sit above Next (every save before the row) is
  the queue at the bottom and keeps its text (left out of the codec while False; FORMAT unchanged). The player sets
  it in SetOrders; nothing reads it off what a rule does. orders.placed keeps the flag a run to the end of the orders
  (Next is one row): SetOrders, an install's append and a join's town rule pass through it, so a rule appended, the
  lowest priority, lands under Next once any rule does. Town rules and other lanes' rules carry the flag where they
  sit, and it changes nothing for them: the queue is the hands' plan.
- Where a rule sits is not what it is: SetOrders keeps a rule's latch across a move past Next, the rule log names it
  unplaced (orders.unplaced) so the move logs nothing, and an install or join matches it unplaced.
- Running hands mix (replaces "Order mix" and "Queue mix" on which mix runs): the mix of the first hands mix rule that
  is on above Next; else the queue's mix; else that of the first one on under Next; else save.active. mix_order is the
  rule whose mix that is (None while the queue's or the player's own runs).
- Evaluation's controlling verb (replaces the order in "Evaluation applies every verb"): the answer orders (wherever
  they sit: they take no hands); the taps of rules on above Next; the controlling queue entry's tap or answer when it
  can act (a tap: ready and its speed above 0) and no mix rule above Next runs; the taps of rules on under Next (a
  hands tap waits while the queue has the hands); then the controlling entry as before, which waits while an order's
  mix runs except under CURE CUT-IN. A queue switch is never the early source: its mix runs by the running mix, and
  its no-op verb is read last, so it cannot end a loop before the rules under Next have acted.
- A ready entry takes the hands when the task under way ends, never mid-task: a tap can act only on a free lane, so it
  starts at that task's landing, ahead of the rules under Next; a hands mix under Next yields to it as to any tap.
- Passed over as a row, never inside the queue: a controlling entry that cannot act holds nothing, so the rules under
  Next run; the entries behind it still wait (the queue head is never passed over).
- order_states: BEHIND_NEXT for a rule under Next that acts on the hands, is on, and would be ON (or outranked by
  nothing but the queue's mix) while the queue has the hands (plan.next_runs: its mix runs, or the hands run the
  controlling entry's tap). Rules above Next read as before.
- No new cascade: the queue taking the hands from a rule under it fills the hands lane, and the rule's mix coming back
  at the entry's pop is the running mix changing at a QUEUE_POP, which "Queue mix" already bounds. The firing cap and
  its waits are unchanged.

INVARIANTS (Next's row; numbered on from 175)
176. At the bottom: a save whose orders are all above Next encodes, evaluates, logs and reads exactly as before the row
     existed; a logged SetOrders without the flag keeps its text.
177. Above wins: a rule above Next that is on outranks the queue exactly as every rule did at the bottom.
178. Under waits: while the controlling entry can act or its mix runs, no rule under Next starts a hands tap or runs its
     hands mix; once the entry pops, they act again at that instant.

REJECTED, AND WHY (Next's row)
- An index of Next on Save: a new Save field (docs/systems.md, "The engine"), and every insertion or removal of a rule
  would have to move it; a flag moves with the rules it marks.
- A sentinel order standing for Next: every reader of the orders would have to skip it.
- Inferring the place from a rule's card (cures above, replenishers below): placement is the player's, never read off
  wording or effect (issue #320).
