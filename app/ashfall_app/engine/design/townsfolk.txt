TOWNSFOLK, INSTALLED RULES AND OFFERS (day two's engine work, docs/design/day-two-plan.md items 1 to 4). Where this
part and an entry above disagree, this part holds. Examples name ids only; what the content puts in them is content's.

FIT PASS
- A townsperson who works is a lane (Lanes, docs/systems.md): work beside the player's hands, one tap at a time, as
  the smoke lane already is. Its task is a tap order on that lane (Standing orders): "a side lane's rule is a single
  task, not a mix" (docs/decisions.md, "The mix"), and a tap order is already that. What is new is only that the
  player never taps such a lane by hand: LaneDef.ruled. The town's mix with people as shares waits for a second
  person (CARDS, "Mixable").
- An installed rule is an Outcome part (Triggers, Cards: "what lands") whose payload is an ordinary Order, a ruled
  lane's always rule and nothing else (a new save holds none). Nothing marks it as installed afterwards, so the player edits or
  removes it as any rule (Command: the order is data the plan holds, not a special case).
- An offer is a Type Object (OfferDef, AnswerDef) whose state lives in the unlock set, as retirement does (CARDS,
  "Retired"): shown, answered, and which answer are facts that only grow, keyed by kind and id. Save holds no new field
  (docs/systems.md, "The engine": a new part never adds one), and the gate that shows an offer is an ordinary trigger
  (Gates, Triggers) whose outcome unlocks it. Answering is a Command, AnswerOffer, which joins OrderVerb, so an order
  or the queue can issue it (docs/systems.md, "Every verb has an order").
- How games do it: a quest log is a set of flags per quest (offered, accepted, declined) checked by story conditions
  (Nystrom's Type Object for the quest kinds, Command for the answer); Idle Awakening and Melvor give a worker a task
  from a list, which here is the order list. Factorio's and RimWorld's work priorities are the per-lane order
  sequence (first true rule that can act).

CHOICES (townsfolk, installed rules, offers)
- Ruled lane: LaneDef.ruled (bool, default False). A player's TapCard of an uncapped card on a ruled lane raises
  InvalidVerbError ("set by rules"); an order, the queue or an Outcome taps it as on any lane. A one-off (a card
  with a cap) the player taps as a hands one-off: one completion, the lane held until it lands (refused while the
  lane is busy, as any tap), then free for the lane's rules and the person's share of the town's mix. No rule is
  made for it, so nothing is left in the orders once it lands. A ruled lane is a
  declared lane (never HANDS), so its cards are never mixable (SaveMix already rejects off-HANDS cards). Nothing
  else differs: rates, LaneSpeed, dry pauses (other lanes, rules order), landings, READY. Why: the person works by
  the player's rules, not under their finger; a repeating hand tap would also make the row's "what the lane is
  doing" a third source. A one-off is the exception (owner, 2026-11-17): its count means one completion per tap, as
  on the hands, and the page draws the lane's tap as the hands strip draws a hands tap.
- The lane's task is its first rule that can act: at each landing on the lane, evaluation taps the card of the first
  true tap order on that lane whose card is ready (unchanged rule, Orders). A task change (a SetOrders editing the
  rule) takes effect at the lane's next landing: the card running runs to its completion (no cancel, KNOWN GAPS
  above). "Always" is AfterTime(0): true from the first instant, never released (can_fall False). A rule on the lane
  below an always rule acts only while the always rule's card cannot, so the lane's default task is an always rule
  placed after the lane's other rules, and the page builds it so (api/contract/a-lane-run-by-rules.txt, "A lane run by rules"). Of
  several always rules on a lane, the one that acts next is the first that is not passed over.
- The queue is the hands' plan: SetQueue refuses (InvalidVerbError) an entry tapping a card on a ruled lane. The
  queue's head is never passed over, and a ruled lane's always rule retaps that lane at each of its landings before
  the queue is read, so such a head would wait forever and hold every entry behind it.
- Evaluation applies every verb that can act (replaces "It applies the TapCard of the first order ... else nothing"
  in Orders for what one evaluation does). Evaluation is a loop: pick the controlling verb, apply it (one firing),
  handle what that raises at this instant, and pick again; it ends when there is no controlling verb or applying it
  changed nothing, or at the firing cap. The controlling verb, in this order: the AnswerOffer of the first order whose
  latch is true and whose answer can act; else the TapCard of the first order whose latch is true and whose card can
  act (ready: unlocked, not completed or retired, lane free, no blocker; and its speed above 0, tap_seconds not
  None); else, while no order's mix runs, the verb of the controlling queue entry. Orders whose verb cannot act are
  skipped when choosing, never chosen, so only the queue's head (which waits, never passed over) can be a chosen verb
  that changes nothing, and it is read last. It terminates: a changing tap fills a free lane, a changing answer
  closes its offer, and the queue's switch changes nothing (Queue mix). Why: one verb per
  evaluation left every other lane idle until something else evaluated, and a ruled lane's always-true rule,
  reapplied at each of its own landings, then kept the queue's head from ever acting (a lane idling on a ready order
  is "finishing into idleness"). It is a behaviour change, shipped as below.
- Shipping the evaluation change: on its own, ahead of the rest of this part, as its own commit and its own deploy.
  It fixes a defect reachable in day one: an instant where a smoke-lane tap order and a hands verb (the queue's head,
  or a hands tap order) could both act applied only the first, and the other lane waited for the next instant that
  evaluated. Whether a shipped route or a live save actually hits such an instant is what the commit measures (below);
  nothing here assumes it. Without offers the controlling verb is the tap order, then the queue; the AnswerOffer step
  joins in front when offers land. The rule it follows is the one the SetOrders latch change followed (Rule changes):
  - The stored save is the save. Loading is the whole promise (engineering.md, "Rules from day one"): every live save
    decodes exactly as before (FORMAT and the rules digest do not change, so there is no rebase), and its time away from its last write runs under the
    new evaluation, forward only. Nothing is rewritten, no flag marks old saves.
  - A save written by the old build may stand at an instant with a ready order left unapplied. It stays so until the
    next instant that evaluates (a landing, a latch, a READY, a trigger, a SetOrders), exactly as under the old build;
    invariant 57 holds only from there on, which is why it is stated for saves the new engine has run.
  - Replays diverge, logs do not break. saves/support.py's `replay` rebuilds from a day's snapshot through the logged
    decisions under the running engine and compares with the stored save. For a save whose span from a kept snapshot
    to its last write crosses an instant where the old build left a second verb unapplied, the rebuilt save differs
    (the other lane started at once, so stores, work and every later landing move), and `replay` reports no match;
    `restore` from such a snapshot would write the new engine's version. Every other save replays as before. A
    divergent save matches again once its snapshots and decisions from before the release are gone: the writer prunes
    them past 30 days (SNAPSHOT_DAYS, DECISIONS_KEPT, saves/write.py), but only when that save is written again, so a
    save still played stops diverging 30 days after the release and a save never written again keeps its mismatch.
  - Two builds during the rollout: with no release raised, old and new pods both serve a save until the rollout ends
    (engineering.md, "Deploys with two builds live"). Each write is a valid save under the build that made it and only
    moves forward, so a save's history may mix the two for those minutes; it shows in `replay` as above, nothing more.
  - The commit carries its exposure, as the latch change's did: day one's playthroughs and simulator re-run (every held
    counter that moves is named in the commit and brought to the lead before it lands, never raised to pass), and
    `python -m ashfall_app.saves replay` from every kept snapshot of every live save, read-only against prod, with
    the count of saves that no longer match and the date their logs age out. A divergent ended save is reported,
    never repaired.
- Lane order: plan.lane_order(save, rules, lane) is the index of the first order whose latch is true, whose verb is
  not dead, and whose verb is a TapCard of the card the lane runs now; None while the lane is free or no such order
  exists (the player's own tap, the queue's, an outcome's start, or a rule that has since let go). View's LaneBar
  carries it as `order`. order_states gains OUTRANKED for tap orders: a tap order whose latch is true and whose verb
  is not dead is OUTRANKED when its lane's lane_order is not None and below its own index; else ON (as before).
  Mix orders are unchanged. Presentation only: evaluation never reads it.
- Installed rule: Outcome.orders, a tuple of RuleInstall(card: CardId, first: bool = False). It installs exactly one
  shape, a ruled lane's always rule, Order(AfterTime(0), TapCard(card)): a shape the page already draws, words and
  edits (api/contract/a-lane-run-by-rules.txt, "A lane run by rules"), so whatever an install creates the player can see, change and
  remove. It lands after `raises` and before `queues` and `starts` (so it sees the outcome's unlocks). Each install,
  in tuple order: if save.orders already holds an equal order, nothing; else the first=True installs go to the top
  as one block in tuple order (the first of them at index 0) and the first=False ones are appended in tuple order,
  each with latch latch_now at that instant (always true), as SetOrders latches a new order (Rule changes: it is
  reported as coming on). Orders and the queue evaluate after the outcome, as after every outcome. Afterwards nothing
  tells an installed order from one the player set: SetOrders replaces, edits, moves or removes it.
- Queued one-off: Outcome.queues, a tuple of CardId, each a HANDS card with a cap, allowed only in an answer's
  outcome. Each puts the page's "When ready" entry, QueueEntry(TapCard(c), AtLeast(CardCount(c), n, n)) with n the
  card's completions at that instant plus one, at the front of the queue (several in tuple order), unless an equal
  entry is already queued. It lands after `orders`, before `starts`. The hands' one-off needs this, not a rule: on
  the hands the page draws only mix rules, so a tap rule there could not be shown or edited, and a start does nothing
  while the hands are mid-tap. As the controlling entry it starts the card at the first instant that evaluates with
  the hands free and no order's mix running (the answer itself when the hands are free, else the tap's landing),
  pops once the card completes (its `until` holds and cannot fall), and is drawn, and removable, as the queue's Next.
  Accepted: the entry it pushes down stops being the controlling one, so its `until` is not watched until it
  controls again, when it pops at once if its `until` holds then (the rule for an entry coming to control). An
  `until` that held and fell in between is missed, and that entry's verb acts once more; the player's plan resumes
  where it was, one step later, which is what putting the answer first means.
- Installs land once: validation allows a non-empty Outcome.orders only in a TriggerDef's outcome and an AnswerDef's
  outcome. A trigger fires at most once per save (invariant 29) and an offer is answered at most once, so no install
  ever lands twice, and an installed rule the player removed never comes back: not at a later instant, not by a
  rebase (step 6 never re-fires a fired trigger; an answer is an unlock, kept), not by a cap raise (card outcomes
  hold no installs). A later release that wants a rule installed again adds a new trigger.
- Retired chores (week-three-plan item 3; docs/decisions.md, "Each season brings its own chores": the rule goes away
  when its season ends): Outcome.retire_orders, a tuple of CardId, the install's inverse. It lands after `raises` and
  before `leaves` and `orders` (so an install in the same outcome stands). At that instant it removes from save.orders,
  with their latches, every order whose verb is a TapCard of a named card, wherever it stands and whoever set it (an
  install or the player); the rest keep their order and latches. Nothing else changes: not the queue, the mixes, the
  unlock set, nor orders whose verb is a SwitchMix or an answer. A tap a removed order started runs on to its landing
  (no cancel, as a task change) and is not tapped again by it. Orders and the queue evaluate after the outcome, as
  always. It lands once (a trigger fires once), so a rule the player sets again afterwards stays. The chore's order
  card is offered again while its line shows uncovered (LedgerLine.covered), unless the content gave it an
  offered_until (OrderCardEntry), an unlock the retiring turn gives: then the page offers it no more. The
  chore's leftover stores are content's to empty (a standing drain on its own pool, the visitor's idiom).
- Logged: rulelog.RuleChange gains `ended: Ended | None = None`, Ended an Enum of SEASON ("season") and LEFT ("left",
  LEAVES). rule_changes(before, after, rules) reports, beside the latch changes, one RuleChange(after.time, o, False,
  level, ended) per distinct order o of `before` that `after` does not hold (by equality) and that an outcome
  removed: SEASON when o taps a card named by the retire_orders of a trigger fired in `after` and not in `before`;
  LEFT when o taps a card on a lane whose Unlock(LEFT) `after` holds and `before` does not. `level` is as for a
  let-go (the store o's condition reads, at after.time; None for none). An order missing from `after` for any other
  reason (a SetOrders removed or edited it) is not reported, as before. It reads the two saves and the rules only:
  nothing is stored. advance_logged, decide_logged and rebase_logged carry them in Step.rule_changes as any change.
  Ended and RuleChange are exported from ashfall_app.engine (defined in rulelog.py). Within one rule_changes call the
  latch changes come first, in `after`'s order, then the ended entries, in `before`'s order, so a Step lists one
  instant's ended rules in the order the player had them. `level` is read off `after`, after the instant's outcomes
  have landed: subject_value of the order's StoreValue at after.time in quantity units, exactly what a let-go of that
  order at that instant would record. An order removed for both reasons at once (it taps a retired cure on a lane that
  left in the same instant) is logged once, SEASON.

- Lane rules: LaneRules(lane: LaneId), a discrete subject: the number of orders in save.orders whose verb is a TapCard
  of a known card on the ruled lane and whose when is not AfterTime(0), the one shape an install makes, so the
  count is the rules the player set; editing the always rule's task, or an install, leaves it unchanged. It is
  allowed only as a trigger's when (an offer's gate is one): validation refuses it in a chip, a card's requires and a
  trigger's requires, and SetOrders and SetQueue refuse it in a condition (InvalidVerbError), so no save holds it
  and the codec, FORMAT and the wire are unchanged. save.orders changes only at a SetOrders and at an install (which
  adds only AfterTime(0) orders), and a rebase drops only orders naming gone ids, so the count changes only at a
  decision: rise_time is None, the pending check at that instant fires the trigger, and its lines land in that
  decision's Step.lines. Forecasts and what-if forks never fire it. A rule the player later removes has still
  landed the line (a trigger fires once). Its definition needs a known ruled lane (InvalidRulesError); a save that
  already holds a qualifying order gets the line at the rebase instant, as any new trigger already true.
  Rejected: a generic orders count (the player's hands rules and the always rules would count, and filters are a

- Offers: Rules.offers, a tuple of OfferDef(id: OfferId, answers: tuple[AnswerDef, ...]), AnswerDef(id: AnswerId,
  outcome: Outcome). OfferId and AnswerId are new NewTypes; answer ids are unique across all offers; an offer has
  at least two answers. Three new UnlockKinds: OFFER (id an OfferId: the offer is shown), ANSWERED (id an OfferId:
  it has been answered, by either answer) and ANSWER (id an AnswerId: this answer was given). An offer is shown once
  Unlock(OFFER, o) is in the unlock set (an Outcome unlocks it: a trigger's, a card's or an answer's), open while
  shown and not answered, and answered from the instant Unlock(ANSWERED, o) is in the set. It never expires: nothing
  in the engine closes an open offer (KNOWN GAPS). Why ANSWERED beside ANSWER: "answered, either answer" is then one
  Unlocked condition, with no disjunction added to the order vocabulary.
- ANSWERED and ANSWER are written to save.unlocked and read from it whatever rules.opening is: with opening None the
  unlock writer adds nothing today (every other unlock counts as held, Unlocks), but it adds these two kinds, and
  is_unlocked and every Unlocked condition count them only when present. View.unlocked with opening None reports
  the effective set as before, with ANSWERED and ANSWER only as save.unlocked holds them. OFFER follows the usual
  rule (with opening None every offer is shown from time 0).
- AnswerOffer(offer: OfferId, answer: AnswerId), a Verb and an OrderVerb. It can act when `answer` is one of
  `offer`'s answers and the offer is open. Applying it adds Unlock(ANSWERED, offer) and Unlock(ANSWER, answer) to the
  unlock set, then lands the answer's outcome as a trigger's lands (field order; its lines emitted with card None),
  then orders and the queue evaluate at that instant (also when the player decided it: the answer's installs and
  queued one-offs must act at once, which a player's TapCard deliberately does not do). From the player: an unknown
  offer, an answer not of that offer, an offer not shown, or one already answered raises InvalidVerbError. From an
  order or the queue: those change nothing. SetOrders and SetQueue refuse (InvalidVerbError) an AnswerOffer whose
  pair is not a declared offer and one of its answers; a pair whose offer is not shown yet is accepted (planning
  ahead, as a locked card is). Decision context (context.py) records it like any verb, with no card.
- Dead: is_dead holds for an AnswerOffer of an answered offer (by anyone), so an order holding one is PASSED_OVER. A
  queue entry holding one is dead for good (an answer is never taken back, so nothing revives it): it settles and
  pops at the instant its offer is answered, whatever its `until`. An AnswerOffer of
  an offer not yet shown is not dead: it waits (an order's is ON while its latch is true and simply cannot act; the
  queue's head waits, as the queue always does).
- Evaluation after a cascade (extends the CARDS entry): when the firing cap suspended evaluation at instant T, a
  READY falls at T + 1 whenever, at the end of T, any verb could act: a ready TapCard candidate (as before), a true

  T + 1, sequence len(rules.cards) and subject the empty string, however many of them wait, and its handler always
  asks for evaluation.

- No new event: an offer shows only when an Outcome lands (a COMPLETION, a TAP_DONE, a TRIGGER or an answer), each of

  lines; the player's own answer is only ever a decision.
- Covered: an AnswerOffer covers no store (it acts once; covered is about what keeps a line fed).
- The answer's card: for the view, an answer's card is the first card its outcome queues, else the card of its first
  RuleInstall, else the first card it starts, else None. Read off the rule, so the engine holds no presentation field.

INVARIANTS (townsfolk, installed rules, offers; numbered on from 56. Invariant 27 now reads: "... no Unlocked
condition is false for a known id, except one on ANSWERED or ANSWER, which is true only once that answer is given".)
57. Every lane driven: for s reached from new_save by advances and by decisions other than a player's SwitchMix and
    SaveMix, and s2 = advance(s, r, t): no order with a true latch has a TapCard that can act in s2 (unlocked, not
    completed or retired, its lane free, blocker None, speed above 0), none has an AnswerOffer that can act, and,
    while no order's mix runs, the controlling queue entry's verb is not a TapCard that can act nor an AnswerOffer
    that can act, and when it is a SwitchMix its mix is view(s2, r).running_mix. It need not hold at an instant that hit the firing cap,
    and holds at every later t that did not itself hit it (Evaluation after a cascade). It holds across a chip
    turning, also when that brings a skipped tap's speed back above 0. In particular two lanes landing at one
    instant, each with a true order whose card is ready, are both running at that instant.
58. Ruled: a player's TapCard of a card on a ruled lane raises InvalidVerbError; the same verb from an order or an
    Outcome behaves exactly as on an unruled lane (the same rules with ruled False give the same saves).
59. Install: with o = Order(AfterTime(0), TapCard(c)), an outcome whose orders are RuleInstalls landing at T, on a
    save whose orders are `before`, gives F + before + L, where F are the first=True installs' orders and L the
    first=False ones', each in tuple order and each left out when `before` or an earlier install of the same outcome
    already holds it (a repeated install lands once), every new latch True.

REJECTED, AND WHY (townsfolk, installed rules, offers)
- The town's mix with the person as a share: with one person a share is always all of them; it waits for the second
  arrival (content's decision).
- A per-lane default task stored in the save (like active for the hands), set by a new verb: a new Save field, a codec
  change and a verb that would need its own order, for what an always-true rule already is.
- An always rule on the lane's feature (Unlocked of a feature): ties every default rule to a content id the page then
  has to build; AfterTime(0) is the same truth and the page can build it for any ruled lane.
- Keeping one verb per evaluation and letting content avoid coincidences: a lane would idle on a ready order, and an
  always-true rule on a ruled lane would keep the queue head from acting forever.
- Holding the evaluation fix for day two's release, whose rules change raises the release and so keeps the two builds
  off one save during the rollout: day one would keep its defect until then, and the mixing it avoids is minutes of
  forward-only, valid writes, which the latch change already accepted.
- A third evaluation order (queue before tap orders, or answers last): an answer's outcome may start a card, which
  should take its lane before a tap order fills it; tap orders before the queue is the existing rule.
- An install record (a set of install ids in the save) so an outcome landing twice installs once: a new Save field
  for what restricting installs to once-only outcomes gives for nothing.
- Reinstalling a removed installed rule when its outcome fires again: the player's edit would be undone by the
  story, and "an ordinary rule the player can edit or remove" would be false.
- Marking installed orders (a flag or an id on Order): the page would draw them differently and the player could not
  treat them as their own; an Order stays (condition, verb).
- Installs as Outcome.starts: a start on a busy lane changes nothing (KNOWN GAPS above), so a moment landing while the
  lane works would be lost; a rule waits for the lane and acts at its next landing.
- Installs of any rule SetOrders accepts (any condition, any lane): the page draws, words and edits only store rules
  and a ruled lane's always rule, so an installed rule of another shape would act unseen and could not be removed.
  Restricting installs to the always rule needs no new page drawing.
- A tap rule on the hands for the hands' answer, and a Rules-tab drawing for hands tap rules: on the hands a rule
  always runs a mix (docs/decisions.md, "The mix"), so it would be a new shape there for one step; the queue's When
  ready entry is the existing shape for "do this once, when the hands are free".
- A queued entry for the ruled lane's answer too: the queue is one list for the hands, its head is never passed over,
  and the lane's always rule would keep it from acting (above).
- Offer state as a new state type on Save (open, answered, answer): docs/systems.md forbids a new Save field, and the
  facts only grow, which is what the unlock set holds (as RETIRED).
- An Answered(offer) subject in the order vocabulary, or an Or condition: more vocabulary (change_time, latches,
  codec, API) for one fact an unlock gives.
- An offer with its own gate in the engine (OfferDef.when): a second firing mechanism beside triggers; the loader
  makes the trigger.
- AnswerOffer by offer kind ("answer every offer of this kind with yes") now: there is one offer, so a kind would be
  a second name for its id; the kind arrives with the second offer kind, when the Rules tab shows answer rules, as a
  new verb beside this one (content's decision 4).
  Superseded 2026-10-28 by OFFER KINDS (the last part): day three's arrival offer and day five's are the second offer
  of one kind, and the answer rule set at the first has to answer the second.
- A player's answer that does not evaluate (as a tap does not): an answer that installs a rule or starts a card would
  wait for some later instant, so answering would idle the lane it gives work to.
- An expiry on offers now: the first offer is the story's next step, and a branch pauses only itself; expiry is
  KNOWN GAPS.
- takes_s computed by the page from the card's work_s: the page would simulate speed (THE CLIENT MAY NOT).
- Leaving a season's chore rules in place (retired chores): docs/decisions.md says the rule goes away when its season
  ends, and a rule tapping a cure for a chip that cannot show again would sit in the Rules tab with no reason on it.
- An outcome part that takes stores, to empty the chore's leftovers with the rule: rejected in FIGHTS (a take that
  lands at once); a standing drain on its own pool, the visitor's idiom, does it.
- Retiring only installed rules: nothing tells an installed rule from the player's (Installed rule), and a chore rule
  the player wrote by hand is the same chore.
- Marking the retired order dead and keeping it (PASSED_OVER forever): the player would have to remove what the story
  ended, and an order card offered beside a dead rule for the same cure would read as a duplicate.
- A stored record of what an outcome removed (to log it): rule_changes reads it off the two saves and the rules.
