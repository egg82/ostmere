SHARE LEVELS (a store rule's level as a share of the store's cap: issue #65). Where this part and an entry above
disagree, this part holds.

FIT PASS
- An instance of Conditions, a Specification given an explicit field: AtLeast and AtMost gain value_of_cap and
  release_of_cap, each saying that its level is basis points of the watched store's cap now (gates.store_cap) rather
  than a quantity. No new subject, condition kind, event or state. A share level becomes the quantity it stands for
  at the instant the condition is read, and everything after that reads the quantity as before.
- The defect it answers: a rule's levels were quantities fixed when it was written, so every rule went stale as its
  store's cap rose, and a rule written at full silently stopped meaning full.

CHOICES (share levels)
- Shape: value_of_cap and release_of_cap, bool, default False, and left out of the codec's text while False (as
  Mix.layer is), so every save, logged verb and rules digest encodes as before: FORMAT is unchanged, nothing is
  rebased and no save is migrated. A level whose flag is False is today's quantity. Each level has its own flag, so
  "under 12, until full" is one condition. A share level is allowed only on a StoreValue subject and only in
  0..BP; anything else is refused (InvalidVerbError from SetOrders and SetQueue). Only the save's orders and queue
  hold one: a chip's, a trigger's or a card's condition with a share level is refused (InvalidRulesError), so
  content, blockers, pending triggers and cure readings read quantities alone.
- Reading: wherever a condition is read (is_true, its rise and fall times, the latch SetOrders sets), each share
  level b is turned into a quantity off the cap at that instant. With Q one item, C = cap // Q (the cap in whole
  items, as the page shows it) and k = value // Q (the store's whole items, as the page shows them), the store's
  whole items are compared with the exact share x = b * C / BP, which is never rounded:
    at_most's value     holds while k <= x          value   = (floor(x) + 1) * Q - 1
    at_most's release   lets go once k >= x         release = ceil(x) * Q - 1
    at_least's value    holds once k >= x           value   = ceil(x) * Q
    at_least's release  lets go once k <= x         release = (floor(x) + 1) * Q
  So both levels of a share are reached at the level itself, from either side: "down to a quarter" holds at a quarter
  and under, "until empty" lets go once the store shows 0, and "full" is reached at C, the cap's whole items, which
  the store always can reach. A quarter of a 10-item cap holds an at_most at 2 and under and an at_least at 3 and
  over: each is exactly true of the amount the page shows. An amount level keeps today's reading, the page's "under
  n" and "until under m" numbering included (api CONTRACT.txt, "a store rule").
- Rounding to whole items, not to the exact cap: the page shows whole items and words a share without a number, so
  a level crossed between two shown amounts would come on at an amount that reads as the wrong side of it; and a cap
  raised by a multiplier need not be whole items, so the exact cap could be a fraction of an item above the full the
  page shows.
- Hysteresis: a condition holding a share level keeps a band of at least one item between its read quantities. Whole
  items can bring two levels together: a quarter and half of a 4-item cap read as 1 and 2, and an at_most on at 1
  and under that lets go at 2 would flip at the quantity 2 items, every second the store moves across it. An amount
  level and a share level can also pass each other as the cap rises. So when the read release is less than one item
  past the read value, it moves to one item past it, but never beyond the store's range: an at_most's release is
  then max(release, value, min(value + Q, C * Q - 1)), so it lets go at full at the latest; an at_least's is
  min(release, value, max(value - Q, Q)), so it lets go once the store shows 0 at the latest. Only a value level
  within one item of full or empty leaves no room, and that condition is a plain threshold. A condition of amount
  levels alone is read as before, plain thresholds included.
- The cap changing mid-rule: a cap only rises (Store caps), and only at an instant where something else fires (an
  outcome's raise at a completion). Share levels are read afresh at each read, so a rule's quantities move with the
  cap at that instant, and the pending check after the raise sees each latch against the new quantities. A latch is
  never reset: it keeps its state, and the new quantities decide its next change. "Up to full, until half", on at 10
  of 10 when the cap rises to 15, stays on until the store falls to 7 (half of 15 is 7.5); "up to full, until three
  quarters" in the same place lets go at that instant (10 is under 11.25). "Down to a quarter, until full", on and
  filling, now fills to the new full. A latch that is off rises at the instant only if the store is already past
  its new value.
- Rule changes: orders are matched by equality, so a share level and an amount level that read the same quantity
  today are different orders, and editing one into the other latches it afresh (latch_now).
- What a rule reads back (rulelog's on_level, the ledger, goal rows) reads the store, not the level, and is
  unchanged.

INVARIANTS (share levels; numbered on from 162. amount(c, s) is c with each share level replaced by the quantity the
reading table gives under store_cap at s, and its flags False.)
162. Read at the cap now: for any save s and condition c holding a share level, is_true(s, r, c) ==
     is_true(s, r, amount(c, s)), and change_time(s, r, c, latch) == change_time(s, r, amount(c, s), latch) for
     either latch.
163. The same share across a raise: a share level crosses at the same share of the whole-item cap before and after
     the cap rises, while an amount level crosses at the same quantity.
164. Full and empty: an at_least whose value is BP of the cap holds exactly while the store's whole items equal the
     cap's; an at_most whose value is 0 of the cap holds exactly while the store shows 0 whole items; an at_least
     whose release is 0 of the cap lets go exactly once the store shows 0.
165. Unchanged for amounts: a condition with both flags False encodes to the text it had before the flags existed,
     and behaves as before.
166. Validation: a share level on any subject but a StoreValue, or outside 0..BP, or a release on the wrong side of
     a value in the same form, raises InvalidVerbError from SetOrders and SetQueue; a share level and an amount level
     are never compared there. One in a chip, trigger or card condition raises InvalidRulesError.
167. A band of one item: a condition holding a share level, whose read value leaves room for it, lets go at least
     one item past where it comes on, and never past full (at_most) or under nothing shown (at_least).

REJECTED, AND WHY (share levels)
- A subject for the store's fullness (StoreShare, read in basis points): a rule with one amount level and one share
  level would have no shape, and a fullness subject could not round to whole items without knowing which side of
  the level each comparison is on.
- A level type (a quantity or a share) on value and release: a scalar union on the wire, which a generated client
  types as an untyped object.
- One flag for the whole condition: each level is either a number or a share (issue #65), and "under 12, until full"
  is the case that wants both.
- Rewriting the saved quantities when a cap rises: a second writer of save.orders, and an order matched by equality
  would become another order, losing its latch, at every raise.
- Reading levels that meet as a plain threshold: the store moves across one quantity every second, so the rule's mix
  would switch at every second; one more item of band costs a level the player wrote one item, and only where whole
  items had already moved it.
- Rounding the share to the nearest item: a quarter of 10 would read as 3 for an at_most, which then holds at 3,
  above a quarter. Comparing the shown amount with the exact share is right on both sides.
- Share levels in content: nothing in the content asks for one, and every reader of the rules' conditions (blockers,
  pending triggers, cure readings) would then need the cap.
